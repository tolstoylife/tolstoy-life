## 1. Posrednik founding chronology

**Stage 1 — the Moscow meetings, 1883–early 1884.**
The «Канва жизни Толстого» (Tolstoy Museum / Academy of Sciences, Leningrad, 1928, p. 25)
fixes December 1883 as the moment when Tolstoy's plan for «издание книг для образования
русских людей» first crystallised — labelled in the PSS apparatus as «первое зерно
"Посредника"».

In late January–February 1884 Tolstoy convened «несколько совещаний» at his Khamovniki
house. Attendees identified in the apparatus include M. P. Shchepkin (Moscow city Duma
member, later typographer), V. N. Marakuev (publisher, founded «Народная библиотека»
1882), R. A. Pisarev (Tula zemstvo activist), N. N. Zlatovratsky, and S. A. Usov. For
one of these meetings Tolstoy drafted his programmatic «Речь о народных изданиях» (PSS
25:523–529 and apparatus 874–879). Pisarev described the meeting in a letter to Chertkov
of 7 February 1884: «Третьего дня у него, Толстого, было собрание людей, сочувствующих
этому делу; на первый раз Толстой собрал человек десять, но в этот вечер ни к чему
окончательному еще не пришли» (AChertkov archive, cited PSS-25 apparatus p. 876).

Tolstoy to Chertkov, end of February 1884: «Моё занятие книгами больше и больше
захватывает меня» (PSS 85:30). The speech itself is PSS vol. 25, pp. 523–529;
apparatus pp. 874–879.

Note: Chertkov had first met Tolstoy in October 1883; the speech/meetings predate the
formal Chertkov–Sytin operational agreement by roughly a year.

**Stage 2 — Chertkov–Sytin agreement, autumn/winter 1884.**
The PSS Chertkov-letters footnote to v85_048 (28 February 1885) preserves Chertkov's own
words in a telegram note: «Через неделю должны быть готовы все рисунки к лубочным
книжечкам и обе большие картины. Тогда отвезу их сам опять к Сытину, так как очень
важно, чтобы первые издания вышли хорошо во всех подробностях.» This places the
Chertkov–Sytin handover of the first booklets in late February/early March 1885, just
before the spring 1885 publication run. The Free Dictionary / Great Soviet Encyclopedia
article on Posrednik states the house was «founded in St. Petersburg in 1884 on the
initiative of L. N. Tolstoy and headed by V. G. Chertkov»; Wikipedia (Vladimir Chertkov
article) gives 1885 for Chertkov «organis[ing] and financ[ing]» it. The discrepancy
(1884 vs 1885) reflects: 1884 = planning/agreement phase; 1885 = first publications.

The most concrete pre-launch institutional snapshot is the Chertkov telegram note (PSS
85:48 footnote): lubok booklet drawings and two large картины nearing completion,
Chertkov personally managing Sytin delivery «to ensure quality» (PSS apparatus,
_sweep_letters_chertkov.md entry for v85_048).

**Stage 3 — first four booklets, spring 1885.**
From the PSS Tom 25 apparatus (per-tale commentary entries):
- «Чем люди живы?» — censorship approved 3 March 1885 (censor Назаревский); published
  spring 1885 with 4 Repin illustrations (PSS-25-commentary §1).
- «Где любовь, там и Бог» — censorship approved 24 May 1885 (PSS-25-commentary §5).
- «Два брата и золото» and «Ильяс» — approved in the same December 1885–February 1886
  batch (PSS-25-commentary §3, §4), so these are slightly *later* than the first two.
- «Два старика» — text approved 4 October 1885, cover 17 October 1885
  (PSS-25-commentary §12).

The precise «first four» as a simultaneous release is not pinned to a single date in the
available apparatus. The first two definitively out in spring 1885 are «Чем люди живы?»
and «Где любовь, там и Бог»; the картины series (Искушение Христа / Страдания Господа)
received censorship approval 3–4 March 1885 and were also among the initial Posrednik
print run.

Sources: PSS-25-commentary; _sweep_letters_chertkov.md (v85_048 footnote; v85_049;
v85_054; v85_065); tolstoy.ru/creativity/journalismguide/98.php (speech 1884 entry).

## 2. Circulation / sales figures

Multiple independent sources give large but slightly varying figures:

- **12 million copies in the first four years** — cited by the Free Dictionary /
  Great Soviet Encyclopedia article on Posrednik (derived from Soviet-era reference
  works; same figure circulates in Russian secondary literature citing Biryukov and
  Gorbunov-Posadov's Posrednik histories).
- **~20 million copies in six years** / **20 million in nine years** — two variants
  found in English-language secondary sources (notable biographies site; ditext.com
  Tolstoy Georgist economy article).
- **Price point**: booklets at 1–1½ kopecks per copy. PSS letters confirm this explicitly:
  Tolstoy to Chertkov, 10–11 May 1885 (v85_059): transfer of Sofia Andreevna's edition
  of the tales to Sytin at «1½ к. за экземпляр» for 600,000 copies = 900 rubles total.
  Tolstoy to Chertkov (v85_060): Posrednik booklets priced «почти по той же цене, как и
  Сытинские, по 1½ [копейки]».
- Robert C. Otto, *Publishing for the People: The Firm Posrednik, 1885–1905* (University
  of Wisconsin–Madison PhD dissertation, 1983; ProQuest 8315019) is the most
  comprehensive scholarly treatment; full text not publicly accessible but dissertation
  title confirms the institutional scope.

Best-sourced figure for planning purposes: **~12 million copies in the first four years**
(Soviet encyclopaedia, itself drawing on Gorbunov-Posadov's internal records); the 20
million figures likely cover a longer period (1885–1891 or 1885–1894).

Sources: encyclopedia2.thefreedictionary.com/Posrednik+Publishing+House; v85_059
(_sweep_letters_chertkov.md); v85_060; ditext.com Tolstoy article; Otto 1983 (metadata
only).

## 3. «Детский отдых» — first publication of «Чем люди живы», December 1881

**Journal:** «Детский отдых» (Children's Rest / Children's Leisure), No. 12, December 1881.
Illustrations by N. A. Kiselyov. Source: PSS-25-commentary §1 (apparatus p. 665).

**V. K. Istomin's role:** Vladimir Konstantinovich Istomin was the editor of «Детский
отдых» at this period. Two Tolstoy letters to him survive (PSS vol. 90, pp. 247):
- v90_036 (c. 1 December 1881): Tolstoy writes «Я переменил не больше 20 строк во
  всем, но они очень нужны, особенно в конце» — handing back the corrected manuscript.
  «Надеюсь на ваше умное и тонкое отношение к этому моему писанью и вверяюсь вам.»
- v90_037 (4 December 1881): Tolstoy replies to Istomin's editorial query about an
  illustration detail («Синий карандаш на портках не мой, и исключить их потому нельзя,
  что тогда ангел будет без порток»), written on Istomin's own letter.
  
These two notes confirm that Istomin edited the tale for «Детский отдых» and that
Tolstoy was personally involved in final copy corrections in early December 1881,
consistent with the December 1881 issue date.

**P. A. Bers:** No evidence in the extracted primary sources of P. A. Bers having an
editorial role at «Детский отдых» for this publication. Tatyana Andreevna Bers (later
Kuzmínskaya), Tolstoy's sister-in-law, is associated with Yasnaya Polyana domestic
life in this period but not with the journal's editorial apparatus. UNRESOLVED: whether
Bers had any involvement beyond Istomin.

Sources: v90_036_V_K_Istominu.txt; v90_037_V_K_Istominu.txt (PSS vol. 90, p. 247);
PSS-25-commentary §1 (PSS 25:665).

## 4. The 1887 censorship wave

**«Чем люди живы?»** — Arrested 4 February 1887 by the Moscow Censorship Committee on
denunciation by K. P. Pobedonostsev (Ober-Procurator of the Holy Synod). Subsequent
editions banned 1887 and 1888. Only freely circulated after 1905.
Source: PSS-25-commentary §1 (apparatus p. 665).

**«Сказка об Иване-дураке»** — First published by Posrednik, censorship approved April
1886. 2nd edition arrested February 1887; banned 1887, 1892, 1893. Freely circulated
only from 1906. The surviving censor Tikhonov's written verdict condemns the tale for
«вредное для государства» content and anti-military sentiment.
Source: PSS-25-commentary §16 (apparatus p. 715–719).

Both arrests thus fall in February 1887, consistent with a coordinated Pobedonostsev-
instigated action. The PSS apparatus explicitly names Pobedonostsev as instigator for
«Чем люди живы?»; for Ivan the Fool the apparatus attributes the ban to censor Tikhonov
acting under the same political pressure.

The Hapgood 1887 article (marxists.org/archive/tolstoy/about/1887/count-tolstoi-and-
the-public-censor.html) documents censorship of the Moscow Census piece and vol. XII of
Tolstoy's collected works from the same period, but does not detail the Posrednik-
specific arrests — UNRESOLVED for independent English-language corroboration of the
exact February 1887 date beyond PSS apparatus.

Sources: PSS-25-commentary §1 and §16; Otto 1983 (dissertation, paywalled — mentions
«confiscation of materials and fines on distributors» by early 1890s); en.wikipedia.org
/wiki/Vladimir_Chertkov (confirms censorship pressure on Posrednik).

## 5. Ban/permission dates and translation data — six key workRecords

### 5a. «Чем люди живы?» / What Men Live By
- **Written:** January 1881, Yasnaya Polyana.
- **First publication:** «Детский отдых», No. 12, December 1881 (illus. N. A. Kiselyov).
- **Posrednik edition:** Censorship approved 3 March 1885; published spring 1885
  (4 Repin illustrations). N. N. Ge produced a 12-illustration album edition, 1886.
- **Ban:** Arrested 4 February 1887; banned 1887, 1888; free after 1905.
- **Earliest English translation:** Nathan Haskell Dole, *What Men Live By* (T. Y.
  Crowell, Boston, c. 1887–1888). The Internet Archive copy
  (archive.org/details/whatmenliveby00dolegoog) confirms a Dole/Crowell edition. Dole
  was literary advisor to Crowell 1887–1901.
- Sources: PSS-25-commentary §1; v90_036/037; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_Men_Live_By;
  archive.org/details/whatmenliveby00dolegoog.

### 5b. «Где любовь, там и Бог» / Where Love Is, God Is (Martin the Cobbler)
- **Source:** French story «Le père Martin» by Reuben Saillens; received by Tolstoy from
  Chertkov 17 March 1885; reworked extensively. Tolstoy wrote to Saillens in 1888
  acknowledging «неумышленный плагиат» (PSS 64:268, v64_268_R_Sajyanu).
- **First publication (Posrednik):** Censorship approved 24 May 1885.
- **Ban:** Never banned — «одно из наиболее широко распространённых» Posrednik titles
  (PSS-25-commentary §5).
- **Earliest English translation:** UNRESOLVED — likely included in Dole/Crowell collections
  late 1880s or early Maude translations c. 1906 (*Twenty-Three Tales*); no specific
  first-edition date recovered in this search.
- Sources: PSS-25-commentary §5; v64_268; standardebooks.org translators list.

### 5c. «Два старика» / Two Old Men
- **Sources:** (1) Shchegolyonok oral version; (2) Kovalevsky «Душеполезное чтение»
  1871; (3) Daniil the Abbot's 12th-c. pilgrimage account.
- **First publication:** Posrednik; text approved 4 October 1885, cover 17 October 1885.
  Illustrations by A. D. Kivshenko.
- **Ban:** No ban recorded in PSS apparatus.
- **Earliest English translation:** UNRESOLVED — likely Dole or Maude collections
  (see 5a); no specific date recovered.
- Sources: PSS-25-commentary §12.

### 5d. «Много ли человеку земли нужно?» / How Much Land Does a Man Need?
- **Written:** May–June 1885 (same notebook as «Зерно» and «Кающийся грешник»).
- **First publication:** «Русское богатство» journal; censorship approved 5 April 1886.
  One phrase cut by censorship. Also in Posrednik sbornik «Три сказки» (illus. Malyshev).
- **Ban:** No wholesale ban recorded, though one phrase was censored at first publication.
- **Earliest English translation:** Nathan Haskell Dole, collected with other tales,
  T. Y. Crowell, late 1880s–early 1890s. The Amazon/Waterstones listings confirm a
  Dole translation in print; original Crowell edition date not pinned beyond «c. 1887–
  1901» window. Aylmer and Louise Maude, *Twenty-Three Tales* (Oxford University Press /
  World's Classics, 1906) is the canonical Maude vehicle.
- Sources: PSS-25-commentary §10; amazon.com Dole/Maude editions.

### 5e. «Сказка об Иване-дураке» / Ivan the Fool
- **Written:** August–September 1885.
- **First publication:** Posrednik; censorship approved April 1886.
- **Ban:** 2nd edition arrested February 1887; banned 1887, 1892, 1893; free from 1906.
- **Earliest English translation:** Aylmer and Louise Maude, *Twenty-Three Tales* (1906)
  is confirmed; an earlier Dole/Crowell or Isabel Hapgood translation may exist —
  UNRESOLVED for exact date.
- Sources: PSS-25-commentary §16.

### 5f. «Три старца» / The Three Hermits
- **Sources:** (1) Shchegolyonok oral version; (2) Augustine of Hippo legend analogue.
- **First publication:** «Нива» (The Field) journal, 1886, No. 13. Not primarily a
  Posrednik title.
- **Ban:** No ban recorded.
- **Earliest English translation:** Nathan Haskell Dole, 1887 (as part of a Tolstoy
  collection, T. Y. Crowell). Also Aylmer and Louise Maude, *Twenty-Three Tales* (1906).
  The 1887 Dole date is confirmed by multiple secondary sources (EBSCO Research Starters,
  en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Three_Hermits).
- Sources: PSS-25-commentary §13; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Three_Hermits;
  standardebooks.org translator list.

## 6. Alchevskaya's «Что читать народу?» and peasant reactions

**Khristina Danilovna Alchevskaya** (1841–1920) ran the Kharkov Women's Sunday School
and compiled the multi-volume critical bibliography «Что читать народу?» (What to Read
to the People). Volume I appeared in **1884**; the complete three-volume set spanned
**1884–1906**. The work won the grand prize at the Paris International Exhibition (1889).
It functioned as a «Критический указатель книг для народного и детского чтения»
(critical index of books for popular and children's reading), drawing on real audience
responses from her Sunday-school literacy classes where pupils — predominantly working-
class women and peasants — were asked to react to texts read aloud.

**Tolstoy's folk tales in «Что читать народу?»:** Alchevskaya's school is documented as
testing texts on a peasant/working-class audience in the 1880s. The Wikipedia article on
Alchevska (en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khrystyna_Alchevska) does not specify Tolstoy's folk
tales among the tested items, but Vol. I (1884) predates most Posrednik publications and
so would engage mainly «Чем люди живы?» (published «Детский отдых» 1881) and early
Marakuev editions. Vol. II–III (published later in the series) more likely contain
responses to the Posrednik tales.

UNRESOLVED: the specific peasant-reader reactions to Tolstoy's folk tales as recorded by
Alchevskaya. This requires direct access to the volumes — not available via open web
sources. The Cambridge *Tolstoy in Context* chapter «Peasants and Folklore» (Chapter 5,
Cambridge University Press, DOI via cambridge.org/core) and Ben Eklof, *Russian Peasant
Schools* (UC Press, 1986) are the recommended secondary channels for this evidence.

Sources: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khrystyna_Alchevska; books.openedition.org/ledizioni/13174
(lower-class reading survey); cambridge.org/core/books/abs/tolstoy-in-context/peasants-
and-folklore/ (chapter abstract, paywalled).

## 7. Writers recruited to Posrednik

### A. N. Ostrovsky
Tolstoy wrote a formal appeal to Ostrovsky via Chertkov (November 1886, letter v85_121,
full text quoted in footnote). Tolstoy's appeal states Posrednik's mission: «Наша цель
издавать то, что доступно, нужно, понятно всем, а не маленькому кругу людей, и имеет
нравственное содержание, согласное с духом учения Христа.» Ostrovsky **died June 1886**
before responding — he did not contribute to Posrednik.
Source: _sweep_letters_chertkov.md §A. N. Ostrovsky; v85_121.

### N. S. Leskov
Leskov became close to Tolstoy in the mid-1880s; first meeting 25 April 1887 (Leskov's
request to visit noted in Wikipedia, Nikolai Leskov article). Tolstoy endorsed a Leskov
article for Posrednik in January 1887 (v86_130): «Статья Лескова, кроме языка, в
котором чувствуется искусственность, превосходна. И по мне, ничего в ней изменять не
надо, а все средства употребить, чтобы её напечатать у нас как есть. Это превосходная
вещь.»

Specific Leskov titles published by Posrednik / I. D. Sytin in the 1880s–90s:
- **«Совестный Данила»** — published by Sytin, 1890, with illustrations by I. E. Repin
  (confirmed by search results citing Moscow printing-house Sytin & Co.).
- **«Фигура»** — published by Sytin, 1890 (confirmed by search results).
- **«Скоморох Памфалон»** — first published «Исторический вестник», March 1887;
  collected May 1887. Closely associated with the Posrednik/Tolstoyan milieu (Leskov
  changed the title to obscure the religious source).
- **«Христос в гостях у мужика»** — Christmas story in the Posrednik spirit; published
  independently (confirmed by multiple Russian library sources).

The Leskov article praised by Tolstoy in v86_130 is not identified by title in the
available extract. Specific Posrednik catalogue numbers for Leskov titles — UNRESOLVED
(requires Otto 1983 dissertation or Posrednik internal catalogue).

Sources: _sweep_letters_chertkov.md §N. S. Leskov; v86_130 (footnote); vita.org.ru
Фигура; cyberleninka.ru Скоморох Памфалон article; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Leskov.

### V. M. Garshin
Vsevolod Garshin is named as a Posrednik contributor in the Wikipedia Chertkov article
(«The publication attracted … Garshin»). The PSS-25 apparatus (§15, v25_713_714) notes
that Garshin «also wrote a description of the same painting» (Bouguereau's Passion) at
the same time as Tolstoy wrote the Posrednik caption text — confirming parallel activity
in the Posrednik circle c. 1885. Specific Garshin title(s) published by Posrednik:
UNRESOLVED — no title confirmed in available sources.
Sources: PSS-25-commentary §15; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Chertkov.

### V. G. Korolenko
Named as a Posrednik contributor in the Wikipedia Chertkov article. No specific title
recovered in available sources. UNRESOLVED.
Sources: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Chertkov.

### A. I. Ertel
«Рассказы Ивана Федотыча» — accepted for Posrednik (v86_215, February 1889; published
1891). Mentioned in Chertkov letters sweep.
Source: _sweep_letters_chertkov.md §A. I. Ertel.

## 8. Contemporary critical reception in the thick journals

**N. K. Mikhailovsky** is documented as having written essays on Tolstoy's short fiction
(cited in multiple sources: Sorokin, *Tolstoy in Prerevolutionary Russian Criticism*,
Ohio State University Press; literariness.org analysis). Mikhailovsky, the leading
Populist (народнический) critic of «Отечественные записки» and «Русское богатство», was
skeptical of Tolstoy's post-crisis «moralising» turn. His critique centred on what he
called Tolstoy's retreat from social analysis into religious didacticism — the folk tales
embodied this tendency.

Specific text and date of Mikhailovsky's review of the народные рассказы: UNRESOLVED.
The most accessible secondary account is in Sorokin's Ohio State Press monograph and in
Boris Eikhenbaum's Tolstoy studies. Direct access to «Отечественные записки» /
«Русское богатство» 1885–87 runs required.

**A. M. Skabichevsky** is similarly cited among critics of the period who engaged
Tolstoy's new direction. Specific verdict on the folk tales: UNRESOLVED.

What can be said: the Populist critical camp broadly objected that the tales substituted
Christian resignation for social critique of the conditions producing peasant poverty —
precisely the opposite of what the Populist programme demanded of literature. This
critical tension is documented in secondary literature (Eikhenbaum; Sorokin) though
specific 1885–87 review texts are not recoverable via open sources in this search.

Sources: ohiostatepress.org/books/BookPages/SorokinTolstoy.html; literariness.org
/analysis-of-leo-tolstoys-stories/.

## 9. Church reaction beyond censorship

Direct Synod/clergy statements specifically targeting the folk tales: UNRESOLVED via
open sources. What is established:

- Pobedonostsev (as Ober-Procurator of the Holy Synod) instigated the February 1887
  arrest of «Чем люди живы?» (PSS-25 apparatus explicitly names him).
- The PSS apparatus (§17, v25_720_722) records that spiritual censorship banned both
  text and picture of «К картине Ге "Тайная вечеря"» on 24 February 1886 — a Posrednik-
  adjacent text blocked by ecclesiastical rather than civil censorship.
- General Posrednik: Wikipedia Chertkov article states the house published «despite the
  hostile attitude of the Eastern Orthodox Church».
- Pashkovite/sectarian publishing context: the apparatus to «Речь о народных изданиях»
  (PSS 25:874) notes that the Synod had already declared some Pashkov Society brochures
  «вредными» (harmful) — establishing the pattern of ecclesiastical censorship of cheap
  popular religious literature in this exact milieu.

No formal Synod resolution or metropolitan's pastoral letter specifically naming the
народные рассказы has been located. UNRESOLVED: specific Synod statement text and date.

Sources: PSS-25-commentary §1 (Pobedonostsev); PSS-25-commentary §17 (spiritual
censorship 24 Feb 1886); PSS 25:874 apparatus (Pashkov precedent);
en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Chertkov.

## 10. International reception — early translations and German request

### English reception
- **Nathan Haskell Dole / T. Y. Crowell (Boston):** First major American translations,
  1887–1888. Dole was Crowell's literary advisor 1887–1901. «The Three Hermits» (1887)
  is the earliest specifically dated English translation of a folk tale (multiple sources
  confirm 1887). «What Men Live By» and «How Much Land Does a Man Need?» followed in the
  same Dole/Crowell programme.
- **Isabel Hapgood:** Writing on Tolstoy censorship in 1887 for American press; her
  translations appeared from the late 1880s.
- **Aylmer and Louise Maude, *Twenty-Three Tales*** (Oxford University Press, 1906): the
  canonical collected English translation; includes all six workRecord tales.
- **Boston D. Lothrop and Company edition** of «What Men Live By» under the English
  title «What People Live By», c. 1886 (cited in Wikipedia What Men Live By article).

### German reception
- **«Барабан» / The Empty Drum** (written 1887, published 1891): the German edition
  «Die leere Trommel» exists (Amazon.de confirmed). The specific 1887 German translation
  *request* referenced in the original research brief — UNRESOLVED; no source confirming
  a 1887 Барабан German translation request has been recovered.
- General German reception of Tolstoy's folk tales was early and enthusiastic; German
  publishers were active in the late 1880s Tolstoy translation boom (confirmed in
  standard Tolstoy biography accounts), but specific edition dates for individual tales
  require specialist bibliographic sources.

Sources: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Three_Hermits (Dole 1887 date); en.wikipedia.org
/wiki/What_Men_Live_By (Lothrop c.1886); standardebooks.org translators page;
en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Empty_Drum; amazon.com Dole collections.

Byte-fidelity extracts from tolstoydigital TEI diary files.

## 1. Не убий (Thou Shalt Not Kill, 1900)

### v54_032_033_1900_08_07 · OS 7 August 1900

**Verbatim quote:**
> Кончил и отослал и Рабство нашего времени и о смерти Гумберта.

**Working gloss:** "Finished and sent off both Slavery of Our Time and [the piece] on Humbert's death."

**Why it matters:** Only diary record confirming simultaneous dispatch of both the "Не убий" response to Umberto's assassination and the parallel essay on serfdom. Fixes the completion date as 7 Aug 1900 OS. The terse phrasing — no celebration, just dispatch — is characteristic.

**People named in entry:** Меньшиков (Mikhail Menshikov, journalist, visiting — note the distancing "цепляющая рогатка"); Страхов Федор Алексеевич (Fyodor Strakhov, Tolstoyan, "весь ясный"); Маша с мужем (Maria Tolstaya and her husband).

## 2. Царю и его помощникам (To the Tsar and His Ministers, 1901)

### v54_090_093_1901_03_19 · OS 19 March 1901

**Verbatim quote (two related passages, same entry):**
> ничего не написал, кроме обращения к Царю и его помощникам, и кое какие изменения, и все скверные, в Хаджи Мурате

> студенческия истории, принявшия общественный характер и заставившия меня написать обращение к Царю и его помощникам и программу. Старался руководиться только желанием служить, а не личным удовлетворением. Еще не посылал.

**Working gloss (combined):** "The student disturbances, which took on a public character, drove me to write the address to the Tsar and his ministers, and a programme. I tried to be guided only by a wish to serve, not by personal satisfaction. Have not yet sent it."

**Why it matters:** Names the exact external trigger (student unrest → censored disturbances), records Tolstoy's self-scrutiny over motive ("не личным удовлетворением"), and establishes it was unsent on the 19th. The excommunication is mentioned as "странное отлучение" — framed as strange, not dramatic.

**People named in entry:** mentions "Александр Петрович" (unidentified visitor he treated coldly); Хаджи Мурат revision noted alongside — the two works were parallel.

## 3. Единственное средство (The Only Means, 1901)

### v54_096_096_1901_04_29 · OS 29 April 1901

**Verbatim quote:**
> Народную программу бросил.

**Working gloss:** "I've abandoned the People's Programme."

**Why it matters:** Documents that Tolstoy dropped the earlier draft ("Народная программа") — which would become the raw material for Единственное средство — as of late April. Confirms the article went through a prior conceptual stage before July.

**People named:** none named.

---

### v54_096_098_1901_05_07 · OS 7 May 1901

**Verbatim quote (contextually relevant):**
> Тут Таня милая. Получил от Черткова письмо о свободе печати и боюсь, что неприятно ответил.

**Working gloss:** "Received from Chertkov a letter about freedom of the press and fear I answered unpleasantly."

**Why it matters:** Chertkov's letter on press freedom arrives just as Tolstoy is reworking the "only means" article. The task prompt notes that Tolstoy was deliberating whether to include "свобода слова/печати" — this Chertkov letter is the correspondence that prompted that deliberation. Tolstoy's discomfort at his own reply is telling.

**People named:** Таня (Tatyana Tolstaya, daughter); Чертков (Vladimir Chertkov, publisher/editor — key correspondent on draft).

---

### v54_104_106_1901_07_16 · OS 16 July 1901

**Verbatim quote:**
> Кончил Единств[енное] средство. Не особенно хорошо, слабо.

**Working gloss:** "Finished The Only Means. Not especially good, weak."

**Why it matters:** The sole diary record of completion. The self-deprecating verdict ("слабо") is significant — Tolstoy rarely dismissed a finished political article this flatly. He had been seriously ill from 27 June; the entry notes this was written after a sustained fever that felt like a "spiritual holiday."

**People named:** Марья Александровна (Mme Schmidt, a follower — mentioned in a subsequent philosophical reflection in the same entry).

## 4. Не убий никого (Thou Shalt Not Kill Anyone, 1907)

### v56_044_048_1907_07_20 · OS 20 July 1907

**Verbatim quote:**
> Оставил Круг Чтения и по случаю заключения в тюрьму Фельтена писал брошюру «Не убий никого». Вчера, хотя и не кончил, прочел ее Черткову и другим. Теперь хочется написать письмо Столыпину и Руки вверх, пришедшее мне в голову во время игры Гольденвейзера.

**Working gloss:** "Left off Krug Chteniya and on account of Felten's imprisonment wrote the pamphlet 'Thou Shalt Not Kill Anyone.' Yesterday, though I hadn't finished it, read it to Chertkov and others. Now I want to write a letter to Stolypin and [the piece] 'Hands Up,' which came to me during Goldenwieser's playing."

**Why it matters:** The only diary entry documenting the genesis of this article. Three things stand out: (1) it displaced Krug Chteniya — the urgency was real; (2) Chertkov heard a draft reading the same day, July 19; (3) the entry reveals two further pieces being planned simultaneously: a direct letter to Prime Minister Stolypin and the unfinished "Руки вверх." The July 20 file spans the whole month since July 1 — there is no separate July 9 diary entry.

**People named:**
- Фельтен (Nikolai Felten) — publisher/distributor of "Не убий," arrested for distributing it; the direct trigger.
- Чертков (Vladimir Chertkov) — heard the draft read aloud; editorial role.
- Столыпин (Pyotr Stolypin, Prime Minister) — Tolstoy wants to address him directly.
- Гольденвейзер (Alexander Goldenweiser, pianist) — his playing sparked the "Руки вверх" idea.
- Черткова/Чертковы — "Радостное общение с ними" mentioned earlier in same entry.
- Таня с мужем; Андрюша (family visits noted at entry start).

**Note on July 1 entry (v56_044_044_1907_07_01):** Brief entry — "Ничего не могу работать. Кое что делал для Круга Чтения." — confirms he was still on Krug Chteniya and not yet working on the Felten article. No Felten reference. The July 20 entry covers the intervening period.

## 5. Смертная казнь и христианство (Capital Punishment and Christianity, 1908–09)

### v56_167_168_1908_12_27 · OS 27 December 1908

**Verbatim quote:**
> Занимался все время статьей о статье Столыпина, и кажется, напрасно.

**Working gloss:** "I've been working the whole time on the article about Stolypin's article, and it seems pointless."

**Why it matters:** The earliest located diary record of sustained work on what will become "Смертная казнь и христианство." Tolstoy frames it as a response to an article by A.A. Stolypin (brother of the Prime Minister, writing in Novoe Vremya defending capital punishment). His self-doubt ("напрасно") matches the Jan 18 tone. The "художественная работа в голове ясна" note in the same sentence shows fictional work was competing for attention.

**People named:** "петербургские студенты с адресом" (St Petersburg students with an address — arriving, complicating his day).

---

### v57_013_014_1909_01_18 · OS 18 January 1909

**Verbatim quote:**
> Нынче написал только маленькую прибавку к статье о Ст-не, прибавку о царе, с тайной целью вызвать против себя гонения. И цель не совсем хороша, а уже совсем нехорошо нелюбовное отношение к нему. Надо будет исправить.

**Working gloss:** "Today I wrote only a small addition to the article about St[olypin], an addition about the tsar, with the secret aim of provoking persecution against myself. And the aim is not entirely good, and even worse is my unloving attitude toward him. I must correct this."

**Why it matters:** The key passage: Tolstoy catches himself in a bad motive — wanting to be persecuted — and in a failure of Christian love toward the tsar. The self-correction is the most intimate evidence of how he held the article's political thrust against his own spiritual standards. The "Ст-не" abbreviation is standard for Stolypin in these entries. He is awaiting Chertkov ("Жду Черткова, ничего не буду затевать" — last line).

**People named:**
- Чертков — expected that evening; key editorial partner.
- царь (Nicholas II) — the addition about the tsar is what Tolstoy doubts.

**Note:** A second file with the date 18 Jan 1909 in the filename — v57_027_028_1909_01_18 — carries the header "18 Ф. 09" (18 February), not January. That file is not this entry. The correct Jan 18 entry is v57_013_014.

## 6. Три дня в деревне (Three Days in the Village, 1909–10)

### v57_180_181_1909_12_01 · OS 1 December 1909

**Verbatim quote:**
> Ходил утром к Курносенковой, заходил и к Шинтякову. Положение голопузых у Курносенковой ужасно. Очень хочется написать три дня в деревне.

**Working gloss:** "Walked in the morning to Kurnosenkov's, stopped by Shintakov's too. The situation of the bare-bellied [starving children] at Kurnosenkov's is terrible. I very much want to write Three Days in the Village."

**Why it matters:** First diary record of the impulse to write the work by its eventual title. The word "ужасно" is the same shock register Tolstoy uses for executions and floggings. The visit to actual peasant poverty is the direct source — not an abstract theme. "Голопузых" (literally bare-bellied) is a raw vernacular word, not a literary one.

**People named:**
- Курносенкова (a local peasant woman whose household Tolstoy visits).
- Шинтяков (another local peasant visited the same morning).
- Марья Александровна — visited on horseback that evening.
- Иван Иванович — arrived that day.

---

### v58_007_008_1910_01_13 · OS 13 January 1910

**Verbatim quote:**
> Перечитал все Три дня. И, кажется, кончу.

**Working gloss:** "Re-read all of Three Days. And it seems I'll finish it."

**Why it matters:** Documents the revision pass — he re-reads the entire work-in-progress and judges it near completion. The "кажется" (it seems) retains uncertainty. Written six weeks after the Dec 1 impulse entry, confirming sustained work through December.

**People named:** no specific people; "Саша" (daughter Alexandra Tolstaya) mentioned as ill.

---

### v58_016_016_1910_02_12 · OS 12 February 1910

**Verbatim quote:**
> Очень жалкий был бродячий мальчик из типографии, сосланный.

**Working gloss:** "There was a very pitiful wandering boy from a print shop, exiled."

**Why it matters:** "Три дня в деревне" includes a vignette of an exiled wanderer (бродяга). This real encounter — a exiled typographer boy appearing at Yasnaya Polyana — is precisely the kind of lived source material for the work's "Бродячие" section (wandering exiles). Shows the work was still being fed by observation in February.

**People named:** Буланже (Pavel Boulanger — Tolstoy wrote a letter for him about the Buddha on the same day); "Саша" (daughter, ill).

---

### v58_029_029_1910_03_25 · OS 25 March 1910

**Verbatim quote:**
> Сильная статья Короленко о смертной казни.

**Working gloss:** "Powerful article by Korolenko on capital punishment."

**Why it matters:** While "Три дня" was near final form, Tolstoy was still absorbing contemporaries writing about executions. Vladimir Korolenko's journalism on capital punishment was among the most powerful of the period. The brevity of the note — a single sentence amid a subdued personal day — suggests Tolstoy was struck but couldn't yet find a form to respond ("Или не имею силы, или не нахожу формы выразить их").

**People named:** Короленко (Vladimir Korolenko, writer and journalist); Дунаев — met on a walk (unrelated).

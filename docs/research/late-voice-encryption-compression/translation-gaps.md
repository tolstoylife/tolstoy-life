# Late texts — the English translation gap ledger

A companion to the [encryption & compression dive](index.md). The dive surfaced many late texts (1900–1910) that exist **only in Russian**, with no finished English translation. Because the platform's mission is to be an *English-language* resource on Tolstoy (see `AGENTS.md`), "untranslated late text" is a directly mission-relevant gap. This ledger maps, text by text, which of the late works the dive touches have a **published English translation** and which do not — and how complete that translation is.

**How this was checked.** The status below was verified by a web pass on 2026-06-12 (publisher pages, archive.org, WorldCat / library catalogues, Wikipedia bibliographies, the Tolstoy-translation literature), not asserted from memory. Scoring is **conservative**: where a complete English edition could not be confirmed, the row says *selection* or *uncertain*, not *translated*. Source URLs sit in each row. A few bibliographic details remain open — collected under [To verify](#to-verify) at the end.

**Status vocabulary.** `translated-complete` · `translated-selection` (a real but partial/abridged English edition) · `uncertain` (an English edition exists but its completeness is unverified) · `untranslated` (no published English edition found).

## The picture in one paragraph

The late **treatises, short articles, and fiction** are essentially all in English already — the Maude circle and the Free Age Press translated the polemics as fast as Tolstoy wrote them, and the novels and plays have many editions. The English gap is concentrated in exactly the two bodies this dive is about: the **channel** (the late **letters** — only ~7 % are in English, and the Chertkov correspondence not at all) and the **compression endpoint** (the **wisdom-anthologies** — *Круг чтения* exists only as a selection that drops all 52 weekly stories; *Мысли мудрых людей* is untranslated; *На каждый день* is uncertain). The two pillars of the dive's argument are also the two largest English gaps. One consequence worth stating plainly: for most of the letter, diary, and anthology-preface passages the dive quotes, **its own "working English" glosses are the only English rendering that exists** — which is what makes [item 3, finishing those translations](#bridge), more than housekeeping.

## Where the English gap is largest — priorities for filling it {#priorities}

Ranked for this platform's mission (an English resource on the late Tolstoy), the dive's own theme weighting it toward the **channel** and the **compression** bodies:

1. **The late letters & the Chertkov correspondence (Toms 71–89).** The whole of Thread 1 — the "can't say it" statements and the abroad-channel protocol — lives in letters that are ~93 % untranslated, with the Chertkov seam entirely absent in English. This is the single largest gap for any 1900–1910 research and the one most central to this dive. The dive's fresh sweep already glosses ~30 of these for the first time.
2. **Круг чтения — the 52 weekly stories, and a complete edition.** The compression endpoint, and its structurally-defining feature (Tolstoy's weekly choice of framing prose) is *entirely* missing from English; Sekirin gives only the daily aphorisms. Highest-value anthology target.
3. **Мысли мудрых людей на каждый день (1903).** The founding anthology, wholly untranslated — and short/tractable enough to be a realistic fill.
4. **На каждый день (1909).** First, *verify* whether Sekirin's *Wise Thoughts for Every Day* is complete; if it is a selection, the 1906→1909 step (which the dive reads as the doctrine going unsigned) is an English gap that matters editorially.
5. **The late diaries (Toms 52–58).** Selection-only, but lower priority than the letters for this theme.

## The bridge to item 3 — the dive already fills a sliver of the gap {#bridge}

The dive cites ~57 late passages, each with a *working-English* gloss. For the treatise and fiction rows above, those passages have published translations to defer to. But for most of the **letter, diary, and anthology-preface** passages — exactly the high-priority gaps — **no published English exists**, so the dive's glosses are currently the only English rendering of those specific sentences. That is the argument for upgrading the keystone glosses from "working" to finished, checked translations (item 3): it is not polishing, it is producing English that is otherwise unavailable.

## To verify {#to-verify}

Open bibliographic questions carried from the verification pass (none block the ledger; each would tighten a row):

- **На каждый день / Sekirin completeness** — confirm whether *Wise Thoughts for Every Day* (2011) is a complete or selected rendering of the 1909 work. Governs row A-3's status (uncertain → selection/complete).
- **Cote *Path of Life* (2002)** — confirm it is unabridged (no page-count check found); the *translated-complete* call rests on the publisher's description.
- **«К духовенству» and «К рабочему народу»** — English texts exist online; the original print translator/edition was not pinned down. Low stakes (both are "complete" regardless).
- **Sekirin *A Calendar of Wisdom*** — re-confirm the count of omitted weekly stories (taken here as all 52); the "selection" verdict holds either way.

## Reading

### The embedded letter (8 December 1884)

The block in guillemets (`»…«*`) is **a letter from Birukoff to S. A. Tolstoj (Sofya Andreyevna Tolstaya)**, dated *Den 8 december 1884*, recounting a sledge ride home through fresh snow with a friend ("min kära Mischa"), contrasted with the train journey that preceded it — a smoking lady in jewellery, a doctor defending capital punishment, an unconscious drunken woman, a man with a bottle, a student, a rude conductor. The arrival in the silent winter night, with Orion and Sirius above the forest, becomes the closing image: snow, fresh air, good horse, good Mischa, and the good God. The footnote `*S. A. Tolstojs arkiv` ("S. A. Tolstoj's archive") identifies the source as Sofya Andreyevna's papers — meaning the letter was preserved in her archive, not Birukoff's.

The choice of this letter as the closing image of *Andra delen* is deliberate: Birukoff identifies the moment (8 December 1884) as the one when his relationship to Tolstoy turned from disciple-to-distant-master into close friendship. The biography's first two volumes therefore *close* exactly where Birukoff's personal involvement *opens*.

### Birukoff's closing afterword

After the letter:

> *"Alltifrån denna dag förändras mitt förhållande till Leo Nikolajevitsch. Han är ej längre för mig blott det litterära geniet och den högt öfver mig stående och fjärran boende läraren. Han är mig vorden en nära förtrogen och varmt afhållen vän."*

(*"From that day on my relationship with Leo Nikolayevich changes. He is no longer for me merely the literary genius and the teacher far above me and dwelling at a distance. He has become for me a close confidant and warmly cherished friend."*)

And the forward-flag to Volume III:

> *"Och härmed afslutar jag nu denna del af Leo Tolstojs lefnadssaga. För den följande, tredje delen af biografien komma äfven mina egna personliga minnen att ligga till grund, kompletterade och jämförda med annat biografiskt material och berörande de viktigaste tilldragelserna i Leo Tolstojs lif, i hvilka det förunnats äfven mig att taga en stor och verksam del. På grund af dessa omständigheter kommer del III [??]tt i litterärt hänseende få en något afvikande karaktär."*

(*"And with this I now close this part of Leo Tolstoy's life-saga. For the following, third part of the biography my own personal memories will also lie at the foundation, supplemented and compared with other biographical material and touching on the most important events in Leo Tolstoy's life, in which it has been granted to me also to take a great and active part. On account of these circumstances Vol III will, from a literary standpoint, take on a somewhat differing character."*)

Closing invariant for the trilogy:

> *"Men för denna lefnadstecknings samtliga trenne delar skall och måste samma grundval alltid vara gällande: Sanning och kärlek till honom, hvars lifsgärning jag skildrar."*

(*"But for all three parts of this life-portrait the same foundation must always hold: Truth and love for him whose life-work I depict."*)

### Place and date

> *"Kostroma. Byn Ivanskoje, den 27 augusti 1908. P. Birukoff."*

(Kostroma [oblast]. The village of Ivanskoye, 27 August 1908.) — The Russian original of *Andra delen* corresponds to the Russian Vol II first edition, published in 1908. Birukoff's village retreat at Ivanskoye in Kostroma oblast is where he wrote from in the late 1900s.

## Why this page matters for the project

Five things this page anchors:

1. **The Swedish edition's Slutord is Birukoff's own Russian Slutord, translated.** The wording matches Russian Vol II's closing in structure and date (27 August 1908 / Byn Ivanskoje — a Birukoff dating that should be checked against the Russian Vol II's closing). The Swedish edition therefore reproduces Birukoff's authorial close, not a translator's editorial close.
2. **The biography's three-volume conception is documented in 1908.** Before Tolstoy's death (November 1910), Birukoff had already publicly framed the project as three volumes, with Vol III to be based on personal memory. The Vol IV that we actually have (1922 Berlin) was therefore not part of the original design — a finding that bears on the §5 verdict (Vol IV's standing relative to the three-volume "biographic" core).
3. **The shift in Birukoff's relationship to Tolstoy is dated to 8 December 1884.** Any Vol III content drawn from "personal memory" — the criterion Birukoff himself names — applies from this date forward. Material before 8 December 1884 in Vol III is, by Birukoff's own definition, reconstructed from sources, not memory.
4. **The closing invariant** — *"Sanning och kärlek till honom"* / "truth and love for him" — is Birukoff's authorial method statement for the whole biography. Worth carrying through verbatim into the §5 verdict if the verdict needs a frame for what Birukoff's biographical project *was*.
5. **The Russian source of the 8 December 1884 letter is named:** S. A. Tolstoj's archive — Sofya Andreyevna's papers. The same letter, in Russian, should be locatable in PSS or in the published S. A. Tolstaya correspondence; if it is, that's a check on Nordenström's translation against Birukoff's Russian against the original Russian letter.

## What this page does not yet tell us

- The corresponding page in the Russian Vol II (1908) — does Birukoff close with the same letter, the same dating, the same forward-flag to Vol III?
- Whether the Slutord runs longer than the photographed page; the closing signature suggests this is the end, but the preceding paragraphs that lead into the "vackra, poetiska skildring" (beautiful, poetic depiction) are off-camera.
- The Andra delen's preface, if any (likely a foreword by Birukoff or by the publisher) — would clarify the "granskade af Leo Tolstoj" claim.
- Whether the Swedish Tredje delen Birukoff promises was ever actually published in Swedish (it would have been a post-Tolstoy-death translation).

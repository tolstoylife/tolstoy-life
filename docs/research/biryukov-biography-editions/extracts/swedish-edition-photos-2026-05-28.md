## Decisive bibliographic findings from the sample

### 1. Russian Vol I = 1906, not 1905 (settles §1.8 open question)

**IMG_0596:** Första delen title page — identical layout to Andra delen, with "FÖRSTA DELEN" in place of "ANDRA DELEN."

**IMG_0596dating:** Title-page verso of Första delen, showing through-paper from the back. Bottom of the verso reads:

> **STOCKHOLM**
> **KUNGL. BOKTRYCKERIET. P. A. NORSTEDT & SÖNER**
> **1906**

The Swedish Första delen was printed in 1906 by **Kungl. Boktryckeriet** (the Royal Printing House — Sweden's highest-status printer, royal court and academic work). Since the Swedish edition is the *authorised translation from the Russian original*, and the translation was made contemporaneously with the Russian Vol I (Tolstoy reviewing in person, per the Vol I preface), this fixes **Russian Vol I = 1906**.

The az.lib.ru / Russian Wikipedia "[1905]" tag is therefore confirmed as a cataloguing artifact (probably the year the Posrednik series started, or the filename-prefix anchor — not the Vol I book's actual publication year). **§1.8 first open question resolved.**

### 2. Swedish Andra delen printed 1909 (Russian Vol II still 1908)

**IMG_0620:** Title-page verso of Andra delen, showing through-paper:

> **STOCKHOLM**
> **KUNGL. BOKTRYCKERIET. P. A. NORSTEDT & SÖNER**
> **1909**

The Swedish Andra delen was therefore printed in **1909** — one year after Birukoff signed the Slutord on 27 August 1908 in Ivanovskoye. The Russian Vol II first edition is dated 1908. The one-year lag between Russian and Swedish is consistent with translation production time and publishing scheduling. **The Slutord's "27 augusti 1908" is Birukoff's authorial completion date, not the Swedish print date.**

### 3. Andra delen ends at Kap. XXI Slutord — the Slutord is a chapter, not a postscript

**IMG_0647** (table of contents, page 463): Andra delen's last two listed chapters are:

> Kap. XX. *»Hvari består min tro?»* ... 377
> Kap. XXI. **Slutord.** Leo Tolstoj blir bekant med V. G. Tschertkoff. Konstnären N. N. Ge. Dennes porträtt af Tolstoj. Återresa till Jasnaja Poljana. Tolstoj förstamar under hösten 1884 på N. N. Ges landtegendom. Tolstojs första besök hos grefvinnan Sofia Andreevnas protester. Mitt första besök hos Leo Tolstoj. Dennes bref från Jasnaja Poljana. **Slutord** ... 442
> **Bibliografiskt material** ... 454

Working English for the chapter titles and Kap. XXI sub-heads:

- **Kap. XX. *»Hvari består min tro?»*** = "What does my faith consist in?" — the Swedish chapter title for Tolstoy's 1884 treatise *В чём моя вера?* / *What I Believe* (the Aylmer Maude English title). The Swedish *Hvari består* is a slightly more literal rendering than the Russian *В чём моя вера?*'s "What is my faith in?"
- **Kap. XXI. *Slutord*** = "Closing word" / "Afterword." The chapter-title-as-summary sub-heads, in order: "Leo Tolstoy becomes acquainted with V. G. Chertkov. The artist N. N. Ge. The latter's portrait of Tolstoy. Return journey to Yasnaya Polyana. Tolstoy stays with N. N. Ge on the latter's country estate in autumn 1884. Tolstoy's first attempts [at applying his new principles to property]; Countess Sofia Andreyevna's protests. My first visit to Leo Tolstoy. The latter's letter from Yasnaya Polyana. Closing word." (The Swedish text reads "förstamar" — likely a typesetter's mis-spacing of *försök* or *försämrar*; from context most plausibly "förstamar" → "tries / first attempts.")
- **Bibliografiskt material** = "Bibliographic material" (the back-matter apparatus, p. 454+).

So Kap. XXI is *titled* "Slutord" but is a **substantive narrative chapter** running pp. 442–453, covering Tolstoy's life through November–December 1884: meeting Chertkov, the Ge portrait, the return to Yasnaya Polyana, the autumn 1884 stay at Ge's country estate, Tolstoy's first attempt at applying his new principles to property, Sofya Andreyevna's protests, Birukoff's first visit to Tolstoy, and the closing 8 December 1884 sledge-ride letter to S. A. Tolstaya. The "Slutord" word appears both as the chapter title and as the page header of p. 453 where Birukoff's authorial sign-off lives.

Bibliografiskt material (Bibliography) follows from p. 454 to p. 463 (and probably further; index ends at 463 in the sample).

**Earlier extract correction:** the [`swedish-andra-delen-slutord-p453.md`](swedish-andra-delen-slutord-p453.md) extract's framing of the Slutord as Birukoff's standalone afterword was imprecise — it is **the closing chapter** of Andra delen (Kap. XXI), titled Slutord, ending with Birukoff's authorial sign-off. The content is unchanged but the framing should be corrected in next session.

### 4. The Swedish Inledning carries Tolstoy authorisation documents in translation

**IMG_0597–0604:** Birukoff's "Inledning" (Introduction) — the Swedish translation of the Russian "Предисловие к первому изданию" I already transcribed from az.lib.ru.

The Swedish Inledning **includes all the same authorisation-chain documents I already captured from the Russian preface** — S. A. Tolstaya's 19 July 1901 letter and Tolstoy's 2 December 1901 "categorically answer" letter are present. The **seven-year-period table** is reproduced as a chronological table on page 7 of the Inledning, laid out with three columns (period number, year range, "Spädperioden — innefattning eller innehåll" / period content). This is a more structured presentation than the Russian text's prose listing — Swedish gives the scheme as a table; the Russian text I have describes it in prose but the original Russian Vol I likely also presents it as a table (worth re-checking the Russian against the Swedish).

### 5. Tolstoy's own preface to his memoirs is reproduced in the Swedish edition

**IMG_0610:** A page headed "**Leo Tolstojs inledning till sina »Minnen«**" — Tolstoy's own preface to his memoirs, which Birukoff embeds. This includes a poem by Pushkin titled "Erinran" / *Воспоминание* — quoted by Tolstoy ("Med sällsporrd styrka trängde sig på mig allt detta, hvarom Puschkin talar i sin dikt »Erinran«") and reproduced as verse. The Tolstoy text is signed "Tolstoj." at the end.

**Editorial implication:** the Swedish edition's chapter and embedded-document structure mirrors the Russian's at the substantive level. The Swedish is not an abridged paraphrase — it reproduces Birukoff's full editorial apparatus including embedded primary documents like the Pushkin poem and Tolstoy's own memoir preface.

### 6. Första delen is in two booklets (häften)

**IMG_0615:** A soft-cover wrapper (the wraparound paper cover of one of the booklets) printed in red:

> **LEO TOLSTOJ**
> **HANS LIF OCH HANS VERK**
> **FÖRSTA DELEN**
> **SENARE HÄFTET**
> [monogram]
> **STOCKHOLM**
> **P. A. NORSTEDT & SÖNERS FÖRLAG**
> **Pris 4 kr. 50 öre**

"Senare häftet" = "later/second booklet." Confirms: **Första delen was issued in two paper-wrappered booklets** ("häfte" — pamphlet/booklet, the standard Swedish pre-binding format), priced 4 kr. 50 öre each. Johan's three physical books are therefore:
- Första delen, första häftet (first booklet)
- Första delen, senare häftet (second booklet)
- Andra delen (printed 1909, single volume)

The "Pris" of 4 kr 50 öre is informative: roughly the price of a popular novel in Sweden c. 1906. Norstedt was not pricing this as an academic monograph.

### 7. Andra delen begins with "Krig och Fred"

**IMG_0625:** Andra delen, FÖRSTA BOKEN / Perioden »Krig och Fred» / FÖRSTA KAPITLET. The volume opens with "Tiden närmast före författandet af romanen »Krig och Fred«." (The time immediately before the writing of the novel *War and Peace*.) Mentions Tolstoy's marriage. First quoted document: a letter Tolstoy wrote to his brother Sergei on 18 October 1862 (about three weeks after his marriage on 23 September 1862).

This confirms **Andra delen = the Tolstoy life period from late 1862 (just after the marriage) through 8 December 1884.** That maps exactly to the Russian Vol II's coverage (1862–1884) and the Slutord's closing letter on 8 December 1884.

The 11 periods go from infancy 1828 through 1905 — exactly the range Birukoff's 1906 Preface anticipated. **The Russian Vol I first edition presents the same scheme in the same form**, since the Swedish is the authorised translation. This is documentary structural evidence that the original Russian Vol I had this seven-year-period chronological apparatus from the start.

## Implications for the §5 verdict (provisional, pending full photo transcription)

The Swedish 1906 / 1909 dates and the structural identity of Swedish ↔ Russian content **strengthen** the §5 verdict on Vols I–II:

1. **The Swedish is a contemporaneous, line-by-line authorised translation of the Russian first edition.** Not an abridgement; reproduces Birukoff's full editorial apparatus including embedded primary documents.
2. **The 1909 Andra delen is the latest authorial Swedish witness for Vols I–II** — Tolstoy was alive (he died November 1910) and the Swedish therefore reflects the textual state of Russian Vols I–II *under Tolstoy's living review*. Any post-1910 Russian re-editing (e.g., Berlin 1921) is by definition unverifiable against Tolstoy himself; the 1909 Swedish is the strongest pre-mortem witness.
3. **The "Glava 10. Роман" chapter in the Russian Vol I part 2 should have a Swedish equivalent in either Första delen häfte 1 (if Russian chs 1–10 map to häfte 1) or häfte 2 (if the split point is different).** Locating it and checking whether it carries the long Arsenyeva correspondence is **the single highest-value next transcription**, because it directly disambiguates Aldanov-reconciliation 1 vs 3 — i.e., whether the 1906 first edition already had the full correspondence (reconciliation 1) or whether the long correspondence was a Berlin 1921 addition (reconciliation 3).

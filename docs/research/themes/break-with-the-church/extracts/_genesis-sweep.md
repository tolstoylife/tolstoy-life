# Composition-years witness sweep — the break with the Church (1901–1902)

Scope: PSS Tom 54 diary (all 1901 + 1902 entries, 85 files) + PSS Tom 73 letters
(1901–1902). Goal: Tolstoy's own witness to (a) the Feb 1901 excommunication and his
state of mind while writing the church-break works, and (b) the people around those works.

Dates in OS (Old Style / Julian); NS = +13 days. Every Russian quote below is
copy-pasted verbatim from the `.txt` extract named in its line. Extracts produced with
`extract_tei.py --choice=reg --notes=auto`. English is a working gloss, not a translation
of record.

The four church-break works tracked here:
- «Ответ Синоду» (Reply to the Synod) — dated 4 Apr 1901 (finished in the diary 31 Mar OS).
- «Что такое религия и в чем сущность ее?» / «О веротерпимости» (On Religion / On Religious
  Tolerance) — late 1901, Gaspra.
- «Обращение к духовенству» (To the Clergy) — autumn 1902, Yasnaya Polyana.
- «Восстановление ада» (The Restoration of Hell) — 1902. (No diary entry names it
  explicitly in Tom 54; it is absent from this witness layer — see WARNINGS.)

**11 Feb 1901 OS (24 Feb NS)** — `diary_v54_089_090_1901_02_11.txt`

> Безбожное время или новое падение Рима.
Gloss: Alive but very weak and, chiefly, ill-tempered. A flood of letters I cannot answer. One

NOTE: the file's final dateline stub is `22 Фев. 1901. М. Е. б. ж.` — i.e. the excommunication
day itself was logged with only the formula "Е. б. ж." (if I live), no text.

**THE GAP** — There is **no diary entry written between 22 Feb and 19 March 1901.** The
excommunication day and the immediate aftermath went unrecorded in real time; Tolstoy reflects
on it only on 19 March (below). This silence is itself a finding.

> За это время было странное отлучение от церкви и вызванные им выражения сочувствия, и тут же
> студенческия истории, принявшия общественный характер и заставившия меня написать обращение к
> Царю и его помощникам и программу. Старался руководиться только желанием служить, а не личным

> теле, и желудком. Нынче получше.
Gloss: In this time there was the strange excommunication from the church and the expressions of
sympathy it provoked, and right alongside it the student affairs that took on a public character
and made me write an address to the Tsar and his helpers, and a program. I tried to be guided
only by the wish to serve, not by personal satisfaction. Haven't sent it yet.
Significance: THE moment he names the excommunication — and the word he picks is «странное»
(strange), paired immediately with the «выражения сочувствия» (expressions of sympathy) it
drew. No wound, no protest: detachment plus mild bemusement. Also in this entry, the Coleridge
epigraph he later uses, and: «все наше православие есть колдовство от страха» (our whole
Orthodoxy is sorcery born of fear, item 11 of the 28 Mar entry).

> Любезный брат, Такое обращение я счел наиболее уместным потому, что обращаюсь к Вам в этом
> письме не столько как к царю, сколько как к человеку — брату. […] пишу Вам как бы с того
> света, находясь в ожидании близкой смерти.

> удаляется от православия […] вчера Аракчеевы, завтра Победоносцевы и Сипягины.
Gloss: Dear brother — I chose this address because I write to you not so much as to the tsar as
to a man, a brother […] I write as if from the other world, awaiting near death. Religious

Significance: written from his sickbed at Gaspra, the major political-religious letter. It
indicts the persecution of faith and (in the draft layer recovered by `--notes=auto`) names

church-break argument carried straight to the throne.

## (B) PEOPLE NETWORK

Every person below is named in a diary entry or letter cited above. "★" = strong wiki-entity
candidate (central to the church break). Loose-match the vault before marking any as missing
(transliteration gotcha).

★ **V. G. Chertkov (В. Г. Чертков)** — closest disciple; ran the abroad publication of the
banned works (Free Age Press / Свободное слово, England). Named: diary 9 Jan, 31 Mar, 7 May 1901;
and decisively in letter v73_245 (5 Apr 1902) as the relay through whom news of the Tsar letter
came back. NOTE: his own correspondence with Tolstoy is in PSS Toms 88/89, NOT in this corpus —
so this layer sees Chertkov only through Tolstoy's side. Wiki candidate (almost certainly already
in the vault).

★ **Grand Duke Nikolai Mikhailovich (вел. кн. Николай Михайлович / Romanov)** — historian, the
intermediary who personally delivered Tolstoy's 16 Jan 1902 letter to Nicholas II. Named: letters
v73_182, v73_200, v73_203, v73_245, v73_254 (1901–02); diary 22 Jan 1902. Wiki candidate.

★ **Nicholas II (Николай II / "любезный брат")** — the addressee of the two open letters
(v73_056, 26 Mar 1901; v73_204, 16 Jan 1902). Wiki candidate (likely present).

★ **K. P. Pobedonostsev (К. П. Победоносцев)** — Over-Procurator of the Holy Synod, architect of
the excommunication. Named in the big Tsar letter draft (v73_204) and diary 4 Nov 1902 as the
type wanting "Christianity with patriotism." Central antagonist. Wiki candidate.

★ **P. A. Boulanger (П. А. Буланже)** — Tolstoyan, editor/helper; accompanied Tolstoy to Gaspra
and was present during the writing of On Religion / On Religious Tolerance. Named: diary 10 Oct,
26 Dec 1901; letter v73_162 (3 Oct 1901, and 25 more Boulanger letters in Toms 73–74). Wiki
candidate.

★ **Aylmer Maude (Эйльмер Моод)** — English translator/biographer; the English-language conduit
for the works. Not named in the Tom-54 diary entries swept, but he is a heavy Tom 73–74
correspondent (27 letters). Wiki candidate. (Not extracted in full here — flagged for the
correspondence sub-step.)

★ **Pyotr Verigin (Пётр Веригин)** — exiled leader of the Doukhobors (the persecuted sect whose
cause runs through the church break). Named: diary 4 Nov 1902 (his visit). Wiki candidate.

**Maxim Gorky (Максим Горький)** — writer, neighbor at Gaspra during the composition winter;
warm relations. Named: diary 11 May, 29 Nov 1901, 6 Oct 1902. Wiki candidate (likely present).

**A. P. Chekhov (А. П. Чехов)** — writer, also near Gaspra; agreeable to Tolstoy. Named: diary
7 May, 29 Nov 1901. Wiki candidate (likely present).

**K. P. Pyatnitsky (К. П. Пятницкий)** — Gorky's publisher ("Znanie"); visited with Gorky.
Named: diary 6 Oct 1902. Minor; wiki candidate only if the publishing network is mapped.

**Grauberger (Граубергер)** — Tolstoyan visitor (the "prison is the only fitting residence"
remark). Named: diary 22 Jan 1902. Minor wiki candidate.

**Mikhailov + the Stundists (Михайлов и штундисты)** — sectarian acquaintances at/near Gaspra;
the living evidence for the "people leave Orthodoxy for the sects" argument in the Tsar letter.
Named: diary 29 Nov 1901. Group/minor candidate.

**Vlasov (Власов)** — wrote a "good letter" of support after the excommunication. Named: diary
28 Mar 1901. Minor (a supporter exemplar).

**Khalileev / Khalilov (Халилеев; the diary also writes "Галилову")** — a correspondent to whom
Tolstoy wrote the long letter (copied verbatim into the 26 Sep 1902 diary) on illness as a
spiritual good. Named: diary 26 Sep 1902. Minor candidate. NOTE: the diary spells the name two
ways («Галилову» in the body, «Халилееву» in the heading) — verify the canonical form.

**D. P. Sipyagin (Д. П. Сипягин)** — Interior Minister, assassinated 2 Apr 1902 OS; named as a
Pobedonostsev-type and as the "dreadful event." Named: Tsar letter v73_204; letter v73_245.
Contextual antagonist; minor wiki candidate.

**B. A. Vlasov / Serebrennikov / Yanzhul / Yartsev / Alexander Petrovich / "Member of the Court"
(Член Суда)** — peripheral names in the diaries (visitors, correspondents); not central to the
church break. Listed for completeness, not flagged as candidates.

**Antagonist absence to note:** Metropolitan **Antony (Vadkovsky)** — the metropolitan who
signed the excommunication and exchanged the famous open letters with S. A. Tolstaya — is **not
named** in any Tom-54 diary entry or Tom-73 letter swept here. (A `v80_291_M_V_Vadkovskomu`
letter exists but is Tom 80 = 1909, out of this window.) His side of the break lives in the
press/Tolstaya correspondence layer, not in Tolstoy's own 1901–02 diary.

## WARNINGS / caveats

1. **Chertkov's own letters are not in this corpus** (PSS Toms 88/89). This witness layer sees
   the most important church-break collaborator only through Tolstoy's diary and his replies.
2. **«Восстановление ада» (The Restoration of Hell, 1902) has no naming entry in Tom 54.** It is
   absent from this diary/letter witness layer; its genesis must come from the work's own
   commentary (Tom 34) and Chertkov-side sources.
3. **Filename-year vs body-date mismatch:** `diary_v54_139_140_1902_08_23.txt` and
   `_140_144_1902_08_26.txt` carry "08" in the filename but the entries are internally dated
   "23/26/27 Сент." (September). The To-the-Clergy drafting is September 1902, not August. Trust
   the body datelines.
4. **Draft layer in the Tsar letter:** the long Pobedonostsev passage in `letter_v73_204` comes
   from a draft variant recovered by `--notes=auto`; it mixes the sent text with an earlier
   redaction. Verify against PSS Tom 73 pp. 184–198 before quoting the Pobedonostsev line as
   final-text.
5. **No real-time entry for the excommunication itself** (22 Feb – 19 Mar 1901 gap); his reaction
   is retrospective (19 Mar).
6. Shell `ls`/`find` are aliased on this machine; all listing was done with `rg --files` / head.

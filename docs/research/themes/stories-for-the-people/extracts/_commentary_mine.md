## 1. Чем люди живы? / What Men Live By

**apparatus file:** v25_665_674_Chem_ljudi_zhivy  
**PSS pages:** 665–674  
**composition:** Written January 1881 at Yasnaya Polyana. Plot source: oral legend recorded from peasant storyteller V. P. Shchegolyonok (Щеголёнок) during Tolstoy's stays at YaP 1879–1881; a close analogue is in Afanasyev's "Народные русские легенды" (No. 13). Tolstoy reworked both sources substantially.  
**firstPublication:** "Детский отдых" (Children's Rest), December 1881, with illustrations by N. A. Kiselyov.  
**posrednik:** Submitted by Chertkov to Moscow Censorship Committee; approved 3 March 1885 (censor Назаревский); published spring 1885 with 4 illustrations by I. Ye. Repin. Arrested 4 February 1887 by Moscow Censorship Committee on denunciation by Pobedonostsev; subsequent editions banned 1887, 1888. Free only after 1905.  
**manuscripts/redactions:** 33 MSS (autographs + copies) in GTM (АЧ) and ATB. Two main redactions; final text heavily revised from the first draft.  
**people named:**
- В. П. Щеголёнок (Shchegolyonok) — peasant storyteller, oral source
- А. Н. Афанасьев — collector of folk legends, printed analogue
- В. Г. Чертков — submitted to censorship, edited Posrednik edition
- Назаревский — censor who approved 3 Mar 1885
- К. П. Победоносцев — Ober-Procurator, instigated 1887 arrest
- Н. Н. Ге — produced 12-illustration album, 1886
- И. Е. Репин — illustrations for 1st Posrednik edition
**keyQuotes:**
1. «Щеголёнок... рассказывал мне легенды» — Tolstoy diary note on oral source.
2. (On Ge's album illustrations): «Умилительные картинки» — Tolstoy to Chertkov.

## 5. Где любовь, там и бог / Where Love Is, God Is (Martin the Cobbler)

**apparatus file:** v25_681_686_Gde_ljubov_tam_i_bog  
**PSS pages:** 681–686  
**composition:** Source: a French story by Reuben Saillens titled "Le père Martin," which had been translated into Russian anonymously as "Дядя Мартын." Tolstoy received the Russian text from Chertkov on 17 March 1885 and reworked it extensively. In 1888 Tolstoy wrote to Saillens acknowledging "неумышленный плагиат" (unintentional plagiarism) and explaining his revision.  
**firstPublication:** Posrednik; censorship approved 24 May 1885.  
**posrednik:** Never banned; one of the most widely circulated Posrednik titles.  
**manuscripts/redactions:** 5 MSS, including pages from the original journal from which Chertkov copied the Russian text and sent to Tolstoy.  
**people named:**
- Рубен Сайлан (Reuben Saillens) — French author of the source story "Le père Martin"
- В. Г. Чертков — provided the Russian translation; Posrednik publisher
**keyQuotes:**
1. «Я написал его на основании рассказа, который мне дали, и не знал, что он уже написан и напечатан» — Tolstoy to Saillens, 1888, admitting unintentional plagiarism.
2. «неумышленный плагиат» — Tolstoy's self-description of the borrowing.

**apparatus file:** v25_710_712_Svechka  
**PSS pages:** 710–712

## 16. Сказка об Иване Дураке и его двух братьях / The Tale of Ivan the Fool

**apparatus file:** v25_715_719_Skazka_ob_Ivane_Durake_i_jego_dvuh_bratja  
**PSS pages:** 715–719  
**composition:** Written August–September 1885. A political fairy-tale satirizing militarism (Tarás the Soldier brother), capitalism (Semyon the Rich brother), and the devil's temptations — set against a peasant kingdom where no one works with their hands except for food. Chertkov suggested removing the phrase «со свиньями» (with the pigs) about the idle; Tolstoy declined.  
**firstPublication:** Posrednik; censorship approved April 1886. 2nd edition arrested February 1887; banned 1887, 1892, 1893. Freely circulated only from 1906.  
**posrednik:** One of the most politically controversial Posrednik titles. Censor Tikhon's written verdict survives in the apparatus.  
**manuscripts/redactions:** 5 MSS + corrected page-proofs.  
**people named:**
- В. Г. Чертков — Posrednik; suggested the «со свиньями» excision (rejected)
- Тихонов — censor who wrote the verdict against the 2nd edition
**keyQuotes:**
1. Censor Tиkhon's verdict (paraphrased in apparatus): the tale contains «вредное для государства» content and spreads anti-military sentiment.

**manuscripts/redactions:** 4 MSS.  
**people named:**
- Т. М. Бондарев (T. M. Bondarev) — peasant author of the underlying essay
- Глеб Успенский (Gleb Uspensky) — journalist whose article introduced Tolstoy to Bondarev, 1884
- В. Г. Чертков — editorial involvement

## Cross-Tale Facts

### The Posrednik system
- Posrednik (Посредник) was founded in early 1885 following a planning meeting in Moscow ~December 1883 / early 1884 (the Speech on Popular Editions, §28). The concept: cheap illustrated editions for peasant readers, printed by I. D. Sytin's press, distributed via railways and kolportage.
- The standard censor route was Moscow Censorship Committee (stricter) or — for some texts — Petersburg committee (more lenient; passed "Греческий учитель Сократ" unaltered, §24).
- Spiritual censorship (духовная цензура) controlled separately and blocked several texts on religious grounds: "Тайная вечеря" (§17), "Крестник" (§19), "Нагорная проповедь" (§29), "Учение двенадцати апостолов" (§23).
- Post-1905 liberation: several texts banned through the 1880s–1900s only became freely publishable after 1905–1906 ("Иван Дурак" §16, "Работник Емельян" §20, "Учение двенадцати апостолов" §23, "Крестник" §19).

### Composition cluster: May–June 1885
- Five tales share a single notebook: "Зерно с куриное яйцо" (§9), "Много ли человеку земли нужно" (§10), "Кающийся грешник" (§11). Written in concentrated burst after the Posrednik project launched.

### Source typology
- Literary/hagiographic sources: Afanasyev's Народные русские легенды (§§1, 11, 18, 19, 20); Prologue / medieval Orthodox collections (§§3, 12, 25).
- Oral / folk: Shchegolyonok at Yasnaya Polyana (§§1, 12, 13).
- Western/French: Saillens "Le père Martin" (§5); Scheffer, Bouguereau, Bouguereau paintings (§§2, 15).
- Oriental philosophy: Legge, St. Julien, Beal, Arnold (§§30, 32).
- Herodotus / classical: §10 (Bashkir land tale).

### Censorship patterns
- Arrests and bans were not random: politically satirical content ("Иван Дурак," §16) triggered secular censorship; content perceived as heterodox ("Крестник," "Тайная вечеря," "Учение апостолов") triggered spiritual censorship; pure moral tales were rarely blocked.
- "Где любовь, там и бог" (§5) was NEVER banned — the sole major Posrednik tale to pass censorship permanently without incident.
- "Чем люди живы" (§1) and "Иван Дурак" (§16) both suffered the 1887 wave of arrests triggered partly by Pobedonostsev.

### Illustration programme
- Tolstoy was closely involved in illustration choices. He specifically praised Repin's response to "Вражье лепко" (§7) and N. N. Ge's 12-illustration album for "Чем люди живы" (§1). Repin also redrew the Christ figure in the Bouguereau print (§15).
- The Posrednik illustration programme involved multiple artists; Savitsky, Kivshenko, Malyshev, and Repin are the most recurrent.

### The religious-philosophical project (1884–1886)
- Alongside the folk tales, Tolstoy pursued a parallel project to produce accessible introductions to world religious wisdom: Confucius/Lao-Tze (~1884, §30), Socrates (joint work 1885, §24), the Didache (1885, §23), Siddhartha/Buddha (1885–1886+, §32), Sermon on the Mount (never completed for Posrednik, §29). These texts represent a coherent programme, not scattered efforts.
- The Buddha project is the most extensively documented unfinished project in the volume: 3 autograph MSS, 1 Chertkov MS corrected by Tolstoy, collaboration spanning 1886–1889, involving 4 named co-workers.

### The "народные рассказы" as a set
- All core folk tales (§§1–20) were written in a 16-month window: January 1881 (§1) and then March 1885–May 1886 (§§2–20). The 1881 tale ("Чем люди живы") predates Posrednik by four years; it was written independently and only later brought into the Posrednik programme.
- The tales cluster around Simeiz/Crimea (March 1885, §4), Moscow (early 1886), and Yasnaya Polyana (spring–summer 1885).
- Tolstoy's revision practice: he consistently lengthened titles (§12), added or moved Gospel epigraphs (§8), and resisted editorial softening of harsh or controversial elements (§§7, 14, 16).

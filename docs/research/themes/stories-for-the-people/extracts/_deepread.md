## 1. KEYSTONES

### Чем люди живы (What Men Live By) — `v25_007_025_Chem_ljudi_zhivy.txt`

**Gospel epigraph (carried).** The tale opens with a chain of verses from 1 John (III–IV). First line, verbatim:

> Мы знаем, что мы перешли из смерти в жизнь, потому что любим братьев: не любящий брата пребывает в смерти (1 посл. Іоан. III, 14).

*(working English)* "We know that we have passed from death unto life, because we love the brethren: he that loveth not his brother abideth in death."

**Keystone quotes.**

> — Узнал я, что жив всякий человек не заботой о себе, а любовью.

*(working English)* "I have learned that every man lives not by care for himself, but by love."

> Понял я теперь, что кажется только людям, что они заботой о себе живы, а что живы они одною любовью. Кто в любви, тот в Боге и Бог в нем, потому что Бог есть любовь.

*(working English)* "I now understood that it only seems to people that they live by care for themselves, but that they live by love alone. He who is in love is in God, and God is in him, because God is love."

> Я понял, что Бог не хотел, чтобы люди врозь жили, и затем не открыл им того, что каждому для себя нужно, а хотел, чтоб они жили заодно, и за тем открыл им то, чтò им всем для себя и для всех нужно.

*(working English)* "I understood that God did not want people to live apart, and therefore did not reveal to them what each one needs for himself, but wanted them to live as one, and therefore revealed to them what they all need for themselves and for all."

**Moral mechanism.** The angel Mikhail, fallen for refusing to take a dying mother's soul, must learn three of God's words by living as a man: *what is in people* (love — known when Matryona pities him), *what is not given to people* (knowledge of their own bodily need — the rich man orders year-long boots and is dead by evening), and *what people live by* (love — the orphan twins survive not through their dead mother's prayer but a stranger woman's milk and pity). The tale dismantles self-provision as the basis of life: God deliberately withholds from each person the knowledge of his own need so that no one can live self-enclosed; survival is structurally outsourced to the love of others. Care-for-self is exposed as an illusion; the social fabric of mutual pity is the actual life-support.

### Где любовь, там и бог (Where Love Is, God Is) — `v25_035_045_Gde_ljubov_tam_i_bog.txt`

**Gospel epigraph.** No verse-block at the head; the Gospel is *embedded* — Avdeich reads Luke 6 and 7 inside the story, and the closing revelation quotes Matthew 25. The operative line, verbatim, near the end:

> — Так как вы сделали это одному из сих братий Моих, меньших, то сделали Мне (Матфея 25 глава).

*(working English)* "Inasmuch as ye have done it unto one of the least of these my brethren, ye have done it unto me (Matthew, chapter 25)."

**Keystone quotes.**

> — Для Бога, Мартын, жить надо. Он тебе жизнь дает, для Него и жить надо. Когда для Него жить станешь, ни о чем тужить не станешь, и всё тебе легко покажется.

*(working English)* "One must live for God, Martyn. He gives you life, and one must live for Him. When you live for Him, you will grieve over nothing, and everything will seem easy to you."

> — И взалкал Я, и вы дали Мне есть, жаждал, и вы напоили Меня, был странником, и вы приняли Меня...

*(working English)* "For I was hungry, and ye gave me meat; I was thirsty, and ye gave me drink; I was a stranger, and ye took me in…"

**Moral mechanism.** The cobbler Martyn, soured to God by his son's death, is told by a pilgrim to live *for God* and to read the Gospel; a voice promises Christ will visit "tomorrow." Christ never appears as Christ — He arrives as the freezing old soldier Stepanych, the starving soldier-wife with her infant, the apple-thief boy and the old woman. Martyn's spontaneous acts of mercy *are* the visitation; only afterward does the Matthew 25 text retroactively name what he already did. The mechanism: the looked-for divine encounter is identical with ordinary neighbour-love; "God" is not a separate event but the form of attention you give to the least person in front of you.

### Два старика (Two Old Men) — `v25_082_099_Dva_starika.txt`

**Gospel epigraph (carried).** Head-block from John 4 (the Samaritan woman). First line, verbatim:

> Иоан. IV, 19. — Женщина говорит Ему: Господи! вижу, что Ты пророк.

*(working English)* "John IV, 19. — The woman saith unto Him: Sir, I perceive that Thou art a prophet."

**Keystone quotes.**

> — Тужить?! Нет, кум! В жизнь ни о чем, кроме о грехах, не тужил. Дороже души ничего нет.

*(working English)* "Grieve?! No, friend! In all my life I have grieved over nothing but my sins. There is nothing dearer than the soul."

> А то пойдешь за морем Христа искать, а в самом себе потеряешь. Надо справить людей!

*(working English)* "Else you go seeking Christ across the sea, and lose Him in your own self. The people must be put right!"

> II понял он, что на миру по смерть велел Бог отбывать каждому свой оброк — любовью и добрыми делами.

*(working English)* "And he understood that, until death, God commanded each man to discharge his obligation in the world — through love and good deeds."

**Moral mechanism.** Two peasants set out on pilgrimage to Jerusalem. Elisei stops at a Ukrainian hut to drink, finds a family dying of famine, spends his pilgrimage money saving them, and turns home without ever reaching the holy places. Efim completes the pilgrimage — and three times sees Elisei standing in spirit at the very front, by the Holy Sepulchre, radiant. The carried epigraph (worship "in spirit and in truth," not on this mountain or in Jerusalem) is the whole argument: the literal pilgrimage is hollow; true worship is the diverted, costly, anonymous act of mercy. Elisei reaches the Sepulchre *because* he never went. The mechanism contrasts ritual destination against love-in-the-detour, and rules the detour the real arrival.

### Много ли человеку земли нужно (How Much Land Does a Man Need?) — `v25_067_078_Mnogo_li_cheloveku_zemli_nuzhno.txt`

**Gospel epigraph.** None — this is one of the non-epigraph tales (the devil-frame replaces it).

**Keystone quotes.**

> Одно горе — земли мало! А будь земли вволю, так я никого, и самого чорта не боюсь!

*(working English)* "The one trouble is — too little land! But give me land in plenty, and I fear no one, not even the devil himself!"

> «Ладно, — думает, — поспорим мы с тобой; я тебе земли много дам. Землей тебя и возьму».

*(working English)* "'Very well,' thinks [the devil], 'we'll have it out, you and I; I'll give you plenty of land. By land I'll take you.'"

> Поднял работник скребку, выкопал Пахому могилу, ровно насколько он от ног до головы захватил — три аршина, и закопал его.

*(working English)* "The labourer took up the spade, dug Pakhom a grave, exactly as long as he measured from foot to head — three arshins — and buried him."

**Moral mechanism.** Pakhom's boast that with enough land he'd fear nothing, not even the devil, is the devil's invitation. Each acquisition (the lady's plots, the Volga land, finally the Bashkir "as-much-as-you-can-walk-in-a-day" bargain) only enlarges the appetite. The famous closing answer — three arshins, grave-length — is the literalized accounting: greed is self-cancelling, the body's actual need is tiny, and the more land grasped the less life left to hold it. The dream of the devil/merchant/peasant laughing over Pakhom's own corpse seals it: acquisition is a contract with death.

### Сказка об Иване-дураке (Ivan the Fool) — `v25_115_138_Skazka_ob_Ivane_Durake_i_ego_dvuh_bratjah_Semene_voine_i_Tarase_brjuhane_i_nemoj_sestre_Malane_i_o_starom_djavole_i_treh_chertenjatah.txt`

**Gospel epigraph.** None — folk-tale frame ("В некотором царстве, в некотором государстве"). The governing maxim is embodied, not quoted: hands and the callus-test.

**Keystone quotes.**

> Только один обычай у него и есть в царстве: у кого мозоли на руках — полезай за стол, а у кого нет — тому объедки.

*(working English)* "There is only one custom in his kingdom: whoever has calluses on his hands — sit down at the table; whoever has none — gets the scraps."

> — Где нам, дуракам, знать, мы всё норовим больше руками да горбом.

*(working English)* "How should we fools know — we always try to manage more by hand and hump [hard labour]."

> Жили — работали, сами кормились и людей добрых кормили.

*(working English)* "They lived and worked, fed themselves and fed good people."

**Moral mechanism.** Ivan's two brothers take the devils' two great worldly instruments — Semyon-the-soldier takes the army (force), Taras-the-belly takes gold (money) — and both kingdoms self-destruct: armies are out-armed, money is out-bought by a richer devil-merchant. Ivan's kingdom of fools is invulnerable precisely because it refuses both: there is no money, so the gold-devil starves; no one will fight, so the conquest "is like cutting jelly." The "head-work" devil topples off the tower because head-work that despises hand-work produces nothing. The mechanism is a political fable: the state founded on labour and non-resistance cannot be captured by force or capital, because there is nothing in it for force or capital to grip. The callus-test is the constitution.

### Три старца (Three Hermits) — `v25_100_105_Tri_startsa.txt`

**Gospel epigraph (carried).** Head-block, Matthew 6:7–8. First line, verbatim:

> А молясь, не говорите лишнего, как язычники: ибо они думают, что в многословии своем будут услышаны. Не уподобляйтесь им; ибо знает Отец ваш, в чем вы имеете нужду, прежде вашего прошения у Него (Матф. VI, 7, 8).

*(working English)* "But when ye pray, use not vain repetitions, as the heathen do: for they think that they shall be heard for their much speaking. Be not ye like unto them: for your Father knoweth what things ye have need of, before ye ask Him."

**Keystone quotes.**

> И древний старец сказал: Молимся мы так: трое Вас, трое нас, помилуй нас.

*(working English)* "And the eldest hermit said: We pray thus — Three are Ye, three are we, have mercy on us."

> — Доходна до Бога и ваша молитва, старцы Божии. Не мне вас учить. Молитесь за нас грешных!

*(working English)* "Your prayer too reaches God, hermits of God. It is not for me to teach you. Pray for us sinners!"

**Moral mechanism.** A bishop, finding three illiterate hermits who pray only "Three are Ye, three are we, have mercy on us," painstakingly drills them in the correct Lord's Prayer. They forget it the moment he sails off — then run across the sea on foot to overtake the ship and ask to be taught again. The epigraph (God knows your need before you ask; no vain many-worded prayer) is enacted as reversal: the hermits' "wrong," wordless prayer carries them walking on water, while the bishop's correct catechesis is exposed as the heathen "much speaking." Authority and literacy kneel to artless faith; the bishop bows and recants.

## 2. «Речь о народных изданиях» (Speech on Popular Editions) — `v25_523_529_Rech_o_narodnyh_izdanijah.txt`

**Core argument (5–8 sentences).** Tolstoy diagnoses why thirty years of "books for the people" have failed despite an explosion of publishers. The enterprise itself he ranks as the most important work in the world — "духовное общение людей… распространение света истины… единение людей около единой истины." But the educated givers fail for three reasons: (1) they don't want to *feed* the hungry reader, they want to *tune* him into a mood convenient to themselves (the religious-mood publications that transmit a feeling instead of the knowledge that produces it); (2) they offer "ошурки" — the cracklings, the leftovers they themselves won't eat, on the assumption that what's no good for "us" is fine for "the people"; (3) they themselves are not in fact well-fed but bloated — their own culture is shallow, knowing only the last fifty years of European thought and faking acquaintance with the great minds of history. The remedy collapses the very division "народ / интеллигенция": there is no such division — the people are simply more numerous, more demanding, and *truer to the truth* than we are. We must stop teaching a separate "people" and instead all learn together, transmitting the genuine spiritual nourishment of humanity's great minds in accessible language; the people's refusal is a reliable instrument that rejects everything false, artificial, and temporary.

**Two verbatim quotes.**

> Дело это, по моему, самое важное в мире, которому только может разумный человек посвятить свои силы. Дело — в духовном общении людей. Дело в распространении света истины. Дело — в единении людей около единой истины.

*(working English)* "This work is, in my view, the most important in the world to which a rational man can devote his powers. The work is the spiritual communion of people. The work is the spreading of the light of truth. The work is the uniting of people around a single truth."

> Надо признать то, что народ есть люди такие же, как мы, только их больше нас и они требовательнее и чутче к правде, и что потому все, что не совсем хорошо для нас, совсем дурно для народа.

*(working English)* "We must recognize that the people are people just like us, only there are more of them than us, and they are more demanding and more sensitive to the truth, and that therefore everything that is not quite good for us is altogether bad for the people."

This text is the programmatic key: the народные рассказы are the practical answer to its complaint. They are written for the people *as the best, not the leftover* — and so, by the speech's own logic, they are for everyone.

## 3. THE SET — thematic map

The cycle has a single moral core stated bluntly in *What Men Live By* and *Where Love Is, God Is*: **God is love, and love is realized only as concrete mercy to the neighbour in front of you** (Matthew 25 / 1 John). Around that core the tales distribute a small set of recurring vectors:

**A. Love / God-in-the-neighbour (the devotional centre).**
*Чем люди живы*, *Где любовь, там и бог*, *Два старика*, *Три старца*, *Крестник*, the picture-text *Страдания* ("Всё, что мы сделаем другому человеку, мы сделаем Ему"). Mercy is the only worship; ritual and literacy are subordinate (*Три старца*, *Два старика*). Children/the simple as the measure: *Девчонки умнее стариков* (epigraph "Аще не будете как дети…").

**B. Labour / hands as the ground of right life.**
*Сказка об Иване-дураке* (the callus-test constitution), *Зерно с куриное яйцо* (the old world fed itself by its own labour, before buying/selling/"mine"-land was even thinkable), *Два брата и золото* (only трудом, not gold, can one serve God), *Ильяс* (loss of property *is* the road to peace — work-for-another brings the rest the rich man never had). Labour is both economic ethic and spiritual discipline.

**C. Non-acquisitiveness / the trap of surplus.**
*Много ли человеку земли нужно* (grave-length is the answer), *Два брата и золото*, *Зерно*, *Как чертенок краюшку выкупал* (surplus grain → distillery → the "lisya/volcha/svinaya krov" of drink: the devil needs only to give a man *more than he needs*). *Ильяс* is the lived inverse: счастье arrives only when nothing is left.

**D. Non-violence / non-resistance (не противься злому).**
*Свечка* and *Крестник* both carry Matthew 5:38–39 as epigraph. *Упустишь огонь — не потушишь* (Matthew 18 unforgiving-servant epigraph): resisting evil with evil burns down your own house — "зло промеж двоих," and "Чужой грех покрой, Бог два простит." *Вражье лепко, а божье крепко* (the good master disarms malice by refusing anger). *Крестник*'s whole plot is the discovery that "нельзя зло злом изводить" — evil cannot be driven out by evil, only by purified, self-forgetting love (the three parables of the rag, the brace, the fire). The picture-text *Искушение* generalizes non-resistance into a refusal of the three worldly temptations (bread/self-display/mammon).

**E. Death as judgment / the hour that unmasks.**
*Много ли человеку земли нужно* (death at the finish line), *Чем люди живы* (the rich man dead by evening, ordering year-long boots), *Кающийся грешник* (the deathbed thief admitted to paradise on love and the apostles' own remembered weakness), *Два старика* (the pilgrimage discharged "по смерть"). Death is the auditor that reveals what a life was actually *for*.

**The parable FORM.** Most tales follow an **epigraph → narrative → moral arc**: a Gospel verse-block at the head (*Чем люди живы*, *Два старика*, *Три старца*, *Свечка*, *Крестник*, *Упустишь огонь*, *Кающийся грешник*), a folk-diction narrative body, and a clinching maxim or revelation at the close that names the verse's meaning in lived terms. Variants: the Gospel is *embedded* rather than headed (*Где любовь, там и бог* reads Luke/Matthew inside the action); the epigraph sits at the *foot* (*Девчонки* closes on "Аще не будете как дети…"); or the frame is folk/diabolic with no Scripture at all (*Иван-дурак*, *Много ли*, *Емельян*, *Как чертенок*, *Ильяс*, *Зерно*). The picture-texts (*Искушение*, *Страдания*) invert the form — Scripture *above*, exhortation *below* a printed image.

**Political vs devotional tales.** The devotional tales (A, and most of D/E) work on the individual soul: one act of pity opens or closes the kingdom of heaven. The **political tales — *Сказка об Иване-дураке* and *Работник Емельян и пустой барабан*** — scale the same ethic up to power and the state, and turn satirical. Their antagonists are not the sinner's own heart but **the devil operating *through* institutions**: army, money, the conscripting/expropriating царь. *Иван* dismantles force and capital as such; *Емельян* (the only tale with almost no overt Christian apparatus — folk-magic, the soldier's-mother, the empty drum that musters and disperses armies) shows worldly power as hollow noise: the "пустой барабан" commands soldiers and then is smashed into the river, and the abusive царь simply loses his hold. Where the devotional tales answer "how shall I be saved?", the political tales answer "what is wrong with power, war, and money?" — and the answer is the same labour-and-non-resistance ethic, now wielded as a critique of the social order rather than a counsel for the heart. (*Свечка* sits on the hinge: a serf-owner story where non-resistance — Mikheev ploughing with a lit candle, refusing the murder plot — is both personal sanctity and a verdict on tyranny, the приказчик impaled by his own malice while the meek man's candle never goes out.)

## 4. FORM & POETICS — vernacular / folk-formula style

**Skaz openings — the formulaic "Жил…".** Tales begin with bare existential predication, often inverted, fairy-tale cadence:

> Жил в городе сапожник Мартын Авдеич. (`v25_035_045_Gde_ljubov_tam_i_bog.txt`)

> Жил Емельян у хозяина в работниках. (`v25_162_169_Rabotnik_Emeljan_i_pustoj_baraban.txt`)

> В некотором царстве, в некотором государстве жил-был богатый мужик. (`v25_115_138_Skazka_ob_Ivane_Durake_…txt`)

**Title-proverbs as formulaic frame.** Several titles *are* folk proverbs, and recur as the moral seal: *Упустишь огонь — не потушишь* closes on "тушить огонь надо в начале"; *Вражье лепко, а божье крепко*; *Девчонки умнее стариков*.

**Formulaic repetition / triadic structure.** The folk "rule of three" governs plot and is marked verbally — Mikhail's three smiles and three words in *Чем люди живы* ("улыбнулся в первый раз / в другой раз / в третий раз"); the three apostle-voices in *Кающийся грешник* ("Так же и я" hammered as refrain); the three head-blows of the churban in *Крестник*; Efim seeing Elisei three times. The repeated formula of the devil's exit in *Иван-дурак*:

> юркнул чертенок под землю, как камень в воду, только дыра осталась. (`v25_115_138_Skazka_ob_Ivane_Durake_…txt`)

**Peasant lexicon and diction.** Dense vernacular and dialect: «деньжонки», «куртушку», «калмыжкам», «забожился мужик» (`v25_007_025_Chem_ljudi_zhivy.txt`); «балакают», «лопотали» and transcribed Ukrainian peasant speech in *Два старика* —

> — Чого, — говорит, — тоби треба? чого треба? Нема, чоловиче, ничого. (`v25_082_099_Dva_starika.txt`)

**Oral rhythm — paired/echoing verbs, brevity.** Action rendered in short verb-strings imitating spoken telling:

> Думал, думал мудрецы… (rhythmic doubling throughout `v25_064_066_Zerno_s_kurinoe_jajtso.txt`)

> Шел, шел, приходит в лес. (`v25_147_161_Krestnik.txt`)

**Embedded proverb-speech in dialogue.** Characters talk in saws: «Куда иголка, туда и нитка» (Ivan's wife, `v25_115_138_…txt`); «без отца, матери проживут, а без Бога не проживут» (`v25_007_025_Chem_ljudi_zhivy.txt`); «час терпеть, а век жить» (Pakhom, `v25_067_078_Mnogo_li_cheloveku_zemli_nuzhno.txt`). The diction is deliberately the reader's own — the *Речь*'s principle made style: the best, in the people's own tongue.

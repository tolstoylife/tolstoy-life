## 1. What Is Religion? (1901–1902)

**Slug:** `1901-1902-what-is-religion`
**Cross-link path:** `../1901-1902-what-is-religion/index.html`

**What it covers and marquee finding.** A work-dive on *Что такое религия и в чём сущность её?* (PSS Tom 35), written August 1901 – January 1902 at Gaspra while Tolstoy was gravely ill. Its marquee finding: the treatise's definitional kernel — "True religion is such an establishment by man, in accord with his reason and knowledge, of his relation to the infinite life surrounding him" — was first written on **8 February 1901, about two weeks before the Holy Synod excommunication decree**. The decree did not prompt the argument; the argument was already forming. The dive also documents the "Church truc": Christianity's specific addition to the universal corruption template is the claim that an assembly of people (the Church) possesses institutional infallibility and can transfer it to those it chooses — the precise authority the Synod exercised against Tolstoy. That critique does not name the excommunication decree anywhere in the text.

**Cross-link hooks for the church-break dive.**

1. **The February 1901 timeline.** The what-is-religion dive establishes that Tolstoy's anti-church argument was structurally complete *before* the excommunication; the church-break dive's section on the Synod decree's timing should link there rather than re-establish this. Quote the sibling's own framing: "The decree did not prompt the argument; it sharpened the urgency of publishing it."

2. **The "Church truc" definition.** When the church-break dive explains *why* the excommunication was, for Tolstoy, theologically predictable — because the Church's defining institutional innovation is the claim of infallibility — link to the what-is-religion dive's Chapter VI exposition of that concept. The what-is-religion dive has the verbatim Russian and working-English translation of the "Church truc" passage.

3. **"Nadо торопиться."** The letter to Chertkov of 12 August 1901 ("I must hurry") and the September 1901 letter to Rusanov are already extracted and byte-verified in the what-is-religion dive. The church-break dive does not need to re-extract these; it can link and cite.

4. **Publication chain.** The what-is-religion dive documents that the treatise was banned in Russia and first published by Chertkov's Free Word press in England (1902). The church-break dive's section on the post-excommunication publishing situation should link there, since the same press and the same censorship regime govern both.

**How this dive frames the Church / excommunication / Synod.** The excommunication is the institutional backdrop — named as the context in which the treatise was written and published, with Pobedonostsev's portrait in the visual record labelled as the "Ober-Procurator of the Holy Synod who drove the excommunication decree." The Synod's action is treated as contextual to the treatise's reception, not as its occasion. The new dive will need to face the decree head-on; this sibling provides the theological grounding from Tolstoy's own text for what the decree was claiming to do.

## 2. The Kingdom of God Is Within You (1890–1893)

**Slug:** `1890-1893-the-kingdom-of-god-is-within-you`
**Cross-link path:** `../1890-1893-the-kingdom-of-god-is-within-you/index.html`

**What it covers and marquee finding.** A work-dive on *Царство Божие внутри вас* (PSS Tom 28), the great non-resistance / anti-state synthesis, written 1890–93, banned in Russia, first published in French (Paris, 1893). Marquee finding: the book that began as a preface to American non-resistance texts (Garrison, Ballou) grew into a three-year treatise arguing that Christianity is not a mysticism but a *жизнепонимание* — a practical understanding of life — and that this understanding makes participation in state violence impossible. The book's relation to the Church is stated precisely: it is among the anti-church writings that fed the hostility culminating in the 1901 excommunication, though the 1901 act named no specific works. The state's army "faces inward" — defending the government against its own subjects — and the whole system is held up by intimidation, bribery, and "hypnotisation."

**Cross-link hooks for the church-break dive.**

1. **The doctrinal lineage the Synod was responding to.** When the church-break dive reconstructs which body of Tolstoy's writing the Synod was reacting to in 1901, the Kingdom-of-God dive is the essential reference — it is "the book Eltzbacher classified Tolstoy from" and the text the Russian censorship called "the most harmful book it had ever had to ban." The new dive should link there rather than summarise the argument.

2. **"Christianity not as a mystical teaching."** The subtitle — *not as a mystical teaching but as a new understanding of life* — is Tolstoy's standing rebuke to readings that file his late religion under mysticism. The church-break dive will need to explain the *theological* basis for the Synod's action; Kingdom of God establishes that Tolstoy's counter-theology was explicit and deliberate from 1893.

3. **The censorship parallel.** Both Kingdom of God and the post-excommunication religious writings were published by the same network (Chertkov, Free Word, Biryukov) using the same smuggling infrastructure. The Kingdom-of-God dive's publication and censorship section is the fullest account of that infrastructure.

4. **The Gandhi afterlife and non-Russian reception.** If the church-break dive covers the international reaction to the excommunication — which was large and sympathetic — linking to the Kingdom-of-God dive's Gandhi section establishes that the text which most shaped international understanding of Tolstoy's religion had been circulating for seven years before the Synod acted.

**How this dive frames the Church / excommunication / Synod.** Precisely: "The book's relation to the Church belongs to the longer story the works record marks with `excommunicationRelated: true`: it is among the anti-church writings that fed the hostility culminating in the Holy Synod's 1901 edict of separation. The 1901 act named no specific works, so the tie is one of climate, not of a direct ban of this book." The new dive will need to be more specific about what the decree *did* name and cite.

## 3. The Christian Teaching (1894–1896)

**Slug:** `1894-1896-the-christian-teaching`
**Cross-link path:** `../1894-1896-the-christian-teaching/index.html`

**What it covers and marquee finding.** A work-dive on *Христианское учение* (PSS Tom 39), Tolstoy's catechism-turned-exposition, written 1894–96, published unfinished by Chertkov in 1898. Marquee finding: the treatise **systematises and calms** the Kingdom-of-God ethic but does not soften it — the anti-state and anti-church passages are as radical as anything in Kingdom of God; what changed is the form and the temperature, not the content. The state's most dangerous instrument is diffused responsibility ("I do this not on my own responsibility, but because I am carrying out the will of a higher power"); the Church's cruellest instrument is the catechising of children with lies. The book was **not singled out in the 1901 excommunication decree**, though it is part of the same theological project.

**Cross-link hooks for the church-break dive.**

1. **The "exposition of faith" that preceded the Synod's action.** The church-break dive needs to establish what Tolstoy's positive alternative to Orthodoxy *was* — what faith he was being excommunicated from (the Synod's) and what faith he was practising instead. The Christian Teaching is the fullest single-document answer. Link there rather than re-stating the catechism/exposition arc.

2. **"The danger of abstract reasoning" vs. the radical content.** The Christian Teaching establishes that Tolstoy's own self-criticism of the work (too abstract, "quite inaccessible to the common man") is entirely compatible with its radicalism on church and state. The church-break dive should not let the "quiet" reputation of this treatise suggest it was a retreat — link to the sibling's finding that the content holds.

3. **The Brotherhood Publishing Co. publisher correction.** The Christian Teaching dive corrects the slip that the 1898 publisher was Free Age Press (founded 1900); the actual publisher was Brotherhood Publishing Co. / Frederick A. Stokes. If the church-break dive mentions Chertkov's publishing operations in 1898–1901, link here rather than risk repeating the slip.

4. **"Truth cannot enter a man except through reason."** This passage from Chapter 38 (PSS 39:157) is the anti-Church anti-catechism in its most concentrated form — the direct rebuttal to the Synod's claim of institutional authority over faith. The church-break dive's section on Tolstoy's reply to the excommunication should cross-reference this.

**How this dive frames the Church / excommunication / Synod.** The Christian Teaching dive notes that the work "was not singled out in the Holy Synod's February 1901 excommunication decree (whose surviving transcription does not enumerate specific titles, and whose proximate trigger is usually taken to be *Resurrection*)." This is a useful negative result for the new dive: it clarifies that the Synod was reacting to an accumulated body of work, not any single text.

## 4. Gospel Translation (1879–1884)

**Slug:** `gospel-translation`
**Cross-link path:** `../gospel-translation/index.html`

**What it covers and marquee finding.** A dive on the history of Tolstoy's translation and harmonisation of the four Gospels (*Соединение и перевод четырёх Евангелий*, PSS Tom 24), the domestic strain it caused (Sofia Tolstaya's diaries as primary witness), and its English publication history. Marquee finding: the harmony is the **keystone of a four-part project**, not a standalone book — (1) *A Confession*, (2) *Critique of Dogmatic Theology*, (3) the Gospel harmony itself, (4) *What I Believe* — all of 1879–1884. The harmony was banned in Russia; first appeared in Geneva (1892–94); not legally published in Russia until **1957**. The institutional reckoning: "The Holy Synod excommunicated Tolstoy in 1901, citing exactly the denial of church doctrine and the supernatural that the harmony had enacted two decades earlier."

**Cross-link hooks for the church-break dive.**

1. **The deep roots of the estrangement — the 1879 turn.** The church-break dive must establish how long Tolstoy's break with Orthodoxy had been building. The gospel-translation dive documents the moment in 1879 when he turned to the Greek text because the Orthodox creed gave "no teaching whatever about life or its meaning." Link there for the origin of the twenty-year arc that ended with the 1901 decree.

2. **The Critique of Dogmatic Theology as the demolition job.** The gospel-translation dive places the *Critique of Dogmatic Theology* as the second panel of the tetralogy — the systematic demolition of the church's dogmatic apparatus. If the church-break dive traces the Synod's specific theological objections, link to the gospel-translation dive's summary of what that demolition argued.

3. **"Not a mystical teaching."** The gospel-translation dive's method section establishes why Tolstoy needed a new translation — because the church's teaching "gave no account of life at all." This is the earliest version of the critique that Kingdom of God would articulate as its subtitle. Link when framing the theological genealogy.

4. **S. A. Tolstaya's witness and the household conflict.** The gospel-translation dive has the diary evidence for how the estrangement from Orthodoxy looked from inside the household. If the church-break dive covers the family dimension of the 1901 crisis, link there rather than re-quoting the same diaries.

**How this dive frames the Church / excommunication / Synod.** Directly: "The full harmony was not legally published in Russia until the Soviet Jubilee Edition — Tom 24, 1957. The institutional reckoning came in between: the Holy Synod excommunicated Tolstoy in 1901, citing exactly the denial of church doctrine and the supernatural that the harmony had enacted two decades earlier." This is the clearest statement in any sibling dive of the direct theological line from the 1879 work to the 1901 decree.

## 5. Christian Anarchism (doctrine dive)

**Slug:** `christian-anarchism`
**Cross-link path:** `../christian-anarchism/index.html`

**What it covers and marquee finding.** A narrow primary-source dive on how Tolstoy engaged the labels *anarchist* and *Christian anarchism* across the corpus. Marquee finding: the double move — he refuses the political label, affirms the substance as religious. In the one place in all ninety PSS volumes where he uses «христианский анархизм» in his own voice (the 1901 letter to Gabriel Sacy), he holds it at arm's length — "Babism has much in common with Christian anarchism" — third-person, provisional. The phrase was not his coinage; it circulated among foreign correspondents (Schmitt, Kenworthy, Crosby) through the 1890s, and Chertkov gave it programmatic Russian form in his 1905 booklet.

**Cross-link hooks for the church-break dive.**

1. **The Sacy letter (1901) as the closest primary-voice moment.** The church-break dive covers exactly the period in which the only self-attestation of «христианский анархизм» appears. Link to the christian-anarchism dive when noting Tolstoy's own framing of the doctrine — the new dive should not re-argue the label question, which is already settled there.

2. **The Eltzbacher rejection (August 1900).** Six months before the excommunication, Tolstoy told Eltzbacher "I am not an anarchist in the sense of a political reformer." This is the best-sourced statement of what Tolstoy was and was not claiming in the months immediately before the Synod acted. The church-break dive should link there when establishing Tolstoy's own account of his position at the time.

3. **The phrase genealogy.** If the church-break dive covers the reaction of Tolstoy's international correspondents to the excommunication — Schmitt, Kenworthy, Crosby, Ortt are all present in this period — the christian-anarchism dive has already mapped that network.

4. **Chertkov's booklet as the movement's self-naming.** Chertkov's 1905 *O khristianskom anarkhizme* (Christchurch: Свободное слово) gives the movement the programmatic name that the international press would apply to the post-excommunication Tolstoy. The new dive should note this as an *external* framing and link to the christian-anarchism dive's genealogy section.

**How this dive frames the Church / excommunication / Synod.** The christian-anarchism dive does not address the excommunication directly; it is focused on the label debate. The new dive will be the primary source for the excommunication's meaning within that ideological context.

## 6. Christian Communism / Socialism (label survey — SKIM only)

**Slug:** `christian-communism-socialism`
**Cross-link path:** `../christian-communism-socialism/index.html`

**Intro and key findings summary.** A survey of where Tolstoy discusses *Christian socialism* («христианский социализм») and *Christian communism* («христианский коммунизм») across the PSS. Key finding: «христианский социализм» appears in Tolstoy's own voice only three times (1889–90), all descriptive/bibliographic — naming American papers *Dawn* and *Nationalist*, never as self-application. «Христианский коммунизм» appears **zero** times in his own voice anywhere in the 90-volume PSS. His stable position 1889–1910: the diagnosis of capitalist society is correct, but the means proposed (organised violence, state coercion) reproduce the disease.

**Overlap and label-handling note.** The church-break dive has no primary overlap with this survey. The relevant cross-link is narrow: *What Is Religion?* (PSS Tom 35) appears in this dive's table as "Re-grounds the political question in religious terms" — so any reader who arrives at the church-break dive from the socialist-label angle already has a pointer. The dive is most useful as negative evidence: Tolstoy never claimed either label, so any framing of the excommunication as a reaction to "Tolstoy's Christian communism" is external commentary, not Tolstoy's self-description. If the church-break dive mentions contemporary socialist or labour-press reactions to the excommunication, cite the christian-communism-socialism dive as authority for what label language Tolstoy himself used (and refused).

## 7. Tolstoyanism (label dive — SKIM only)

**Slug:** `tolstoyanism`
**Cross-link path:** `../tolstoyanism/index.html`

**Intro and key findings summary.** A narrow dive on where Tolstoy uses «толстовство» and «толстовцы» in his own voice. Key finding: of 44 corpus files carrying the term, only 4 are in Tolstoy's body voice — and all four are disownings. Keystone: 1897-12-02 diary — «Никакого толстовства и моего учения не было и нет» ("There is no Tolstoyism and no teaching of mine; there is one eternal, universal teaching of truth"). The label was overwhelmingly external, often applied by the same press circles that would cover the excommunication.

**Overlap and label-handling note.** The church-break dive will face heavy use of "Tolstoyan" in contemporary press accounts of the 1901 excommunication. The tolstoyanism dive establishes the canonical rule: attribute the label to the press, the Synod's framing, or third-party observers; never let it stand in the dive's own voice as a descriptor of Tolstoy or his practice. The keystone diary is the primary source for Tolstoy's own refusal. Cross-link to the tolstoyanism dive whenever the church-break dive's discussion of the excommunication's cultural reception uses or describes the "Tolstoyan" label. Also note that the 1907 Stakhovich letter shows the label had real *social force* even when it had no doctrinal reality for Tolstoy — relevant for describing the post-excommunication atmosphere.

## 8. 1903 Folk Tales — cross-link note on «Разрушение ада»

**Slug:** `1903-folk-tales`
**Cross-link path:** `../1903-folk-tales/index.html`

**What the 1903-folk-tales dive says about «Разрушение ада и восстановление его».** This tale is covered in detail in the 1903-folk-tales dive (not the church-break dive). Key facts already established there:

- The legend was conceived as a companion-illustration to the treatise *Обращение к духовенству* and has its roots in an 1879 oral legend from the skazitel' V. P. Shchegolyonok — the same reciter who fed the 1880s folk-tale cycle.
- It was **published abroad by Chertkov's Свободное слово in 1903** (Christchurch, England) — the year it was written — and was banned in Russia in 1906, 1910, and 1913, reaching legal Russian print only in 1917. Tolstoy never saw it in legal Russian print.
- Its structural anti-Church argument: "I reworked it so that people believe not in his teaching but in mine, which they call by his name... Things were going well, but I feared they might notice the too-obvious deception — and so I invented the Church."
- The 1903-folk-tales dive frames this tale as the "satirical inverse" of the unity-of-life doctrine and explicitly links it to the 1901 excommunication context as its backdrop — written two years after the decree, as a companion to an address to the clergy.

**Cross-link instruction.** The church-break dive must **not** re-cover «Разрушение ада» in its own right — the 1903-folk-tales dive owns that ground. The church-break dive should cross-link there when noting that Tolstoy's anti-Church satire in this period extended to fiction and folk-tale form, and should not re-quote from the tale without citing the sibling. The appropriate framing: the church-break dive covers the institutional event (the decree and its immediate documents); the folk-tales dive covers the literary response two years later.

## VOICE & LABELS

**Loaded terms and how this project handles them.**

The terms "heretic," "apostate," "schismatic," and "Tolstoyan" are **the Synod's language**, the Russian press's language, or mainstream scholarship's filing system. They are not this project's descriptors. The rule across all sibling dives is: attribute, never assert.

Specific guidance for the church-break dive:

- **"Heretic" / "apostate" / "schismatic."** If the Synod decree itself uses such language, quote and attribute it — "the Synod's decree described Tolstoy as…" — and let Tolstoy's own response (the reply to the Synod, the letter to the newspapers, his diaries) speak for itself. Do not adopt the Synod's vocabulary as the dive's own framing.

- **"Tolstoyan" / "the Tolstoyans."** See tolstoyanism dive above. The excommunication prompted massive international response by people who identified with Tolstoy's teaching; the press called them "Tolstoyans." Attribute that use to the press and to the movement's self-presentation; never assert it as a factual description. Tolstoy's own 1897 keystone — "There is no Tolstoyism and no teaching of mine" — predates the excommunication by four years and should be cross-referenced when the topic comes up.

- **"Chertkov the antagonist."** The Sofia-centred frame (Popoff's *Alexandra Tolstoy*, *The Last Station*) treats Chertkov as the villain who drove Tolstoy away from his family toward doctrinaire sectarianism. The project's memory (corpus-dive-ground-in-primary-not-mainstream) and the sibling dives consistently reject this frame. Chertkov in the church-break period is: the publisher of the banned religious writings, the manager of the international distribution network, and a frequent correspondent — not an antagonist. His role should be described functionally, attributed to the record, not filtered through the Last-Station lens.

- **"Excommunication" vs. "отлучение."** The Russian word is *отлучение* — separation, not excommunication in the Roman Catholic sense. The Synod's own document used careful language about this. If the dive uses "excommunication" (the established English translation), note once that the original Russian is *отлучение* — a separation from the Church, not a condemnation to hell — and that Tolstoy's reply specifically addressed this distinction.

- **The "soft Tolstoy" trap.** Multiple sibling dives flag the reflex that reads Tolstoy's calmer late register as a mellowing of his position. The Christian Teaching dive establishes this most directly: the temperature dropped, the demands did not. The church-break dive, covering the period 1901–02 when Tolstoy was gravely ill at Gaspra, should not let the illness and the quieter register of *What Is Religion?* suggest any retreat from the Kingdom-of-God ethic.

**Canonical memory: corpus-dive-ground-in-primary-not-mainstream.** The dives lead with Tolstoy's own words, Biryukov's biography, Chertkov's letters, and the prior sibling dives. Mainstream accounts — Maude, Bartlett, Popoff, Simmons — are read as contrast, not as baseline. The Synod's decree and Tolstoy's reply are the two primary institutional documents for this dive; read both before reaching for any secondary account of what the excommunication "meant."

Do not cite it as authoritative. The dedicated dives each note explicitly that the combined survey is superseded for their portion.

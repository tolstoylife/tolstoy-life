# Composition-years witness sweep — *Father Sergius* (Отец Сергий, 1890–1898)

Verbatim diary entries and letters Tolstoy wrote *while composing the novella*, capturing (a) his work-mood / self-understanding and (b) the people around the work. Every Russian quote below is reproduced exactly as `extract_tei.py --choice=reg --notes=auto` renders the TEI (so a later `verify_quotes.py` pass can confirm it), followed by a working-English rendering explicitly labelled "(working English)". Source line is given for each (TEI id + PSS Tom/page).

Scope note: the genesis runs in three bursts — the conception & main drafting of 1890, the deepening of 1891, and the 1898 completion of ch. VIII to fund the Doukhobor emigration. The marquee self-interpretation ("the main struggle is not with lust but with human glory") lives in the **letters** of Feb–March 1891, not the diary.

---

## Work-mood & self-understanding (diaries)

### 6 June 1890 — the conception note ("Started Father Sergius and thought my way into it")
- TEI id: `v51_047_048_1890_06_08` · PSS Tom 51, p. 47–48. **Filename/date caveat:** this is a *bundled retrospective* entry headed "8 июня.², 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 Июня. Я. П." The famous "Начал Отца Сергия…" line falls under the "6-го." day inside the bundle — so the PSS commentary's "т. 51, стр. 47" (= 6 June) is correct, but there is no standalone `…1890_06_06` file; it is recovered from the June-8 bundle.

> 6-го. Анненкова здесь, а приходил Пастухов с письмом грустным от Долнера. Надо написать ему. — Тоже ничего не делал и страшно тягочусь жизнью. Начал Отца Сергия и вдумался в него. Весь интерес — психологические стадии, которые он проходит.

(working English) "The 6th. Annenkova is here, and Pastukhov came with a sad letter from Dolner. Must write to him. — Likewise did nothing and am terribly weighed down by life. Started *Father Sergius* and thought my way into it. The whole interest is the psychological stages he passes through."

Significance: the foundational artistic statement of the dive — Tolstoy locates the work's interest not in plot but in the hero's interior *psychological stages*. This is the seed of the eight-chapter ladder.

### 11 June 1890 — "The very holiest, alone, is himself the devil"
- TEI id: `v51_048_049_1890_06_12` · PSS Tom 51, p. 48–49. (Bundled entry "9, 10, 11, 12 Июнь"; this thought sits under the "11" material. PSS prints the parallel notebook line at т. 51, стр. 139.)

> Самое главное, что думал, это о том, что самому самого победить нельзя. Самый святой сам — сам чорт. Надо устроить в себе обиталище достойное Бога, чтобы он пришел и вселился. —

(working English) "The chief thing I thought was that one cannot conquer oneself by oneself alone. The very holiest, by himself, is himself the devil. One must build within oneself a dwelling worthy of God, so that He may come and take up residence. —"

Significance: the theological hinge of Sergius's fall — self-reliant sanctity is demonic pride; grace, not effort, is the only ground. Recurs verbatim in the 1898 notes ("Один хорош, с людьми падает").

### 13 July 1890 — the compositional pivot ("must begin with the harlot's journey")
- TEI id: `v51_062_062_1890_07_13` · PSS Tom 51, p. 62.

> Хорошо выспался. После кофе писал Отца Сергия. — Не дурно. Но не то. Надо начать с поездки блудницы.

(working English) "Slept well. After coffee wrote *Father Sergius*. — Not bad. But not it. Must begin with the harlot's journey."

Significance: the structural turn — Tolstoy wants to open *in medias res* with Makovkina's seduction sortie rather than the biography. (Per PSS he did not in fact execute this new plan in the manuscripts.)

### 14 July 1890 — "I want to start Father Sergius again from the beginning"
- TEI id: `v51_062_062_1890_07_14` · PSS Tom 51, p. 62.

> Встал позднее. Сны всю ночь. Выпил кумысу, походил. Сажусь за статью. Хочется и начать отца Сергия сначала.

(working English) "Got up later. Dreams all night. Drank kumys, walked a bit. Sitting down to the article. I also want to start *Father Sergius* again from the beginning."

Significance: confirms the mid-1890 restlessness about the opening; the work competes for attention with his polemical "article" (the Garrison/Ballou non-resistance preface).

### 3 August 1890 — "Wrote a little of Father Sergius. It was working itself out clearly"
- TEI id: `v51_071_071_1890_08_03` · PSS Tom 51, p. 71.

> Никого не было. Только перед обедом зашел уходящий от Булыгина Виктор Николаевич. Писал немного Отца Сергия. Ясно обдумывалось.

(working English) "No one was here. Only before dinner Viktor Nikolaevich, on his way from Bulygin's, dropped in. Wrote a little of *Father Sergius*. It was being thought through clearly."

Significance: a rare day of actual drafting amid a summer mostly spent *thinking* the work; note the same letter-day brought "3 remarkable letters" incl. one from Urusov (a prototype — see below).

### 4 August 1890 — "thought well about Father Sergius… and lost the notebook"
- TEI id: `v51_071_071_1890_08_04_2` · PSS Tom 51, p. 71.

> Жив. Встал рано, купался. Молился. Думал хорошо об Отце Сергии, записал и потерял записную книжку. — Читал статью Урусова, переводил с Таней, чинил сапоги, поехали на пожар в Колпну.

(working English) "Alive. Got up early, bathed. Prayed. Thought well about *Father Sergius*, wrote it down, and lost the notebook. — Read Urusov's article, translated with Tanya, mended boots, drove to the fire at Kolpna."

Significance: the texture of the working day — the novella is one thread among prayer, manual labour, translation; and again the Urusov adjacency.

### 6 August 1890 — the Makovkina-scene note + the "harlot in heat" detail
- TEI id: `v51_072_072_1890_08_06` · PSS Tom 51, p. 72. (Note Tolstoy's own thinking on *human glory / juródstvo* in the same entry — the doctrinal core surfacing.)

> Еще думал: чтобы победить заботу о людской славе, надо заботиться о худой славе — не минуешь юродства.

(working English) "Also thought: to conquer the care for human glory, one must care for ill repute — one cannot avoid holy-foolishness (*juródstvo*)."

> Было записано к Отцу Сергию. Она объясняет свой приезд, говорит чепуху, и он верит потому, что она — красота. Она в охоте. Он не видит подвига, а напротив, ему стыдно, что он поддался. Уже после она идет в монастырь. Он не красавец, а просто лицо, щиплет себе бороду, но глаза.... и это-то разжигает ее.

(working English) "Noted for *Father Sergius*. She explains her arrival, talks nonsense, and he believes her because she is beauty. She is in heat. He sees no feat in it but, on the contrary, is ashamed that he gave way. Only afterward does she go to a convent. He is no handsome man but just a face, he plucks at his beard, but the eyes…. and it is this that inflames her."

Significance: the Makovkina temptation worked out as psychology, plus the explicit naming of *juródstvo* (the embrace of bad repute) as the answer to "human glory" — the very doctrine the marquee letters will name.

### 10 August 1890 — "Describe a new state of happiness — freedom, firmness"
- TEI id: `v51_074_074_1890_08_10_1` · PSS Tom 51, p. 74.

> К Отцу Сергию. Описать новое состояние счастия — свободы, твердости человека, потерявшего всё и не могущего упереться ни на что, кроме Бога. Он узнает впервые твердость этой опоры.

(working English) "For *Father Sergius*. Describe a new state of happiness — of the freedom, firmness of a man who has lost everything and can lean on nothing but God. For the first time he learns the firmness of this support."

Significance: the redemptive resolution in nuce — the man who has lost all human standing finds the only firm ground in God. Pairs directly with the Zolotarev letter's "lower the hands to stand on the feet."

### 11 August 1890 — the pride-and-fall structure ("pride of holiness… fell with the general and the abbot")
- TEI id: `v51_074_075_1890_08_11` · PSS Tom 51, p. 74–75.

> К О [ тцу ] С [ ергию ]. Он предался гордости святости в монастыре — и пал с генералом и игумном. В затворе, он кается и высок в то время, как приезжает блудница.

(working English) "For F[ather] S[ergius]. He gave himself over to the pride of holiness in the monastery — and fell with the general and the abbot. In seclusion he repents and stands high at the moment the harlot arrives."

Significance: the moral architecture — the *fall* is not the carnal one but the prideful submission to worldly hierarchy (the visiting general, the careerist abbot); the carnal temptation comes when he is already "high."

### 18 August 1890 — "The temptation of human glory… deceit to hide faith"
- TEI id: `v51_078_078_1890_08_18` · PSS Tom 51, p. 78.

> К Отцу Сергию. Подробность, долженствующая дать уровень реальности. Адвокат на морозе втягивает сопли. И от¹ него пахнет духами, табаком и ртом. — Всё глубже и глубже забирает эта история. Соблазн славы людской и прославления, — т. е. обман, чтоб скрыть веру. —

(working English) "For *Father Sergius*. A detail meant to give the level of reality. The lawyer in the frost sniffs back his snot. And he smells of perfume, tobacco and mouth. — Deeper and deeper this story takes hold of me. The temptation of human glory and of being glorified — i.e. a deceit, to hide faith. —"

Significance: the marquee idea stated *in the diary* before the letters — "соблазн славы людской" as the work's true subject; "всё глубже и глубже забирает эта история" registers his absorption. (The "адвокат на морозе" realism-detail is one PSS singles out.)

### 17 September 1890 — the work-day texture (wrote to Chertkov and Biryukov)
- TEI id: `v51_089_089_1890_09_17` · PSS Tom 51, p. 89.

> Был на деревне, рубил деревья с Андрияном. Написал два письма Черткову и Бирюкову.

(working English) "Was in the village, felled trees with Andriyan. Wrote two letters, to Chertkov and to Biryukov."

Significance: dates the parallel 17 Sept letter to Chertkov (below) and shows the Chertkov–Biryukov correspondence axis through which the novella's progress was reported.

### 31 October 1890 — ★ the PROTOTYPE note (Kuzminsky & Urusov)
- TEI id: `v51_098_101_1890_10_31` · PSS Tom 51, p. 98–101. **Byte caveat:** the TEI renders the surname as "Кузмннский" (double-н — a source/OCR slip for *Кузминский*); quoted verbatim below.

> Потом писал — так как тетради заключенья были у Маши, то стал писать Сергия сначала. Кое-что поправил, но, главное, уяснил себе. Надо рассказать всё, что было у него в душе: зачем и как он пошел в монахи. Большое самолюбие ( Кузмннский и Урусов ), честолюбие и потребность безукоризненности.

(working English) "Then wrote — since the notebooks of the 'conclusion' were with Masha, I began writing *Sergius* from the start. Corrected some things, but, chiefly, clarified it for myself. Must tell everything that was in his soul: why and how he became a monk. Great self-love (Kuzminsky and Urusov), ambition, and a need for impeccability."

Significance: ★ the single most important genesis datum for the *person* layer — Tolstoy explicitly grounds Sergius's defining trait (vainglorious self-love + ambition + a craving to be beyond reproach) in two real men: his brother-in-law A. M. Kuzminsky and the Tula vice-governor L. D. Urusov.

### 22 May 1891 — "Decided to finish everything begun"
- TEI id: `v52_032_034_1891_05_22` · PSS Tom 52, p. 32–34.

> Забыл записать, что один из этих последних дней я писал Отца Сергия. Решил кончить всё начатое. Написал дурно, но пригодится.

(working English) "Forgot to note that on one of these last days I wrote *Father Sergius*. Decided to finish everything begun. Wrote it badly, but it will come in useful."

Significance: opens the 1891 deepening campaign; the same entry's conspectus of "9 chapters" shows the structure firming up.

### 2 June 1891 — the merchant-woman model ("such must be the merchant's wife who seduced Father Sergius")
- TEI id: `v52_036_037_1891_06_02` · PSS Tom 52, p. 36–37.

> В Туле же видел женщину; глаза близко и прямые брови, как будто готова плакать, но пухлая, миловидная, жалкая и возбуждающая чувственность. Такая должна быть купчиха, соблазнившая Отца Сергия.

(working English) "In Tula I saw a woman; eyes set close and straight brows, as if ready to weep, but plump, comely, pitiable and arousing sensuality. Such must be the merchant's wife who seduced Father Sergius."

Significance: a live, observed model for Mar'ya (the merchant's daughter of the second temptation) — the dive's clearest instance of Tolstoy taking a face from the Tula street into the text.

### 10 June 1891 — "He learned what it means to rely on God only when he was utterly lost in people's eyes"
- TEI id: `v52_038_039_1891_06_10` · PSS Tom 52, p. 38–39.

> К Отцу Сергию. Он узнал, что значит полагаться на Бога, только тогда, когда совсем безвозвратно погиб в глазах людей. Только тогда он узнал твердость, полную жизни. Явилось полное равнодушие к людям и их действиям. Его берут, судят, допрашивают, спасают — ему всё равно. — Два состояния: первое — славы людской — тревога, второе — преданность воле Божьей, полное спокойствие.

(working English) "For *Father Sergius*. He learned what it means to rely on God only when he had perished irrevocably in people's eyes. Only then did he learn a firmness full of life. Total indifference to people and their actions appeared. They take him, try him, interrogate him, rescue him — it is all the same to him. — Two states: the first — of human glory — anxiety; the second — surrender to God's will, total calm."

Significance: PSS calls this one of the leading-idea formulations; it states the binary the whole novella dramatizes (human-glory anxiety vs. God-surrendered calm) and forecasts the ch. VIII trial-and-Siberia ending.

### 17 June 1891 — "Father Sergius waits for the sun"
- TEI id: `v52_039_040_1891_06_17` · PSS Tom 52, p. 39–40. (The Sergius note PSS prints here — «после того как он убил, сидит в темноте… заря занимается» / «Отец Сергий ждет солнца» — is in the *notebook*, т. 52, стр. 41 & 172, not in this diary entry; the diary entry itself is a travel/peasant-life day. Noted under "Notes for the orchestrator.")

> Дома невесело — раздел. Вера побранилась с матерью, Таня с Машей поссорились, Марья Федоровна мешает. Не весело.

(working English) "At home it is cheerless — the division [of property]. Vera quarreled with her mother, Tanya and Masha fell out, Mar'ya Fyodorovna gets in the way. Not cheerful."

Significance: the domestic mood-frame around the work in mid-1891; the substantive Sergius idea for this date lives in the notebook (see Notes).

### 12 August 1891 — the central maxim ("not to seek good deeds, feats…")
- TEI id: `v52_048_050_1891_08_12` · PSS Tom 52, p. 48–50. (PSS calls thought #3 here "the basic [idea] for *Father Sergius*.")

> 3) Сколько раз думал и записывал это, но опять и опять это поражает меня с новой силой: не нужно искать добрых дел, подвигов. Если только будешь делать, что от тебя требуется сейчас, в том положении, в каком ты находишься, наилучшим образом, по христиански, во всю, то жизнь будет полна, и нечего будет искать добрых дел и подвигов.

(working English) "3) How many times I have thought and written this, yet again and again it strikes me with fresh force: one need not seek good deeds, feats. If you simply do what is required of you right now, in the position you are in, in the best, Christian, all-out way, then life will be full, and there will be nothing to seek in good deeds and feats."

Significance: the ethical resolution of the novella — the renunciation of the heroic *podvig* in favour of the immediate, humble duty; this is what Sergius reaches only after his fall (the Pashenka chapter, ch. VIII).

### 18 September 1891 — work-mood, the famine turn
- TEI id: `v52_053_054_1891_09_18` · PSS Tom 52, p. 53–54. (The Sergius note PSS attaches near this date — «приступы половой похоти… весь мир потемнеет» — is the diary's general self-observation, here about money/famine; the explicit "К Отцу Сергию. От похоти затемняется мир" is the *notebook*, т. 52, стр. 186.)

> Из столовых до сих пор ничего не выходит. Боюсь, что я ошибся. Не надо искать, а только отвечать на требования.

(working English) "Nothing has yet come of the [famine] canteens. I fear I was mistaken. One must not seek, but only answer demands."

Significance: the famine-relief work that absorbs late 1891 begins to crowd out the novella; "не надо искать, а только отвечать на требования" is the same "don't seek feats" ethic now lived rather than written.

### 25 September 1891 — "Read Father Sergius… to my surprise, not bad as it is"
- TEI id: `v52_054_055_1891_09_25` · PSS Tom 52, p. 54–55.

> В Пирогове прочел Отца Сергия и, к удивлению, недурно, как есть. Начал поправлять начало, но не пошло.

(working English) "At Pirogovo I read *Father Sergius* and, to my surprise, it is not bad as it is. Began to correct the beginning, but it did not go."

Significance: marks the effective end of the 1891 campaign — he reads the draft at his brother's Pirogovo estate, judges it serviceable, fails to revise the opening, and then drops it for nearly seven years.

### 6 November 1891 — the pride / "vicious circle" formulation
- TEI id: `v52_057_058_1891_11_06` · PSS Tom 52, p. 57–58.

> Надо, чтобы он боролся с гордостью, чтоб попал в тот ложный круг, при котором смирение оказывается гордостью; чувствовал бы безвыходность своей гордости и только после падения и позора почувствовал бы, что он вырвался из этого ложного круга и может быть точно смиренен. И счастье вырваться из рук дьявола и почувствовать себя в объятиях Бога.

(working English) "He must struggle with pride, fall into that false circle in which humility turns out to be pride; he must feel the inescapability of his pride and only after the fall and disgrace feel that he has broken out of this false circle and can be truly humble. And the happiness of breaking free from the devil's hands and feeling oneself in God's embrace."

Significance: the paradox at the novella's heart — *humility-as-pride*, escapable only through public disgrace; "из рук дьявола… в объятиях Бога" closes the 1891 work with the same God-vs-devil framing as the 11 June 1890 note.

### 18 March 1895 — the seven-year lull ("wrote a little… but not well")
- TEI id: `v53_013_013_1895_03_18` · PSS Tom 53, p. 13.

> Один раз немного пописал к Отцу Сергию, но не хорошо.

(working English) "Once I wrote a little toward *Father Sergius*, but not well."

> Писательство, особенно художественное, прямо нравственно вредно мне. И я, когда я писал Хозяина и Работника, поддавался желанию славы.

(working English) "Writing, especially artistic writing, is directly morally harmful to me. And when I wrote *Master and Man*, I gave way to the desire for glory."

Significance: the lone touch on the novella in the 1893–1898 gap; the second quote is the meta-irony of the whole project — the author confessing to the very "славы людской" (desire for glory) that is Sergius's sin.

### 12 June 1898 — ★ "Quite unexpectedly began to finish Sergius"
- TEI id: `v53_196_199_1898_06_12` · PSS Tom 53, p. 196–199.

> Нынче совсем неожиданно стал доканчивать «Сергия». Нет известий из Англии. Есть очень много записанного.

(working English) "Today, quite unexpectedly, began to finish *Sergius*. No news from England. There is a great deal noted down."

Significance: opens the 1898 completion campaign (ch. VIII). "Нет известий из Англии" = the Doukhobor-emigration logistics (Chertkov in England) that motivate the rush to print.

### 14 June 1898 — "Both days I wrote Father Sergius. It clarifies nicely"
- TEI id: `v53_199_199_1898_06_14` · PSS Tom 53, p. 199.

> Вечер. Оба дня писал Отца Сергия. Недурно уясняется. Написал письма. Нынче были крестины.

(working English) "Evening. Both days I wrote *Father Sergius*. It clarifies nicely. Wrote letters. Today there was a christening."

Significance: the steady drafting of the final chapter is underway.

### 28 June 1898 — "Today wrote Father Sergius… both not bad"
- TEI id: `v53_199_201_1898_06_28` · PSS Tom 53, p. 199–201.

> Писал немного воззвание. Нынче писал Отца Сергия. И то и другое недурно. Писал довольно много писем. Вчера получил все неприятные, от Monet’a, а главное от Гали с известием, что они перессорились. Поша уезжает в Швейцарию, а Буланже в Болгарию.

(working English) "Wrote a little of the appeal. Today wrote *Father Sergius*. Both not bad. Wrote a good many letters. Yesterday received all unpleasant ones, from Monet, and chiefly from Galya with news that they have quarreled. Posha [Biryukov] is leaving for Switzerland, and Boulanger for Bulgaria."

Significance: the novella is written in tandem with the Doukhobor "appeal"; names Biryukov ("Поша") and Boulanger ("Буланже"), both in the work's network.

### 30 June 1898 — "These days wrote Sergius — not well"
- TEI id: `v53_201_203_1898_06_30` · PSS Tom 53, p. 201–203.

> Вчера получил письмо от Хилкова с письмом Miss Picard о духоборах. Написал письма Crosby и Willard’y и Хилкову. Дело духоборов важное и большое и, очевидно, выйдет из него что-то совсем не такое, что мы ладим — что это дело Божие. Нынче приехала Анненкова. Меншиков телеграфирует, что напечатает Гайдебуров с урезками. Эти дни писал Сергия — не хорошо.

(working English) "Yesterday received a letter from Khilkov enclosing Miss Picard's letter about the Doukhobors. Wrote letters to Crosby and Willard and Khilkov. The Doukhobor affair is important and large, and evidently something will come of it quite unlike what we are arranging — for it is God's work. Today Annenkova arrived. Menshikov telegraphs that Gaideb:urov will print it with cuts. These days wrote *Sergius* — not well."

Significance: ties the novella's completion directly to the Doukhobor cause and the practical question of *publishing it* (Menshikov / Gaideb:urov, "with cuts").

### 17 July 1898 — ★ the completion decision ("Decided to give my novellas… to print for the Doukhobors")
- TEI id: `v53_203_204_1898_07_17` · PSS Tom 53, p. 203–204.

> Решил отдать свои повести: Воскресение и Отец Сергий в печать для духоборов.

(working English) "Decided to give my novellas — *Resurrection* and *Father Sergius* — to print, for the Doukhobors."

> 4) К Отцу Сергию. Один хорош — с людьми падает.

(working English) "4) For *Father Sergius*. Alone he is good — among people he falls."

> 6) Нет успокоения ни тому, который живет для мирских целей среди людей, ни тому, который живет для духовной цели один. Успокоение только тогда, когда человек живет для служения Богу среди людей.

(working English) "6) There is no peace for the man who lives for worldly ends among people, nor for the man who lives for a spiritual end alone. Peace comes only when a man lives to serve God among people."

Significance: ★ the decision that the novella exists in finished form *because* of the Doukhobor emigration to Canada; #4 ("Один хорош — с людьми падает") and #6 (peace only in serving God *среди людей*) are the resolved meaning of ch. VIII — the corrective to the hermit's solitary pride.

## The marquee letters

### ★★ 16 February 1891 — to V. G. Chertkov ("the main struggle is… with human glory")
- TEI id: `v87_280_a16` · PSS Tom 87, p. 71–73. This is the marquee self-interpretation: lust is only one *step*; the real subject is "слава людская."

> О Сергие не смею думать.² А кое как не хочется. Я его и отложил от того, что он очень мне дорог. Борьба с похотью тут эпизод, или скорее одна ступень, главная борьба с другим — с славой людской. Да не хочу так рассказывать.

(working English) "I dare not think about *Sergius*. And I don't want to do it just any old way. I have set it aside precisely because it is very dear to me. The struggle with lust is here an episode, or rather one step; the main struggle is with something else — with human glory. And I don't want to tell it carelessly."

Significance: ★★ the single defining external statement of the dive. Against the popular reading of *Father Sergius* as a tale of carnal temptation, Tolstoy tells his closest collaborator that lust is merely "одна ступень" and the true theme is "слава людская" — vainglory / human esteem. Everything in the diary's pride-and-fall architecture (10 Aug 1890, 11 Aug 1890, 18 Aug 1890, 6 Nov 1891) converges here.

### ★★ 14 March 1891 — to V. P. Zolotarev ("Must lower the hands to stand on the feet")
- TEI id: `v65_254_V_P_Zolotarevu` · PSS Tom 65, p. 268–269. Carries the "two different foundations of activity" passage and the aphorism.

> Вы спрашиваете о моей истории Отца Сергия. Там я хотел бы выразить эти две различные основы деятельности. То он думал, что живет для бога, а под эту жизнь так подставилось тщеславие, что божьего ничего не осталось, и он пал; и только в падении, осрамившись навеки перед людьми, он нашел настоящую опору в боге. Надо опустить руки, чтобы стать на ноги.

(working English) "You ask about my story of *Father Sergius*. There I would like to express these two different foundations of activity. He thought he was living for God, but vainglory so substituted itself under that life that nothing of God's remained, and he fell; and only in the fall, having disgraced himself forever before people, did he find a true support in God. One must lower the hands in order to stand on the feet."

Significance: ★★ the second marquee — the clearest single-sentence statement of the novella's redemptive logic. The "two foundations" (a life secretly hollowed out by vainglory vs. the ground found only after public disgrace) and the aphorism "Надо опустить руки, чтобы стать на ноги" are the prose distillation of the 10 June 1891 diary binary and the 12 June / 17 July 1898 resolution.

### Supporting letter — 18 October 1900 (6/18 Oct, OS/NS) — to Felix Ortt, editor of *Vrede* ("a novel… but my time is short")
- TEI id: `v72_396_FeliksuOrtuFelixOrtt` · PSS Tom 72, p. 479–482. Written in German; the TEI also carries the PSS Russian translation. Ortt had asked whether the newspaper reports of Tolstoy working on a "novel *Father Sergius*" were true. (PSS commentary cites "т. 72, стр. 480" and dates it 18 Oct; the TEI header dates it "Октября 6/18" — Old/New Style — consistent.)

> Sie fragen mich, was ich arbeite? Ich habe etliche Anfänge, unter welchen auch der Roman,¹ vom welchem Sie sprechen, aber meine Zeit ist kurz und mein Gewissen fordert mich nicht dass zu schreiben, was mir angenehm ist, aber dass, [was] mir notwendig scheint.

(working English, from the German) "You ask me what I am working on? I have several beginnings, among them also the novel of which you speak, but my time is short and my conscience demands of me to write not what is pleasant to me, but what seems necessary to me."

> Я начал несколько вещей, между прочим и роман,⁶ о котором вы упоминаете, но времени у меня осталось мало, и совесть требует писать не то, что мне приятно, а то, что мне кажется нужным.

(working English, the PSS Russian rendering) "I have begun several things, among them the novel you mention, but my time is short, and conscience demands that I write not what is pleasant to me, but what seems necessary to me."

Significance: the late, valedictory witness — by 1900 Tolstoy classes *Father Sergius* as one of the unfinished "romans" he will not return to, subordinated to what "conscience" requires (the doctrinal writing). Confirms the work was never declared finished in his lifetime.

## Notes for the orchestrator

1. **Bundled diary entries — the conception note is not where the filename suggests.** The famous "Начал Отца Сергия…" 6 June 1890 line is *inside* `v51_047_048_1890_06_08.xml` (a retrospective bundle headed "8 июня… 2,3,4,5,6,7,8 Июня"), under the "6-го." day. Likewise the 11 June "Самый святой сам — сам чорт" note is in the June-9-to-12 bundle `v51_048_049_1890_06_12.xml`. PSS page references (т.51 стр.47, стр.139) are correct; there is simply no standalone `…06_06` or `…06_11` entry-file. Verify quotes against the bundle ids given above, not against a non-existent dated file.

2. **Diary vs. Notebook (Записная книжка).** Several Sergius notes the PSS commentary quotes are from Tolstoy's *notebook*, not the diary proper, and so are **not in the diary TEI files**. These include: 14 Aug 1890 «когда он падает, он видит рожи… пухлые рожи» (т.51 стр.160-ish); 22 June 1891 «когда он пал с купеческой дочерью… лучше бы ему пасть тогда с красавицей А.» (т.52 стр.46); 17 June 1891 «Отец Сергий ждет солнца» (т.52 стр.172); 18 Sept 1891 «От похоти затемняется мир» (т.52 стр.186). I captured the surrounding *diary* entries verbatim where they exist and flagged the notebook origin inline. If the dive needs these notebook lines byte-verified, the notebook may be a separate TEI tree (not searched here) or only present in the PSS PDF — worth a targeted check.

3. **3 Feb 1890 first-telling entry not found as a TEI entry-file.** The «Хохотал с добродушной Марьей Михайловной…» entry (т.51 стр.16) — the very first note — has no `v51_..._1890_02_03.xml` in the corpus (the earliest Feb file is `v51_020_021_1890_02_19`). It is preserved verbatim *inside* the genesis-commentary extract (`_genesis_commentary.txt`, line 9), which quotes it in full from PSS. I therefore quote it only via the commentary, not from a primary diary file. Flag for the person-layer (Marya Mikhailovna).

4. **Verbatim caveats for `verify_quotes.py`:** (a) the 31 Oct 1890 prototype note renders the surname as **"Кузмннский"** (double-н, a source slip) — I quoted it exactly; a normalized search for "Кузминский" will miss it. (b) The diary openers carry an `[opener]` tag and footnote-digit superscripts (e.g. "И от¹ него"); these are part of the extractor output and are preserved in the blockquotes. (c) The Ortt letter is German-primary with a PSS Russian translation appended in the same TEI; I quoted both the German marquee sentence and its Russian rendering.

5. **Ortt letter date.** PSS commentary says "18 October 1900"; the TEI header reads "Октября 6/18" (Old Style 6 / New Style 18). No discrepancy — same letter, dual-calendar. Gorky's plot-telling is dated 8 Oct 1900 (PSS), same window.

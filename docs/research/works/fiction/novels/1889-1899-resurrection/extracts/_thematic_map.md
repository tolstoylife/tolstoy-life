## Scene 1 — The courtroom and the fatal verdict error (Part I)

Maslova is tried with two co-defendants for poisoning and robbing the merchant Smelkov. The jury
(Nekhlyudov among them, having recognised her as the girl he seduced years earlier) concludes she
did not steal — so the ring was a gift, not robbery — and, exhausted, frames its answer to drop
the robbery intent. But no juror adds the parallel qualifier that she had no intent to *kill*. By
the letter of the answer she is therefore guilty of poisoning with no robbery motive: convicted
to katorga (hard labour). Tolstoy lays the blame not on malice but on fatigue, procedure, and the
foreman's omitted formula.

The foreman proposes the answer (extract line 1395):

> — Так как же, господа, — обратился старшина, — признаем виновной без умысла ограбления, и имущества не похищала.

(working English) "Well then, gentlemen," the foreman said, "we find her guilty, but with no
intent to rob, and she did not steal the property."

The decisive omission (extract lines 1409 and 1411):

> Все так устали, так запутались в спорах, что никто не догадался прибавить к ответу: да, но без намерения лишить жизни.

> Нехлюдов был так взволнован, что и он не заметил этого. В этой форме ответы и были записаны и внесены в залу суда.

(working English) "Everyone was so tired, so tangled in arguments, that no one thought to add to
the answer: yes, but with no intention of taking life." / "Nekhlyudov was so agitated that he too
failed to notice this. In this form the answers were written down and entered into the courtroom."

Tolstoy underlines the arbitrariness with a Rabelais aside (line 1413) that a verdict reached this
way is no better than throwing dice. The presiding judge, reading the form, "spread his hands"
in surprise (line 1419): the jury exonerated the theft but not the killing, so on paper Maslova
poisoned a man for no visible reason. The procedural cure — the appeal turns on exactly this gap
(line 2713) — is itself denied later in the Senate.

## Scene 2 — The land question (Part II)

Nekhlyudov, settling his late mother's estate, tries to detach himself from landed property: first
at Kuzminskoye (where he merely lowers the peasants' rent and keeps the income — which later shames
him), then at Panovo, where he attempts a fuller scheme drawn from Henry George's single tax. The
narrator states the intellectual lineage plainly: Spencer's *Social Statics* in youth, then "George"
(line 3695); George reappears as the named source of the single-tax project (lines 4049, 4279). The
peasants' response is the point of the scene: not gratitude but suspicion, splitting into a party
that sees advantage and a party that fears an incomprehensible trick.

Nekhlyudov explains the single-tax mechanism (extract line 4287):

> Как же сделать, чтобы уравнять? А так, чтобы тот, кто будет владеть хорошей, платил бы тем, которые не владеют землею, то, что его земля стоит, —сам себе отвечал Нехлюдов. — А так как трудно распределить, кто кому должен платить, и так как на общественные нужды деньги собирать нужно, то и сделать так, чтобы тот, кто владеет землей, платил бы в общество на всякие нужды то, что его земля стоит. Так всем ровно будет. Хочешь владеть землей — плати за хорошую землю больше, за плохую меньше. А не хочешь владеть — ничего не платишь, а подать на общественные нужды за тебя будут платить те, кто землей владеет.

(working English) "How is one to make it equal? Like this: let whoever holds the good land pay,
to those who hold no land, what his land is worth — Nekhlyudov answered himself. — And since it is
hard to work out who should pay whom, and since money has to be collected for common needs, then
arrange it so that whoever owns land pays into the community, for all its needs, what his land is
worth. So it will be equal for all. Want to own land — pay more for good land, less for poor. Don't
want to own — you pay nothing, and the dues for common needs are paid on your behalf by those who
do hold land."

The peasants' reception — wary, then settling on a folk-theological explanation rather than the
economics (extract lines 4307, 4313): the meeting breaks up undecided ("На этом кончилось
совещание."), and what finally reconciles the commune is an old woman's guess that the master is
"thinking of his soul" and acting "for its salvation," reinforced by the alms he hands out — not the
single-tax argument:

> На третий день однако все согласились принять предлагаемые условия и пришли к Нехлюдову объявить решение всего общества. На согласие это имело влияние высказанное одной старушкой, принятое стариками и уничтожающее всякое опасение в обмане объяснение поступка барина, состоящее в том, что барин стал о душе думать и поступает так для ее спасения.

(working English, line 4313 excerpt) "On the third day, however, they all agreed to accept the
proposed terms and came to Nekhlyudov to announce the decision of the whole commune. This consent
was influenced by an explanation voiced by one old woman, accepted by the old men, that dispelled
every fear of deceit: that the master had begun to think of his soul and was acting thus for its
salvation."

Tolstoy keeps the scheme provisional in Nekhlyudov's own mouth: handing land to peasants instead of
working it is "not the solution of the question, but a step toward its solution... a transition from
a cruder to a less crude form of violence" (line 3695). At Panovo he judges even his Kuzminskoye
arrangement self-deceiving (line 4049): owning the land at all, he now thinks, is the wrong he
cannot license by gifting part of it.

## Scene 3 — The ending: the Gospel and "resurrection" (Part III, ch. XXVIII)

In the final chapter Nekhlyudov, worn down by all he has seen in the prisons and on the road, opens
the Gospel an Englishman gave him (line 7931, Matthew 18) and reads through to the Sermon on the
Mount. He recasts the five commandments (Matt. V) not as exalted, impossible ideals but as plain,
practicable rules (lines 7991–8003) whose keeping would dissolve the violence that has appalled him
and bring the Kingdom of God on earth.

The reading itself (extract line 8007):

> Он не спал всю ночь и, как это случается со многими и многими, читающими Евангелие, в первый раз, читая, понимал во всем их значении слова, много раз читанные и незамеченные. Как губка воду, он впитывал в себя то нужное, важное и радостное, что открывалось ему в этой книге. И всё, что он читал, казалось ему знакомо, казалось,подтверждало, приводило в сознание то, что он знал уже давно, прежде, но не сознавал вполне и не верил. Теперь же он сознавал и верил.

(working English) "He did not sleep all night, and, as happens with very many who read the Gospel,
for the first time, reading, he understood in their full meaning words read many times and never
noticed. Like a sponge soaking up water, he absorbed what was needful, important, and joyful that
opened to him in this book. And everything he read seemed familiar, seemed to confirm, to bring to
consciousness what he had long known before but had not fully realised and had not believed. Now he
realised and believed."

The book's close (extract line 8017):

> С этой ночи началась для Нехлюдова совсем новая жизнь, не столько потому, что он вступил в новые условия жизни, а потому, что всё, что случилось с ним с этих пор, получало для него совсем иное, чем прежде, значение. Чем кончится этот новый период его жизни, покажет будущее.

(working English) "From that night a wholly new life began for Nekhlyudov, not so much because he
had entered new conditions of life, as because everything that happened to him from then on took on
a wholly different meaning for him than before. How this new period of his life will end, the future
will show."

The very last lines (8019–8021) carry the date `16 декабря 1899 года.` and `Конец.` ("16 December
1899" / "The End"). Note that the title's "resurrection" is realised not as a plot resolution
(Maslova does not marry him; she chooses Simonson) but as this reading-and-recognition: the
awakening is interior and the future deliberately left open ("the future will show").

## Thematic map

**Guilt and responsibility.** The engine of the book is Nekhlyudov's recognition that Maslova's
ruin began with his own seduction of her, and that his guilt is not private but structural — the
same gentry-and-state order that ruined her also seats him on the jury that condemns her. The
courtroom scene makes the point formally: the wrongful verdict is no one's deliberate crime but
everyone's diffuse negligence (fatigue, omitted formula, a procedural cure later refused). Tolstoy
keeps individual responsibility and systemic responsibility in the same frame rather than letting
either absolve the other.

**Repentance.** Nekhlyudov's turn is staged as concrete restitution attempted through institutions:
he pursues Maslova's appeal, follows her to Siberia, offers marriage, and tries to divest his land.
Tolstoy is unsentimental about it — the restitution is repeatedly refused or deflected (the appeal
fails; Maslova will not be the "object of his magnanimity," line 4595; she ultimately chooses
Simonson), so repentance is shown as a change in the penitent more than a repair of the wrong.

**Moral "resurrection" / awakening (the title).** The title names the structural device: Nekhlyudov
is "resurrected" by stages, each triggered by an institution that shocks him awake. The awakening
is completed not by action succeeding but by the Gospel reading of the final chapter, where the
Sermon on the Mount is reread as practicable law (lines 7991 ff.) and "Now he realised and believed"
(line 8007). The ending withholds closure on purpose ("the future will show," line 8017).

**The justice and prison system.** Across Parts I–III the courts and prisons are shown as machinery
that grinds without regard to guilt: the jury's blunder, the Senate's refusal, the transit of
prisoners, the deaths on the march. Nekhlyudov generalises it himself — the prosecutor who hounds a
boy who could only inspire pity, the one who "brought the reading of the Gospel under criminal law,"
and "the whole activity of the courts consists only in such senseless and cruel acts" (line 6029).
The sectarians exiled merely for reading and interpreting the Gospel (lines 4405–4417) are the
sharpest case: the legal order punishes the very text the final chapter holds up as the law.

**Land and property.** The land question (Scene 2) carries Tolstoy's Henry-George conviction that
land cannot be private property "like water, like air, like the rays of the sun" (line 4049).
But the novel dramatises the gap between the idea and the peasants: they receive the single-tax
offer with suspicion and accept it only on a folk-religious reading of the master's motive (line
4313), not the economics. Tolstoy marks the scheme as transitional, "a step toward" a solution and
"a transition from a cruder to a less crude form of violence" (line 3695) — property reform is
necessary but not yet the resolution.

**The Church.** (Cross-reference the communion/Mass centrepiece, handled separately — not re-quoted
here.) The institutional Church is presented as complicit with the state order the book indicts:
the prison priest who has "administered the oath to several tens of thousands" over the very Gospel
that forbids swearing, and is untroubled by it (line 355); the oath-administration scene (lines
359 ff.); and the sectarians punished for reading the Gospel outside church-sanctioned
interpretation (line 4417). The structural irony is that Nekhlyudov's resurrection arrives through
the Gospel text itself — read privately, from a copy handed out by an English visitor in the
prisons — and explicitly *not* through the Church that administers it.

**Structural device.** Nekhlyudov's awakening is refracted through institutions in sequence — court
(Part I), then prison and the justice apparatus, then land and property (Part II), then the Church
and the transit/penal system (Parts II–III) — each an institution he is implicated in and each a
station of his moral resurrection. The final Gospel chapter steps outside all of them: the text the
institutions distort or punish becomes, read plainly and alone, the resolution the institutions
could not provide.

# Father Sergius — redactions & textual history

Source layer for the novel-dive. NOVEL MODE: this is a SAMPLE of the redaction history with ONE keystone variant extracted first-class, not a full collation. All quoted Russian is verbatim from the PSS T. 31 variants extract (`v31_203_210_variants.txt`); the redaction narrative is from the PSS «История писания» (`_genesis_commentary.txt`, Gudziy); manuscript counts from «Описание рукописей» (`v31_267_269_manuscripts.txt`).

## Redaction history

The novella has no fair copy. Tolstoy considered it roughed-out but unfinished ("hasn't been polished"), so the surviving text is a composite of parts written at different times across 1890, 1891 and 1898. The PSS prints it from the manuscripts. The stages, in order:

- **Conception (late 1889 / Jan 1890).** Per Biryukov the idea arose late 1889 or early January 1890. After Chertkov's 28–30 Jan 1890 visit to Yasnaya Polyana, Chertkov — fearing an oral telling would discharge the impulse — asked Tolstoy to send the plot by letter, deliberately to pull him into the work. The first written trace is the 3 Feb 1890 diary entry ("history of a saint's life and a music teacher"), which already names the core motifs, including the verb «убивает» ("kills") — which the commentary explicitly attaches to the hero, who in one variant (№ 7) axed his temptress to death.

- **Stage 1 — the "epistolary first redaction" (variant № 1; manuscript № 1).** Tolstoy drafted the opening of the novella INSIDE a letter to Chertkov (PSS T. 87, pp. 12–17, dated "February 1890"; internal evidence puts the actual writing not before March, not after May 1890). It opens «Ну вот вам история. Надо поскорее рассказать, а то забуду…» and breaks off as the merry sleigh-party sets out for the hermit's cell. Holds: the Kasatsky biography, the monastery, the run-in with the careerist abbot and the general, the hermitage and healing fame, the start of the Makovkina episode. The whole text is printed as variant № 1. (Autograph: 4 postal-format leaves + 1 quarto.)

- **Stage 2 — "Chertkov's manuscript" (Чертковская рукопись; manuscript № 2; variants № 2, 4–7).** Chertkov copied the letter-draft in full, leaving wide interlinear gaps, broad margins and blank leaves for further authorial development, and returned it with the original to Tolstoy on 3 June 1890. Tolstoy then filled those gaps and continued the story across the summers of **1890 and 1891**, working it from `Ну вот вам история` to `Да, надо кончить, нет бога`. The cover bears, in Maria Lvovna's hand, "Отец Сергий. 1891 год. Лето." This is the redaction in which **Sergius murders Marya** — the second part of the manuscript (autograph) ends with the killing of Marya and Sergius's flight from the monastery. (8 large postal leaves of copy + 16 quarto leaves of autograph continuation.)

- **Stage 3 — Maria Lvovna's fair copy, 3 July 1891 (manuscript № 3).** Tolstoy's daughter Maria Lvovna recopied the Chertkov manuscript on 3 July 1891. Tolstoy entered minor corrections and appended a new three-page autograph: Sergius's meditation on suicide, the impossibility of prayer ("бога не было"), the brief dream, and the memory of the girl Pashenka. Cover, in M. L. Tolstaya's hand: "3 июль 1891 год. Отец Сергий." (52 quarto leaves + 2 fragments.) Then the work was set aside for nearly seven years.

- **Stage 4 — the 1898 Remington typescript, the FINAL redaction (manuscript № 6, with № 4/№ 5 leaves folded in).** In summer 1898, raising money for the Doukhobors' emigration to Canada, Tolstoy decided to publish three manuscript works (*Resurrection*, *The Devil*, *Father Sergius*). To do so the whole preceding redaction (from M. L. Tolstaya's copy) was retyped on a Remington. On that typescript Tolstoy: developed the fiancée backstory and the monastery/hermitage chapters; **struck out the description of Marya's murder**; added the **Pashenka episode** and the **chapter of wandering** (странничество), ending with the indifferent encounter with the smart carriage-party — Sergius "reckoned among the tramps, tried, exiled to Siberia," teaching children and nursing the sick. This added **chapter VIII** (written 1898). Marginal note in Tolstoy's hand on the last page: "Всё очень скверно." Work on the novella ends here.

Chapter attribution (PSS, approximate): chs. I–III and part of IV — 1890; second half of IV, V, VI, VII — 1891; ch. VIII — 1898.

## Keystone variant: the axe-murder of Marya (cut)

In the 1891 (Chertkov-manuscript) redaction, after his fall with the merchant's daughter Marya, Father Sergius does not merely succumb — he kills her with an axe and flees toward the river to drown himself. The final 1898 redaction CUTS the murder entirely. Verbatim, variant № 7 (рук. № 2):

> На рассвете он вышел на крыльцо. Неужели всё это было? Отец придет. Она расскажет. Она дьявол. Да что ж я сделаю? Вот он тот топор, которым я рубил палец. Он схватил топор и вбежал в келью. Она лежала раскинувшись и спала. Он подошел, примерился и, взмахнув топором, ударил ее вдоль головы ниже темени. Она не крикнула, но вся привскочила и тотчас же опять упала, а он взял свою шапку и хлеб и выбежал и пошел вниз к реке, у которой он не был 4 года. На реке шел плот. — Братцы, возьмите меня. — Кто ты? — Грешник. Свезите. Вдоль реки шла большая дорога, он пошел по ней и прошел до обеда. В обед он вошел в рожь и лег в ней. К вечеру он пришел к деревне на реке. Он не пошел в деревню, а к реке, к обрыву. Да, надо кончить, нет бога.

Working English: "At dawn he went out onto the porch. Could all of it really have happened? The father will come. She will tell. She is a devil. What am I to do? There is the very axe I chopped my finger with. He seized the axe and ran into the cell. She lay sprawled out, asleep. He went up, took aim, and, swinging the axe, struck her along the head below the crown. She did not cry out, but jerked up all over and at once fell back again; and he took his cap and bread and ran out and went down to the river, which he had not been to for four years. A raft was going down the river. — Brothers, take me. — Who are you? — A sinner. Carry me over. — Along the river ran a high road, and he set off down it and walked till dinner-time. At dinner he went into the rye and lay down in it. Toward evening he came to a village on the river. He did not go into the village, but to the river, to the bluff. Yes, it must be ended, there is no God."

How cutting it changes the reading: in the early redaction Sergius is literally a murderer — the temptation-with-Marya climaxes in homicide and an intended suicide ("надо кончить, нет бога"), making his fall a violent, irreversible crime and his story a fugitive's flight from blood-guilt. In the final text the same despair ("there is no God") is reached through the sexual fall alone; with the killing gone, the act that breaks him is his own lust and pride, not violence against another, and the door stays open to the redemptive turn (the Pashenka memory, the wandering, anonymous service). The 1898 revision thus converts a crime-and-suicide ending into a spiritual collapse from which recovery is possible — the difference between Sergius as killer and Sergius as a broken man who finds God only after being disgraced. (The very axe is the one he earlier used to chop off his own finger to resist Makovkina — so the early version turns that instrument of ascetic self-mutilation into a murder weapon.)

## Other notable variants

- **The composition plan shifted mid-1890** (variant set / commentary): by 13–14 July 1890 Tolstoy wanted to restart "from the prostitute's drive" («начать с поездки блудницы») and to begin the whole thing afresh; he never executed this re-ordering, so the published opening keeps the biography-first structure.
- **The "история жития" (saint's-life) structure**: the 3 Feb 1890 diary frames the work as the staged life of the hero (monastery → hermitage → world) plus "the music teacher" (Pashenka) — the skeleton that survives into the final text.
- **The thematic centre of gravity moved from lust to vainglory**: Tolstoy's 16 Feb 1891 letter to Chertkov reframes the novella as "not a struggle with lust but mainly a struggle with vainglory, pride — 'human glory'"; the Makovkina/Marya carnal episodes become one rung of a ladder, not the subject.
- **Variant № 2 (рук. № 2)**: a fuller, named opening ("Степан Касатский-Ростовцев," with the 300 souls / 5,600 roubles income and the four-tier anatomy of "high society") that the 1898 redaction reworked into the printed ch. I.
- **Variant № 3 (рук. № 5)**: introduces young Kasatsky's relation to Nicholas I ("Николай Павлович знал его еще в корпусе и отличал его всегда") — the seed of the love-of-the-Tsar / cadet-corps material that the 1898 manuscript № 6 develops ("впервые рассказывается о влюбленности юного Касатского в Николая Павловича и о замечании царя в кадетском корпусе").
- **Variant № 4 (рук. № 2)**: a struck-through character sketch of Makovkina (the gifted, attractive woman who understands the good but does the bad).
- **Variant № 5 (рук. № 2)**: a struck-through, more explicit version of the partition-peephole scene (Sergius watching Makovkina undress through the knot-hole and resolving to go to her before catching himself).
- **Variant № 6 (рук. № 2)**: a struck-through domestic beat in the healing-fame chapter (Sofya Ivanovna pressing food on the fasting elder).
- **Eleven editorial conjectures** in the 1911 first edition reconciling chronological contradictions across the drafts (e.g. "architect"→"advocate"; "200"→"300 versts"; year/length-of-seclusion arithmetic). The PSS adopts only four of them (nos. 3, 4, 6, 9).

## The censored Nicholas-I passages

First publication: *Posthumous Artistic Works* vol. II, ed. Chertkov, Moscow **1911**, pp. 3–48, in eight chapters. The Russian 1911 edition was forced by the censor to make a series of cuts in the early chapters, marked by rows of ellipses. Per the commentary: "Пропуски касались упоминаний Николая I и особенно его любовных историй" — the cuts concerned **references to Nicholas I and especially his love affairs** (Kasatsky's youthful adoration of the Tsar; the cadet-corps episode; the strand in which the fiancée had been the mistress of "an important personage" understood to be the Emperor). The **Berlin 1912 edition** (Chertkov, vol. II) restored all the censored passages, set off in **square brackets** [ ], and renumbered the chapters (six instead of eight); it carried fewer editorial corrections than the Moscow text.

The variants and commentary extracts do NOT reproduce the bracketed Berlin-1912 text verbatim — they only describe what was cut and where it was restored. So there is no byte-fidelity quote to capture here; the cut material is identified by subject (Nicholas I and his love affairs) and by the Berlin restoration mechanism (square brackets).

# Concept / philosopher juxtaposition map — *What Is Art?* (Что такое искусство?, 1897–1898)

How Tolstoy reads and judges Western aesthetics. Source: `v30_027_203_Chto_takoe_iskusstvo.txt` (verbatim modern-orthography Russian, extracted from PSS vol. 30). The survey-and-demolition of the «красота» (beauty) criterion runs across Chapters III–V; the verdict on bad/counterfeit art arrives in Chs IX–XIII.

**Reading conventions.** The *primary source leads*: each row anchors on Tolstoy's own catalogue entry (verbatim Russian + working-English translation). The "received reading" column is the scholarly-consensus *foil* — it is offered as the mainstream understanding a reader can hold up against Tolstoy's rendering, **not** as a truth Tolstoy "got wrong." All "faithful or flattened?" judgements are the dive author's own analytical reading, not settled fact. Verbatim Russian quotes are copied byte-for-byte from the extract (footnote superscripts omitted; quoted spans chosen to avoid OCR artifacts). Foreign names and titles are reproduced as Tolstoy spells them.

| Thinker / concept | The received reading (scholarly-consensus foil) | Tolstoy's rendering & verdict | Faithful or flattened? (dive author's reading) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Баумгартен / Alexander Baumgarten** (coined "aesthetics") | On the consensus view, Baumgarten founded *aesthetica* (1750) as the "science of sensory cognition," a sister to logic — beauty being the perfection of sensible knowledge, not a moral or hedonic category. | Tolstoy makes him the founder and the original sin of the whole discipline. «По Баумгартену, объект логического познания есть истина; объект эстетического (т. е. чувственного) познания есть красота. Красота есть совершенное (абсолютное), познанное чувством.» **(working English)** "According to Baumgarten, the object of logical cognition is truth; the object of aesthetic (i.e. sensory) cognition is beauty. Beauty is the perfect (absolute), cognized by feeling." (Ch III) Verdict: the founding error — beauty installed as the criterion of art, and the spurious triad Истина–Красота–Добро that follows. | Largely faithful as a summary of Baumgarten's *scheme*, but Tolstoy frames the whole programme as педантическая (pedantic) German invention catering «по вкусу культурной толпе» (Ch VII), which caricatures Baumgarten's epistemological ambition as mere class-flattery. |
| **Кант / Immanuel Kant** (37 raw mentions — the most-cited authority) | The consensus reads Kant's *Critique of Judgement* as grounding the judgement of taste in *disinterested* pleasure — beauty as "purposiveness without purpose," subjectively universal, free of concept and of desire — i.e. a deliberately autonomous account that refuses to reduce beauty to mere agreeableness. | Tolstoy actually rates Kant the clearest: «является опять совершенно новая эстетическая теория Канта (1724—1804), более всех других уясняющая сущность понятия красоты». He renders the core as «способность суждения (Urtheilskraft), которая составляет суждения без понятий и производит удовольствие без желания: „Urtheil ohne Begriff und Vergnügen ohne Begehren“». **(working English)** "the faculty of judgement (Urtheilskraft), which forms judgements without concepts and produces pleasure without desire." (Ch III) Verdict (Ch IV): Kant's "disinterested pleasure" is, "as it should be recognized," just *что нам нравится* — what pleases us — so it too collapses into a definition of beauty as enjoyment. | **Flattened.** Tolstoy weaponizes Kant's own *disinterestedness* against him: he concedes the formula then re-reads "pleasure without desire" as a species of наслаждение (enjoyment), which is precisely the reduction Kant's disinterestedness was built to block. The dive author reads this as a deliberate flattening of Kant's autonomy thesis into hedonism. |
| **Шеллинг / Friedrich Schelling** | Consensus: in Schelling's *System des transzendentalen Idealismus*, art is the supreme organon of philosophy — the point where the unconscious and conscious, real and ideal, are intuited as one; beauty is the infinite presented in finite form. | «По Шеллингу, искусство есть произведение или последствие того мировоззрения... Красота есть представление бесконечного в конечном. И главный характер произведения искусства есть бессознательная бесконечность.» **(working English)** "According to Schelling, art is the product or consequence of that worldview by which the subject turns into its object... Beauty is the presentation of the infinite in the finite. And the chief character of a work of art is unconscious infinity." (Ch III) Verdict: filed with the объективно-мистическое (objective-mystical) camp (Ch IV) — fantastical, unfounded. | Faithful as a *précis* of Schelling's formulas (the German Idealist phrasing is reported accurately), but Tolstoy's verdict — туманно и мистично (foggy and mystical) — declines to engage the system on its own terms and dismisses by labelling. The dive author reads the summary as accurate, the dismissal as caricature-by-category. |
| **Гегель / G. W. F. Hegel** | Consensus: for Hegel beauty is *das sinnliche Scheinen der Idee* — the sensuous shining-forth of the Idea — art being one of the three modes (with religion and philosophy) by which Spirit comes to self-knowledge. | «По Гегелю (1770—1831), Бог проявляется в природе и искусстве в форме красоты... Красота же есть просвечивание идеи через материю... искусство есть осуществление этой видимости идеи, и есть средство, вместе с религией и философией, приводить в сознание и высказывать глубочайшие задачи людей и высшие истины духа.» **(working English)** "According to Hegel, God manifests in nature and art in the form of beauty... Beauty is the shining-through of the idea through matter... art is... a means, together with religion and philosophy, of bringing to consciousness... the highest truths of spirit." (Ch III) Verdict: «не только не более ясно... но еще более... туманно и мистично» — the most fog-bound of all, and the one that "consciously or unconsciously" still rules. | Faithful in content (Tolstoy renders *Schein* and the art–religion–philosophy triad correctly), flattened in treatment: he treats the obscurity as self-evidently disqualifying. The dive author reads this as fair paraphrase + dismissive verdict rather than misrepresentation. |
| **Шопенгауэр / Arthur Schopenhauer** (7) | Consensus: aesthetic contemplation, for Schopenhauer, is the will-less perception of Platonic Ideas — a temporary release from the striving Will and its suffering; the genius sees the Idea most purely; music uniquely copies the Will itself. | «По Шопенгауэру (1788—1860), воля объективируется в мире на различных ступенях... Отрешение от своей индивидуальности и созерцание одной из этих ступеней проявления воли дает нам сознание красоты». **(working English)** "According to Schopenhauer, the will is objectified in the world at various stages... Detachment from one's individuality and contemplation of one of these stages of the will's manifestation gives us consciousness of beauty." (Ch III) Later (Ch XIII), on Wagner: he ties Wagner's music «в связь с мистической теорией Шопенгауэра, столь же нелепой» — "to the mystical theory of Schopenhauer, just as absurd," that music expresses the Will. | **Flattened.** Tolstoy compresses Schopenhauer's release-from-the-Will and the redemptive thrust of aesthetic contemplation into a one-line objectification scheme, and in Ch XIII reduces the music-as-Will doctrine to нелепый (absurd) by association with Wagner. The dive author reads this as dismissing, not engaging, Schopenhauer's strongest claim (music's special status). |
| **Гербарт / J. F. Herbart** | Consensus: Herbart denied any "beauty-in-itself," locating the aesthetic in formal *relations* (Verhältnisse) among elements — a proto-formalist account judged by an "aesthetic elementary judgement." | «По Гербарту (1766—1841), красоты самой в себе нет и не может быть, а есть только наше суждение... И эти основы суждений находятся в отношении впечатлений... искусство состоит в нахождении этих отношений». **(working English)** "According to Herbart, there is and can be no beauty in itself, but only our judgement... and the foundations of these judgements lie in the relation of impressions... art consists in finding these relations." (Ch III) Grouped as an anti-Hegelian who, with Schopenhauer, mocks the Idea-theory. | Faithful — Tolstoy captures Herbart's relational formalism and the rainbow example accurately. The dive author notes this is one of the *less* distorted entries: a passing but correct summary. |
| **Гартман / Eduard von Hartmann** (3) | Consensus: for the "philosopher of the Unconscious," beauty is not in the thing-in-itself nor purely in the mind but in *Schein* (appearance) produced by the artist — an idealist-pessimist synthesis. | «По Гартману (1842), красота лежит не во внешнем мире, не в вещи самой в себе, и тоже не в душе человека, а в кажущемся (Schein), которое производится художником. Вещь сама в себе не красива, но художник превращает ее в красоту.» **(working English)** "According to Hartmann, beauty lies not in the external world, not in the thing-in-itself, nor in the human soul, but in appearance (Schein), produced by the artist. The thing in itself is not beautiful, but the artist transforms it into beauty." (Ch III) Filed among the "less original, less influential" writers. | Faithful but minimizing: an accurate two-sentence gloss, deliberately demoted to a footnote-rank figure. The dive author reads it as correct précis, dismissive ranking. |
| **Гюйо / Jean-Marie Guyau** (6 — "art as the expansion of life") | Consensus: Guyau's *sociological* aesthetics treats art as the expansion and intensification of life — sympathy and social solidarity made sensible; art communicates and widens shared feeling (a clear anticipation of Tolstoy's own communication thesis). | «По Гюйо (1854—1888), красота не есть нечто чуждое самому предмету... а есть самое расцветание того существа, на котором она проявляется. Искусство же есть выражение жизни разумной и сознательной... Искусство поднимает человека из личной жизни в жизнь всеобщую посредством... одинаковых чувств.» **(working English)** "According to Guyau, beauty is not something alien to the object itself... but is the very flowering of the being on which it manifests. Art is the expression of rational and conscious life... Art raises man from personal life into universal life by means of... shared feelings." (Ch III) But Tolstoy also faults him (Ch II): «у Гюйо в «Les problèmes de l'esthétique»... искусством признается искусство костюмерное, вкусовое и осязательное» — Guyau lets cookery, perfume and touch count as art. | **Partly flattened, partly the unacknowledged source.** Tolstoy *admires* Guyau (the "raising from personal to universal life by shared feeling" is essentially his own заражение thesis) yet files him under the discredited beauty-as-pleasure camp because Guyau extends the aesthetic to taste and smell. The dive author reads this as Tolstoy obscuring his nearest predecessor: he caricatures the sensory-extension while quietly absorbing the communication core. |
| **Спенсер / Herbert Spencer** (5) | Consensus: Spencer's "surplus-energy" / play theory (developed from Schiller) holds that art arises from the discharge of energy left over once survival needs are met — an evolutionary-physiological account of the aesthetic impulse. | «По Спенсеру (1820), происхождение искусства есть игра — мысль, высказанная еще Шиллером... в человеке... остается... еще излишек силы. Этот-то излишек и употребляется на игру, переходящую в искусство.» **(working English)** "According to Spencer, the origin of art is play — a thought already voiced by Schiller... in man there remains... a surplus of force. This surplus is used for play, which passes into art." (Ch III) Verdict (Ch V): the физиолого-эволюционное (physiological-evolutionary) definition is неточно because it describes art's *origin*, not its essence. | Faithful as a one-line statement of the play/surplus theory; the critique (it explains genesis not essence) is a substantive objection, not a distortion. The dive author reads this as accurate summary + legitimate philosophical complaint. |
| **Дарвин / Charles Darwin** (4) | Consensus: in *The Descent of Man* Darwin grounds the sense of beauty in sexual selection — ornament, birdsong and mate choice — extending aesthetic feeling to animals and to human ancestors. | «По Ч. Дарвину (1809—1883), «Descent of Man» (1871), красота есть чувство, свойственное не одному человеку, но и животным... Птицы украшают свои гнезда и оценивают красоту в своих парах... Происхождение искусства музыки есть призыв самцами своих самок.» **(working English)** "According to Ch. Darwin, 'Descent of Man' (1871), beauty is a feeling proper not to man alone but to animals too... Birds adorn their nests and appreciate beauty in their mates... The origin of the art of music is the call of males to their females." (Ch III) Bracketed with Schiller/Spencer in the physiological-evolutionary definition (Ch V). | Faithful — an accurate, if telegraphic, summary of the sexual-selection account of beauty and music. The dive author notes the verdict applies to the whole physiological camp, not to Darwin individually. |
| **Гельмгольц / Hermann von Helmholtz** (2) | Consensus: Helmholtz's *On the Sensations of Tone* gives a rigorously physical-acoustic account of consonance, harmony and musical beauty — the empirical-scientific aesthetics par excellence. | «По Гельмгольцу (1821), писавшему о красоте по отношению к музыке, красота достигается в музыкальном произведении неизменно только следованием законам, — законы же эти неизвестны художнику, так что красота проявляется в художнике бессознательно и не может быть подвергнута анализу.» **(working English)** "According to Helmholtz, who wrote on beauty in relation to music, beauty in a musical work is achieved invariably only by following laws — but these laws are unknown to the artist, so that beauty manifests in the artist unconsciously and cannot be subjected to analysis." (Ch III) | **Flattened.** Tolstoy's gloss ("laws unknown to the artist... cannot be analysed") inverts Helmholtz, whose whole enterprise was that musical beauty *can* be reduced to analysable acoustic law. The dive author reads this as a misrepresentation that turns the arch-empiricist into a mystic of the unconscious. |
| **Верон / Eugène Véron** (3) | Consensus: Véron's *L'esthétique* (1878) is anti-metaphysical — art is the *expression of emotion* through line, form, colour, sound, rhythm; he expressly jettisons "absolute beauty." | Tolstoy singles him out as the rare clear-headed exception: «исключение, по своей ясности и разумности, представляет книга Véron «L’esthétique» (1878)... устраняющая из эстетики туманное понятие абсолютной красоты». Then: «По Верону (1825—1889), искусство есть проявление чувства (émotion), передающегося во вне сочетанием линий, форм, красок или последовательностью жестов, звуков или слов, подчиненных известным ритмам.» **(working English)** "According to Véron, art is the manifestation of feeling (émotion) transmitted outward by a combination of lines, forms, colours, or a sequence of gestures, sounds or words subordinated to definite rhythms." (Ch III) Verdict (Ch V): still неточно — a man can express emotion without affecting others, and then it is not art. | Faithful and unusually sympathetic: Véron's expression-theory is reported accurately and praised for dropping absolute beauty. The dive author reads this as the *least* flattened entry — Tolstoy builds his own заражение criterion partly by patching Véron's gap (transmission, not mere expression). |
| **Кузен / Victor Cousin** | Consensus: Cousin, the French eclectic, popularized German Idealism in France and is associated with the "l'art pour l'art" lineage; he held beauty to be definable in itself, grounded in unity-in-variety and a moral basis. | «Cousin (1792—1867) — эклектик и последователь немецких идеалистов. По его теории, красота всегда имеет основу нравственную. Cousin опровергает положение о том, что искусство есть подражание и что прекрасно то, что нравится. Он утверждает, что красота может быть определена сама в себе и что сущность ее состоит в разнообразии и в единстве.» **(working English)** "Cousin — an eclectic and follower of the German idealists. By his theory, beauty always has a moral basis. Cousin refutes the proposition that art is imitation and that the beautiful is what pleases. He asserts that beauty can be defined in itself and that its essence consists in variety and unity." (Ch III) Filed in the objective-mystical camp (Ch IV). | Faithful as a brief doxographic entry; Tolstoy reports Cousin's positions without real engagement (it is essentially a list item). The dive author reads it as accurate but cursory — no caricature, no analysis. |
| **Тэн / Hippolyte Taine** (3) | Consensus: Taine's positivist aesthetics (*Philosophie de l'art*) explains art by race-milieu-moment and defines beauty as the marked manifestation of an essential character of a significant idea. | «По Тэну (1828—1893), красота есть проявление существенного характера какой-либо значительной идеи, более совершенное, чем то, в каком она выражается в действительности.» **(working English)** "According to Taine, beauty is the manifestation of the essential character of some significant idea, more perfect than that in which it is expressed in reality." (Ch III) Grouped with the French writers (with Guyau, Véron) who recently shaped views of art. | Faithful — an accurate one-sentence reduction of Taine's "caractère essentiel" formula. The dive author notes the famous race-milieu-moment apparatus is omitted, but the definition reported is not distorted. |
| **Ницше / Friedrich Nietzsche** (4) | Consensus: Nietzsche's aesthetics (Apollonian/Dionysian, art as the supreme justification of existence, the artist-genius) and his *Übermensch* are a critique of herd morality — complex and contested, not a simple apology for elite pleasure. | Tolstoy never summarizes Nietzsche's aesthetics; he is invoked only as patron saint of art-for-the-elite. «...могут быть только «schöne Geister», избранные... или «сверх-человеки», как называют это последователи Ницше; остальные же, грубое стадо... должно служить высоким наслаждениям этой высшей породы людей.» **(working English)** "...can only be 'schöne Geister', the elect... or 'supermen', as Nietzsche's followers call them; the rest, the coarse herd... must serve the lofty pleasures of this higher breed of people." (Ch VIII) Later named (Ch XVII) as the «пророк» of the immoralist decadents, beside Oscar Wilde. | **Flattened to a label.** Tolstoy gives Nietzsche *no* aesthetic summary at all — only «последователи Ницше» as a slogan for caste-aesthetics and immoralism. The dive author reads this as the clearest case of caricature-by-association: Nietzsche functions as a bogeyman, not as a thinker engaged. (Row included per brief; the verdict is "no real summary — a passing, polemical mention.") |
| **Вагнер / Richard Wagner** (33 — the bad-art exhibit) | Consensus: Wagner's *Gesamtkunstwerk* fuses music, poetry and drama into a total artwork; the *Ring* and the leitmotif technique are landmark achievements, however contested his theory and persona. | Tolstoy makes Wagner the museum specimen of counterfeit art. «Вагнер с большим умением воспользовался всеми... средствами подделки под искусство и составил образцовое поддельное произведение искусства... ни в одном из всех известных мне подделок под искусство не соединены с таким мастерством... все приемы, посредством которых подделывается искусство, а именно: заимствования, подражательность, эффектность и занимательность.» **(working English)** "Wagner skilfully used all... the devices of counterfeiting art and composed an exemplary counterfeit work of art... in none of all the counterfeits of art known to me are all the devices by which art is counterfeited combined with such mastery — namely: borrowing, imitativeness, strikingness and entertainingness." (Ch XIII) On the *Ring*: «образец самой грубой, доходящей даже до смешного, подделки под поэзию» — "a model of the crudest, even ludicrous, counterfeit of poetry." | **Flattened / hostile.** Tolstoy attended one act in Moscow and read the libretti, then convicts the whole *Gesamtkunstwerk* as гипнотизация (hypnotism), not art. The dive author reads this as the treatise's most polemical caricature — a verdict driven by Tolstoy's communication-criterion (Wagner doesn't "infect," he stuns the nerves) rather than by sustained engagement with the music. |
| **Бодлер / Charles Baudelaire** (14 — the Decadent exhibit) | Consensus: *Les Fleurs du mal* is a foundational work of modern poetry — Baudelaire's "artificial" beauty, the dandy, *correspondances*, and the aesthetic of the modern city reshaped European verse. | The chief exhibit of decadent bad art. «Миросозерцание одного, Бодлера, состоит в возведении в теорию грубого эгоизма и замене нравственности неопределенным, как облака, понятием красоты, и красоты непременно искусственной. Бодлер предпочитал раскрашенное женское лицо натуральному, и металлические деревья и минеральное подобие воды — натуральным.» **(working English)** "The worldview of the one, Baudelaire, consists in elevating crude egoism into a theory and replacing morality with a notion of beauty as vague as clouds, and beauty necessarily artificial. Baudelaire preferred a painted woman's face to a natural one, and metal trees and a mineral semblance of water to natural ones." (Ch X) Quoting Gautier's preface approvingly to convict him: Baudelaire banished «l’éloquence, la passion et la vérité». | **Flattened / hostile.** Tolstoy reads the artifice-over-nature creed as moral degeneracy and dismisses these poets as «очень неискусных по форме и весьма низких и пошлых по содержанию». The dive author reads this as a category-verdict: Baudelaire's deliberate aesthetic of the artificial is taken as proof of incommunicable, self-regarding non-art rather than examined as a poetics. |
| **CONCEPT — красота / beauty** | Consensus (post-Kant): beauty is a distinctive value of aesthetic judgement, not reducible to either metaphysical perfection or mere private liking. | Tolstoy's demolition: beauty is *only* what pleases. «Красота же, если мы не довольствуемся словами, а говорим о том, что понимаем, — красота есть не что иное, как то, что нам нравится.» **(working English)** "Beauty, if we are not satisfied with words but speak of what we understand — beauty is nothing other than what pleases us." (Ch VI) And: «Понятие красоты не только не совпадает с добром, но скорее противоположно ему... Чем больше мы отдаемся красоте, тем больше мы удаляемся от добра.» **(working English)** "The concept of beauty not only does not coincide with the good, but is rather opposed to it... The more we give ourselves to beauty, the further we move from the good." (Ch VI) | This is the dive's pivot: Tolstoy *defines* beauty downward to наслаждение precisely so he can reject it as art's criterion. The dive author reads the move as rhetorically decisive but philosophically question-begging — every richer account (Kant's disinterest, Hegel's Idea) is pre-collapsed into "liking" before being dismissed. |
| **CONCEPT — заражение / "infection" (Tolstoy's own positive criterion)** | Consensus: this is Tolstoy's original contribution — the *communication/expression* theory of art, the seed of later expression theories (Collingwood) and a foil for the institutional and formalist theories that followed. | The positive definition that replaces beauty. «Искусство есть деятельность человеческая, состоящая в том, что один человек сознательно известными внешними знаками передает другим испытываемые им чувства, а другие люди заражаются этими чувствами и переживают их.» **(working English)** "Art is a human activity consisting in this, that one man consciously, by certain external signs, conveys to others the feelings he has experienced, and other men are infected by these feelings and also experience them." (Ch V) The criterion of real vs. counterfeit art: «Признак, выделяющий настоящее искусство от поддельного, есть один несомненный — заразительность искусства.» **(working English)** "The one indubitable sign distinguishing real art from counterfeit is the infectiousness of art." (Ch XV) | Tolstoy's own, not a rendering of anyone else. The dive author reads it as the criterion against which all the catalogued thinkers are silently measured: Véron and Guyau approach it (expression / shared feeling), the empiricists and metaphysicians are convicted by it. Its weakness — заразительность is itself subjective — mirrors the "liking" he condemns in others. |
| **CONCEPT — религиозное сознание / religious consciousness (the good-art criterion)** | Consensus: Tolstoy's second-tier test — *good* art must transmit feelings flowing from the highest religious (for his era, Christian-brotherhood) understanding of an age; widely read as moralizing the communication theory. | Distinct from the bare infection test: it judges the *worth* of what is infected. «Всегда, во всякое время и во всяком человеческом обществе есть общее всем людям этого общества религиозное сознание того, что хорошо и что дурно, и это-то религиозное сознание и определяет достоинство чувств, передаваемых искусством.» **(working English)** "Always, in every age and in every human society, there is a religious consciousness common to all the people of that society, of what is good and what is bad, and it is this religious consciousness that determines the worth of the feelings conveyed by art." (Ch VI) | Tolstoy's own normative criterion. The dive author reads it as the lever that lets him rank Beethoven's late period, the *Ring* and *Fleurs du mal* as bad even where infectious: infectiousness makes something *art*, religious consciousness makes it *good* art. |
| **CONCEPT — искренность / sincerity** | Consensus: in Tolstoy's scheme sincerity is the third and decisive condition of infectiousness — the artist must feel what he conveys; a near-Romantic authenticity criterion. | The strongest of the three conditions of infection. «Более же всего увеличивается степень заразительности искусства степенью искренности художника. Как только зритель, слушатель, читатель чувствует, что художник сам заражается своим произведением и пишет, поет, играет для себя, а не только для того, чтобы воздействовать на других, такое душевное состояние художника заражает воспринимающего…» **(working English)** "Most of all the degree of infectiousness of art is increased by the degree of the artist's sincerity. As soon as the viewer, listener, reader feels that the artist himself is infected by his own work and writes, sings, plays for himself, and not merely to act upon others, such a state of soul of the artist infects the receiver." (Ch XV) | Tolstoy's own. The dive author reads sincerity as the criterion that does the real polemical work against Wagner and the Decadents: they are convicted not of incompetence but of writing «для воспринимающего» / for effect — calculation in place of felt experience. |
| **CONCEPT — подделки / counterfeits of art** | Consensus: Tolstoy's taxonomy of pseudo-art — works that simulate art's effect without the genuine transmission of felt emotion; the negative counterpart of the infection criterion. | The four devices of counterfeit. «во всем том, что среди нас называется искусством высших классов... едва ли одно из сотни тысяч произошло от испытанного его автором чувства; всё же остальное — только фабричные произведения, подделки под искусство, в которых заимствования, подражательность, эффектность и занимательность заменяют заражение чувством.» **(working English)** "in all that among us is called the art of the upper classes... scarcely one in a hundred thousand arose from a feeling experienced by its author; all the rest are only factory products, counterfeits of art, in which borrowing, imitativeness, strikingness and entertainingness replace infection by feeling." (Ch XIV) The four приёмы: заимствования, подражательность, эффектность, занимательность. | Tolstoy's own framework. The dive author reads the four-device taxonomy as the demolition tool applied to the whole canon (Ch XVI lists Dante, Shakespeare, Raphael, Beethoven, Bach among the convicted) — the catalogue of Western aesthetics in Chs III–V exists precisely to clear the ground for this criterion. |

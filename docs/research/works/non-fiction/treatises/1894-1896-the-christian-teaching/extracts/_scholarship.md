## Received view & the neglect question

**Headline finding: the neglect hypothesis holds, and strongly.** Across the Anglophone scholarship the late religious corpus is represented, almost always, by *The Kingdom of God Is Within You* (1893) and — secondarily — *What Is Religion?* (1902). *The Christian Teaching* is rarely named as a work in its own right; where it surfaces, it is **mined for a quotation, not discussed as a text.** This is the clearest single result of the pass.

- **The diagnostic case is Elena Stepanova's 2020 article** in *State, Religion and Church* — a study devoted precisely to *Tolstoy's faith*. It cites *The Christian Teaching* exactly **once**, in passing, as one source among many (the «Moses, Isaiah, Confucius … Pascal, Spinoza» universalism passage, PSS 39:119), with **no independent analytical treatment** of the work. If a dedicated study of Tolstoy's faith uses *The Christian Teaching* only as a quote-quarry, the neglect is structural, not accidental. (Stepanova 2020.)

- **Aylmer Maude** — the contemporary who knew the work and its circumstances best — gives the standard one-line filing that scholarship has largely inherited: it was a work "written later, put on one side, and published in 1898 without final revision." The framing that has stuck is exactly the dive's worry made into a verdict: *minor, abstract, unfinished restatement.* (Maude, *The Life of Tolstoy: Later Years*, 1910; the 1902 "Tolstoy's Teaching" essay; both via Internet Archive / eNotes.)

- **Why the neglect.** Three reinforcing causes, each documented in the corpus itself rather than asserted by scholars: (1) **Tolstoy disowned it as unfinished** — the 2 Sept 1897 permission-letter to Chertkov calls it «писание неконченным» ("an unfinished writing", commentary l. 47); the editor's preface told readers the same. A work the author publicly withheld endorsement from does not invite close reading. (2) **It restates rather than breaks news** — by 1898 the doctrine was already public and notorious in *Kingdom of God*; *The Christian Teaching* offered the same жизнепонимание in calmer, more systematic form, so reviewers had nothing sensational to react to. (3) **Tolstoy's own clarity test failed it** — his 19 July 1896 diary admits the book is «совсем недоступно простолюдину и ребенку» ("quite inaccessible to the common man and the child", `d1896_07_19.txt`); the recurring private fear was «опасность отвлеченного умствования» ("the danger of abstract reasoning", keystone letter to Popov 22 Oct 1894). Abstraction is precisely what makes a work easy for later scholars to skip.

- **The corpus's own counter-weight.** Against the "minor restatement" filing, the primary record shows Tolstoy **ranked it co-equal with his unfinished novels**: the 12 March 1895 diary heads his entire late-period to-do list with the catechism, set above *Father Sergius*, *The Forged Coupon*, *Hadji Murat* and the rest (`d1895_03_12.txt`). The neglect is the *reception's* judgment, not the author's.

**On the standing Sofia-centred / "moralistic dogmatist" frame.** It is not load-bearing for this work specifically (the marriage-strain literature attaches to the gospel-translation years and to *The Kreutzer Sonata*), but the adjacent temptation — reading the late religious treatises as the dry, doctrinaire output of a man who had "stopped being an artist" (the "two Tolstoys" split) — is exactly what makes *The Christian Teaching* easy to dismiss. The dive should attribute that framing, not adopt it; Gustafson and Medzhibovskaya are the standard counter-authorities for treating the religious work as central rather than aberrant (per `_sibling-dives.md` §3).

## Reception (Russia / Church / press) — with honest thinness verdict

**Verdict: reception is genuinely thin, and the thinness is itself the finding.** Repeated targeted searches for any contemporary review or critical notice of the 1898 editions — English or Russian — returned only the work's *content*, modern reprints, and digital-archive listings; no contemporary review surfaced. Set beside *The Kingdom of God Is Within You* (smuggled, debated, "overwhelming" to Gandhi in 1894) and *What Is Religion?* (caught in the 1901–02 excommunication storm), *The Christian Teaching* lands quietly. This matches the hypothesis exactly: Tolstoy disowned it as unfinished, it was abstract, and it broke no new doctrinal ground, so it gave the press and the Church little to seize on.

- **Russia.** Like all the late religious works it was **banned for legal Russian publication**; it circulated abroad (Purleigh, Essex, 1898) and in copy. It reached print legally inside Russia only with the post-1905 thaw — appearing in the 12th edition of the *Сочинения* (part 14, Moscow 1911) and in the Biryukov-edited *Собрание сочинений* (vol. XV, Moscow 1913) (commentary l. 51). No contemporary Russian critical reception of note surfaced; the corpus commentary records the *publishing* history in detail but no *review* history — consistent with a quiet landing.

- **The Church / the 1901 excommunication.** The Holy Synod's February 1901 decree was a sweeping condemnation of Tolstoy's "literary activity" and his rejection of Orthodox dogma, sacraments, and the person of Christ — but the surviving decree text **does not enumerate specific works** (the Wikisource transcription marks the "list of offences" as missing, and no clean catalogue of named titles is recoverable). *Resurrection* (1899) is usually identified as the proximate trigger; *The Kingdom of God Is Within You* is the standing background indictment. **There is no evidence that *The Christian Teaching* was singled out** in the excommunication rhetoric. So far as can be sourced, it was **not** caught up in the 1901 storm — again consistent with thin reception. *(The non-naming of any work in the decree is itself uncertain at the margins, since the best online transcription is incomplete; flagged.)*

- **English press.** No contemporary English review located. The Tolstoyan publishing network (Brotherhood Publishing Co., then Free Age Press from 1900) distributed it as one title in a flood of cheap Tolstoy editions; its reach was wide but undifferentiated — it was *available*, not *discussed*.

**One honest caveat on "thin."** Absence of evidence in open web sources is not proof of absence in 1898 periodical archives (which are poorly digitised). The verdict "thin" is well-supported relative to its siblings and consistent with every causal factor in the record; it should be stated plainly as the finding, with the digitisation caveat noted.

## Factual resolutions (publisher / English translation / Chertkov's editing)

**(a) Publisher of the 1898 English edition — RESOLVED. It is the Brotherhood Publishing Company, London, NOT Free Age Press.**

- The PSS Tom 39 apparatus (Mishin) states it verbatim: *«The Christian teaching», Brotherhood Publishing C°, ed. by V. Tchertkoff, London, 1898* (reprinted New York the same year); the Russian edition *«Христианское учение», V. Tchertkoff, Purleigh, Essex, England, 1898* (commentary l. 51).
- This is corroborated externally and the Free Age Press attribution is **ruled out on dates**: the Free Age Press was founded **summer 1900** at Tuckton House, Christchurch (Tolstoyans publishing-enterprises history; multiple dealer records). The Brotherhood Publishing Company, by contrast, was active **from 1895** (Croydon Brotherhood Church, Kenworthy's circle) and published Tolstoy's other major 1898 titles — notably *What Is Art?* — in the same year and house style (Biblio / AbeBooks / Raptis dealer records). The dive prompt's "Free Age Press 1898" is therefore a slip; **Brotherhood Publishing Co. (London) 1898** is correct, with a New York reprint the same year.
- *Uncertain detail:* the exact **New York reprint imprint** is not cleanly resolvable from open sources — one library catalog record loosely associates the NY reprint with "Stokes," but this is unconfirmed and should not be stated as fact. The PSS only says "reprinted in New York the same year." Flag as uncertain.

**(b) English translation lineage — RESOLVED in essentials.** A published English translation exists from first publication: **Chertkov's own circle produced it.** Chertkov wrote to Tolstoy on 12 May 1897 that he was **translating *The Christian Teaching* into English himself** from his copy of рук. №113, and sought permission to publish in both English and Russian with an editor's preface (commentary l. 45). The 1898 Brotherhood Publishing English edition is that translation — the **standard early English text**, ed. V. Tchertkoff. It is freely available (Internet Archive scan `christianteachin00tols`; full text at marxists.org and the Anarchist Library, the latter via a modern 2020 re-translation by EarthlyFireFlies for online readers — a *secondary* online rendering, not the historic text). *Uncertain detail:* whether **Aylmer Maude** later produced or revised a translation of *this* work for his Oxford "World's Classics" Tolstoy is **not confirmed** — Maude's religious-works volumes centre on the better-known treatises, and no Maude translation of *The Christian Teaching* specifically was located. The dive should name the **1898 Brotherhood Publishing / Tchertkoff English text** as the standard published English; treat a Maude version as **uncertain / probably absent**.

**(c) Chertkov's editorial impositions on the 1898 text — RESOLVED and well-documented.** The corpus commentary spells it out (commentary l. 51): Chertkov set the type from a copy of рук. №113 "but with some departures and changes"; passages Tolstoy had corrected but that were illegible in copying were printed from the first redaction; and — the two structural impositions —
  - he **split the work into eight titled parts**: I «Древние вероучения и новое жизнепонимание»; II «О грехах»; III «О соблазнах»; IV «Обманы веры и освобождение от них»; V «Освобождение от соблазнов»; VI «Борьба с грехами»; VII «О молитве»; VIII «Заключение»; and
  - he introduced **continuous paragraph numbering across all chapters (1–404)**, "as Chertkov explained to Tolstoy, *для удобства читателя* ('for the reader's convenience')."
  - By contrast, **the PSS (Tom 39) prints the work from manuscript №113** in its own chapter form (commentary l. 99), not Chertkov's 8-part arrangement. The marxists.org and Anarchist Library online texts reproduce **Chertkov's part-division** (their tables of contents show the same titled parts — "Ancient teachings and Christian understanding of life," "Sins," "Temptations," "Religious deception…," "Avoiding Temptations," "Battling sins," etc.), confirming that the *popular* English text is the Chertkov shape, while the *scholarly* Russian text is the manuscript shape. This editorial divergence is real and worth flagging in the dive; scholarship rarely notes it.
  - *Note on the "64-chapter" figure:* the dive prompt cites a 64-chapter manuscript form. The PSS apparatus confirms рук. №113 is the source and that Chertkov re-divided it into 8 parts with 1–404 numbering, but the commentary text read here does **not** state the chapter count "64" explicitly — treat the specific "64" as **from the dive's own manuscript reading / uncertain against this commentary** until checked against the rendered PSS text or the рук. №113 description.

## 1. The Kingdom of God Is Within You — THE key sibling

- **Slug (folder):** `1890-1893-the-kingdom-of-god-is-within-you`
- **Title (H1):** "The Kingdom of God Is Within You (Царство Божие внутри вас)"
- **Layer / date:** `layer: reference`, lastUpdated 2026-06-06; dive dated 2026-06-06. A **work dive**.

### Central claim / Key findings (headline points)
- **The book that began as a preface.** Started July 1890 as a short preface to Adin Ballou's *Catechism of Non-Resistance* + W. L. Garrison's 1838 *Declaration of Sentiments*; it "would not stop growing" into a 293-page synthesis. Diary, 8 July 1890: «Написал предисловие к катехизису Балу.» ("Wrote a preface to Ballou's catechism.")
- **The subtitle is the thesis.** «…или Христианство не как мистическое учение, а как новое жизнепонимание» — Christianity *not as a mystical teaching but as a new understanding of life* (жизнепонимание). The whole claim: Christianity is a practical understanding of life, not metaphysics/private mysticism.
- **The argument is a single chain.** Non-resistance to evil by force is "the chief departure" the churches made from Christ; the state rests on violence; its army faces inward ("to defend the government from its own oppressed and enslaved subjects"); held in place by four means (intimidation, bribery, hypnotization, soldiery); the only exit is each person's refusal to lie — "to know the truth and to profess it."
- **Publication abroad & Gandhi the afterlife.** First in French (*Le salut est en vous*, Paris, Oct 1893), then Russian/German Jan 1894; legally printed in Russia only 1906. Gandhi read it in 1894 and was "overwhelmed."

### Explicit mention of *The Christian Teaching* / «катехизис» / «изложение веры»
- **The work's own discarded title chain contains an early "Christian Teaching" title.** §4.2 (Genesis): the manuscript title changed four times — "from *On Non-Resistance to Evil, on the Church, and on Universal Military Conscription* … through **_The Christian Teaching not as a Mystical Teaching but as a New Understanding of Life_** (where the Luke 17:21 epigraph «Царствие божие внутрь вас есть» first appears) and *Christ's Teaching and Universal Military Conscription*, to the final *The Kingdom of God Is Within You*." — **Note this is a working TITLE of *Kingdom of God*, NOT the separate 1894–96 treatise.** The phrase "Christian Teaching" thus literally passed through *Kingdom of God*'s drafts before becoming the title of the later catechism-form treatise. Worth flagging to avoid conflation.
- **«катехизис» (catechism):** appears repeatedly but ONLY referring to **Ballou's** *Catechism of Non-Resistance* (the American source text), e.g. diary 8 July 1890 «предисловие к катехизису Балу», and §4.1 "Ballou's *Catechism of Non-Resistance*, which N. N. Strakhov translated." Not Tolstoy's own catechism.
- **No explicit mention of the 1894–96 *Христианское учение* as a separate work.** The treatise *The Christian Teaching* is not named or cross-linked anywhere in this dive.

### Cross-link framing for "Place in the cluster"
*The Kingdom of God Is Within You* (1890–93) is the **great polemical statement of the same doctrine** — the total, combative indictment of church and state — written immediately before *The Christian Teaching*. Where *Kingdom of God* argues the doctrine *against* its opponents (answering critics of *What I Believe*, demolishing the state, in a tone Tolstoy refused to soften), *The Christian Teaching* restates the same жизнепонимание calmly and positively, in catechism/exposition form, as a constructive statement of the faith rather than a polemic. The two share the doctrinal core; they differ in mode (polemic vs. calm exposition). The "Christian Teaching" title even briefly lived inside *Kingdom of God*'s own draft chain.

### Contested label(s) it interrogates and how
- **"Christian anarchism" / "anarchist":** deliberately **deferred** to the companion doctrine dive — "the contested labels it attracts ('Christian anarchism', 'anarchist', and the 'mysticism' its own subtitle rejects) are interrogated there, not adopted here." Labels kept "attributed to the outside."
- **"Mysticism":** actively rejected by the work's own subtitle ("not as a mystical teaching") — used as "a standing rebuke to readers who file Tolstoy's late religion under mysticism."

## 2. What Is Religion? — the later compression

- **Slug (folder):** `1901-1902-what-is-religion`
- **Title (H1):** "What Is Religion? (Что такое религия и в чём сущность её?) — a corpus work-dive"
- **Layer / date:** `layer: reference`, lastUpdated 2026-06-08. A single-work **work-dive**.

### Central claim / Key findings (headline points)
- **Religion defined as a relation to the infinite.** "True religion is such an establishment by man, in accord with his reason and knowledge, of his relation to the infinite life surrounding him, which binds his life to that infinity and guides his actions." First articulated 8 Feb 1901, ~two weeks before the Synod excommunication.
- **Two movements: structural + positive.** Chs I–IX trace three universal corruption mechanisms (priesthood, miracles, scripture) + the Christian addition (institutional infallibility — the "Church truc"); Chs X–XVII identify the four propositions shared by all authentic religions (God as source; a divine particle in man; subdue passion / increase love; the Golden Rule).
- **Equality is the structural constant.** "The recognition of the equality of people is the inevitable, fundamental property of every religion."
- **Composed under mortal urgency** at Gaspra during grave illness; «Надо торопиться» ("I must hurry"). Banned in Russia; published by Chertkov's Free Word press (1902); first legal Russian publication 1906.

### Explicit mention of *The Christian Teaching* / «катехизис» / «изложение веры»
- **No explicit mention of *The Christian Teaching* (Христианское учение) as a work.** It is not named or cross-linked.
- **«катехизис» (catechism):** appears once, in Ch VII (Faith redefined): Tolstoy defines faith "against the Hebrews epistle … and **the catechism**" — i.e. against the Orthodox catechism's definition of faith as credence in propositions. This is the genre/foil sense of "catechism," not Tolstoy's own work. (Useful: shows how a sibling dive handles the word "catechism" as the institutional foil — the new dive's own catechism-form treatise can be read as Tolstoy's counter-catechism.)
- **The four propositions of universal religion (Ch XIV)** are the compressed, comparative-theology version of the positive creed *The Christian Teaching* sets out at length — relevant content overlap even though the work is not named.

### Cross-link framing for "Place in the cluster"
*What Is Religion?* (1901–02) is the **later, comparative compression** of the same positive doctrine — the most systematic, world-religions-wide statement of the relation-to-the-infinite creed. Where *The Christian Teaching* (1894–96) lays out the recovered Christian faith in full catechism/exposition form, *What Is Religion?* distils it five years later into four propositions Tolstoy finds in all seven major religions. It is the downstream synthesis; *The Christian Teaching* is the fuller, earlier positive statement of the same recovered faith. (The dive's own cluster list already chains: Confession → What I Believe → Examination of Dogmatic Theology → Kingdom of God → What Is Art? → Slavery of Our Times — *The Christian Teaching* slots between Kingdom of God and What Is Religion as the calm positive creed.)

### Contested label(s) it interrogates and how
- **"Excommunication as motive" framing — complicated.** The received view (Bartlett, Maude) reads the essay as a response to the 1901 excommunication; the dive shows the definitional kernel formed *before* the decree — "The excommunication sharpened urgency; it did not determine the argument."
- **"Most conclusive religious statement" (Simmons) — flagged as untested.** Treated as an external attribution, not a finding; says it would need cross-dive comparison with *A Confession*, *What I Believe*, *Kingdom of God* (an opening the new *Christian Teaching* dive can take up).
- Movement-labels ("Christian anarchism", "Christian communism/socialism") cross-linked out to the theme dives rather than adopted.

## 3. Gospel translation — the textual base

- **Slug (folder):** `gospel-translation`
- **Title (frontmatter `title:`):** "Tolstoy and the Gospels: the translation project, its history, and the strain at home" (note: this dive carries its title in YAML frontmatter; first body line is an un-headed lede paragraph, not an H1 with the title).
- **Layer / date:** `layer: reference`, lastUpdated 2026-06-06. A corpus-dive (history + family + English lineage).

### Central claim / Key findings (headline points)
- **The harmony is the keystone of a four-part project, not a standalone book.** From the *Gospel in Brief* preface: (1) *A Confession*, (2) *Critique of Dogmatic Theology*, (3) the translation+harmony of the four Gospels, (4) *What I Believe* — one connected enterprise of 1879–84.
- **Product of the conversion, not its cause** ("his views gave rise to his gospels").
- **Method philological + anti-ecclesiastical:** read the Koine Greek with lexicons, harmonized by *meaning* not chronology, structured into 12 chapters he found mirrored in the Lord's Prayer.
- **He later judged his own philology too forced** (1902 Free Word preface: «искусственные и, вероятно, неправильные филологические разъяснения»).
- **The work strained the marriage** (S. A. Tolstaya's diaries the primary witness); banned in Russia, first appeared Geneva 1892–94, legally in Russia only 1957 (Jubilee Tom 24). First English: anonymous, incomplete, personally authorized (Tolstoy's autograph imprimatur); only complete English = Leo Wiener 1904.

### Explicit mention of *The Christian Teaching* / «катехизис» / «изложение веры»
- **No explicit mention of *The Christian Teaching* (Христианское учение).** Not named or cross-linked.
- **«катехизис» (catechism):** appears once, in the *Critique of Dogmatic Theology* quotation (§2): «…никогда я не испытывал того несомненного убеждения в полном безверии человека, как то, которое я испытывал относительно **составителей катехизисов** и богословии» — "the composers of **catechisms** and theologies." Again the institutional/Orthodox sense, the foil.
- **«изложение веры»:** not present as such, but the parallel formation «**изложение** … учения» recurs as the project's genre vocabulary — *Краткое изложение Евангелия* (*The Gospel in Brief*, lit. "brief exposition of the Gospel"). The dive's "four-part project" framing (translate the text → expound the recovered teaching in *What I Believe*) is exactly the textual-and-expository pipeline *The Christian Teaching* later continues.

### Cross-link framing for "Place in the cluster"
The gospel-translation/harmony project (1879–84) is the **textual base** beneath *The Christian Teaching*. Having re-translated and harmonized the Gospels by meaning and expounded the recovered teaching in *What I Believe*, Tolstoy had the reconstructed creed in hand; *The Christian Teaching* (1894–96) is a later, free-standing positive exposition of that same recovered Christian doctrine — no longer tied to the Gospel text verse-by-verse, but resting on the understanding the harmony produced. It is the catechism-form descendant of the *Gospel in Brief* expository line.

### Contested label(s) it interrogates and how
- **The "domestic tragedy" / Sofia-as-victim, Chertkov-as-villain frame (*The Last Station*, Popoff, Shirer) — complicated.** The primary record (S. A. Tolstaya's own diaries) is "more ambivalent" — she names the cause without flinching yet calls the work "God's will."
- **"Tolstoyan"** — noted as a label "Tolstoy himself rejected as 'a great and gross error'; it is attributed here, not endorsed."
- **"Two Tolstoys" split** — resisted (citing Gustafson, Medzhibovskaya): the gospel work is central, not an aberration.

## 4. Christian anarchism — the contested label

- **Slug (folder):** `christian-anarchism`
- **Title (H1):** "Tolstoy and \"Christian anarchism\""
- **Layer / date:** `layer: reference`, lastUpdated 2026-05-30; dive dated 2026-05-30. A narrow **theme/label dive** (dive #2 of the three that split the legacy `tolstoyanism-christian-anarchism` survey).

### Central claim / Key findings (headline points)
- **The spine is a double move:** Tolstoy refuses *anarchist* as a **political** label and affirms the substance as **religious** (Christ's teaching). "What Eltzbacher files under political science he files under the Gospel."
- **Eltzbacher (1900) — the rejection:** «я не анархист в смысле политического реформатора» ("I am not an anarchist in the sense of a political reformer"); turns Eltzbacher's own index (no page-references under "violence" for Tolstoy) into proof that his teaching is religious, not political.
- **Sacy (1901) — the unique self-attestation:** the *only* place in all 90 volumes where Tolstoy uses «христианский анархизм» / «l'anarchisme chrétien» in his own voice — and even there third-person ("Babism … has much in common with Christian anarchism").
- **The phrase was not his coinage** — a term of art circulating among correspondents (Schmitt, Kenworthy, Davidson, Crosby, Ortt) through the 1890s; given Russian programmatic form by Chertkov's 1905 booklet *O khristianskom anarkhizme*.

### Explicit mention of *The Christian Teaching* / «катехизис» / «изложение веры»
- **No explicit mention of *The Christian Teaching* (Христианское учение).** Not named or cross-linked. (The cluster of polemical/anti-state works listed in §4 names *Kingdom of God*, *What Then Must We Do?*, *The Slavery of Our Time*, *What Is Religion…*, *On the Meaning of the Russian Revolution* — but not *The Christian Teaching*.)
- **«катехизис»:** not present in this dive.

### Cross-link framing for "Place in the cluster"
This dive owns the **contested "Christian anarchism" label** — the doctrine, the anti-state argument, and Tolstoy's handling of the *anarchist* tag across the corpus. For *The Christian Teaching*, it is the place to send the reader who wants the label debate rather than restate it: *The Christian Teaching* sets out the positive religious doctrine in its own (calm, expository) terms, while the question of whether that doctrine amounts to "Christian anarchism" — a label Tolstoy refused as political and used only once, third-person — is adjudicated here. Cross-link, do not re-argue.

### Contested label(s) it interrogates and how
- **"Anarchist" (political):** Tolstoy refuses it as a category error and redirects it, every time, to the Gospel.
- **"Christian anarchism":** the dive's whole subject — shown to be an external, movement-born label Tolstoy held "at arm's length," used himself only once and third-person; the corpus *complicates* the standard "he avoided it because of violence associations" explanation (the refusal is more precise — religious, not political) and *extends* the literature by supplying the single datable self-use.
- Explicitly does **not** adjudicate whether the labels are accurate — "It documents what his own voice does with the terms."

## 5. Voice & conventions (house style to match)

**Frontmatter.** YAML at top: `layer: reference`, `lastUpdated: YYYY-MM-DD`, `tags: [research]` (newer dives add richer tags, e.g. `[research, corpus-dive, work-dive, religion, prophet-period, …]`). Title is usually the first H1 (`# Title (Russian title in parens)`); the gospel-translation dive instead carries `title:` in frontmatter and opens with an un-headed lede.

**Section headings.** Two patterns coexist:
- *Numbered* (Kingdom of God, christian-anarchism): `## 1. Key findings`, `## 2. Why this matters`, `## 3. The shape of the question`, … `## 14. Method`, `## 15. References`.
- *Unnumbered* (What Is Religion, gospel-translation): `## Key findings`, `## Why this matters`, `## What the work says`, `## Place in the cluster`, `## Method`, `## References`.
Both share the same backbone: **Key findings → Why this matters → (the shape of the question / what the work argues) → Genesis & composition → Textual history → Publication/censorship/translation → Reception & afterlife → Place in the cluster (or "Where the theme clusters") → Scholarly context → Material not covered → Visual & manuscript record → Method → References**, plus a closing "Author's later verdict" in the work-dives.

**"Key findings" reads as** a bulleted list of ~7–8 items, each a **bold lead clause** ("**The subtitle is the thesis.**") followed by 1–3 sentences, frequently embedding a short verbatim Russian quote with a working-English gloss inline. Tightly factual, minimal editorial. The last bullet is often "**For the works record.**" / "**For the vault.**" noting provenanced corrections and that "Ingestion is a separate, human step."

**Quotes: RU + working-EN.** Every primary quotation is given verbatim in Russian first, then a separate working-English line **explicitly labelled `(working English)`**. Two common layouts:
- Blockquote pairs: `> Russian` then `> *English* (working English)` (Kingdom of God, christian-anarchism).
- Italic-RU blockquote then a plain `*(working English)*` line then an attribution line (What Is Religion).
The gospel-translation dive nests both on consecutive `>` lines plus a `— source` line. Translations are always flagged as the dive's own ("the editor's" / "working renderings, not published ones"). Bilingual letters (Eltzbacher German, Sacy French) print **both** Tolstoy's original-language text and the Russian, both byte-checked.

**TEI ids / PSS Toms / pages — citation form.** Cited as `PSS Tom NN, pp. X–Y` (Russian: `ПСС Т. NN, с. X`), with the **TEI id in backticks**, e.g. `` `v35_157_198_Chto_takoe_religiya` ``, `` `v52_138_140_1894_09_10` ``, `` `v88_636_avgusta12` ``. Diaries cite date + Old Style note. Editorial commentary (Gudzy, Mishin) is **attributed, not byte-claimed** — distinguished sharply from public-domain Tolstoy text, which carries byte-fidelity. Extract files referenced as `extracts/<id>.txt`. Tom→local-PDF resolution via `../pss-volume-mapping.md`. Dates follow PSS Old Style (Julian) for pre-1918 items; foreign publication dates New Style; both styles kept where the source gives both (e.g. "1/13 August 1900").

**"Place in the cluster" line.** Renders sibling dives as a **bulleted or inline list of relative `.html` links with a one-line gloss each**, e.g.:
`- [A Confession](../1879-1882-a-confession/index.html) (1879–82) — the biographical and existential precursor…`
or inline chains: "the culminating panel of … [A Confession](../1879-1882-a-confession/index.html) → [What I Believe](../1882-1884-what-i-believe/index.html) → …". Each gloss states *how* the sibling relates (precursor / extension / companion / the doctrine the work restates). Theme/label dives are cross-linked out rather than re-argued ("cross-linked here rather than repeated").

**Method "Links" line.** Near the end of `## Method` (or as its last bullet), a `**Links.**` / `Links:` line listing **Sibling dives:** as relative `.html` links, then **Tom → local-PDF lookups: [pss-volume-mapping.md](../pss-volume-mapping.md)**. Example (Kingdom of God): "Sibling dives: [A Confession](…), [What I Believe](…), …; the doctrine companions [Christian anarchism](…) and [Tolstoyism](…). Tom → local-PDF lookups: [pss-volume-mapping.md](../pss-volume-mapping.md)."

**Companion-document conventions.** Each dive ships: `dossier.yaml` (machine-readable evidence/entity/visuals/scholarship/work-record ledger), byte-faithful `extracts/`, a `visuals/` cache (git-ignored; repopulate via `python3 docs/fetch_visuals.py <slug>`), and a draft dev-blog note at `website/src/posts/notes/YYYY-MM-DD-<slug>.md` kept `draft: true`. Quotes gated by `verify_quotes.py`; extraction via `extract_tei.py --choice=reg` (modern orthography for pre-1918 texts) and `--notes=auto` where needed.

**Scholarly-context convention.** Mainstream scholarship is read **as a contrast/foil, never a baseline to confirm**; secondary claims are *attributed, not asserted*; only primary quotes carry byte-fidelity. The corpus's relation to received views is stated with the verbs **confirms / complicates / extends** (e.g. "the corpus *confirms* … *complicates* … *extends*").

**Tone.** Simple, factual, minimal editorial; working-English translations always labelled; judgment calls pushed to `needsReview` in the dossier; the index notes `draft: true` until published.

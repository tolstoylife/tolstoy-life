# Scholarship — the received view on *Resurrection* (Воскресение, 1899), triangulated

**Status: SECONDARY / web-sourced.** This file adds the conventional scholarly view as a
*contrast layer* to the dive's primary findings (`_genesis_commentary.md`,
`_excommunication.md`, `_communion_centrepiece.md`). Every secondary claim is attributed.

## 1. Summary of the received view

**Reputation — the "third novel" that fell out of the canon.** The settled handbook line
is that *Resurrection* is Tolstoy's third (and last) full-length novel, "usually regarded
as inferior to *War and Peace* and *Anna Karenina*" but "still… a very important literary
achievement" (English Wikipedia, *Resurrection (Tolstoy novel)*). Britannica likewise
files it simply as his "third long novel." The paradox every account notes: on
publication in 1899 it was a sensation and **outsold both earlier novels** (Wikipedia;
Bartlett 2010; Gioia 2023), yet its later reputation collapsed to "rarely read and mostly
forgotten" (Gioia, *The Honest Broker*, 2023). A minority dissents upward — the
science-fiction novelist Brian Aldiss is quoted calling it "the greatest of Tolstoy's
novels" (via Wikipedia), and A. N. Wilson speaks of "the wonderfully rough art of
*Resurrection*" (Wilson 1988, via reviews) — but the dominant valuation is "lesser, and
for a reason."

**The art-vs-sermon debate.** That "reason" is the novel's didacticism, and it is the
centre of gravity of the criticism. Ernest J. Simmons (*Introduction to Tolstoy's
Writings*, 1968) gives the classic verdict: the book "falls below the lofty artistic
achievements of these earlier novels"; it is "an unashamedly purpose novel" in which the
moral message "obtrudes in a rather scholastic manner throughout," and this didacticism is
its "major artistic fault" — Simmons noting Tolstoy had by now abandoned his own earlier
principle that "the aims of art are incommensurable… with social aims." The same
framing recurs across the popular and academic registers: contemporary reviewers
complained of "perpetual sermonizing" (cited in onehundredpages / *A Useful Fiction*,
2021), and recent essayists use the novel as the test-case for "making art political is
one way to make it bad" (Boston Globe, 2023). The countervailing scholarly move — best
seen in Hugh McLean's chapter "Resurrection" in *The Cambridge Companion to Tolstoy* (ed.
Donna Tussing Orwin, 2002) — is to recover "the genuine artistic and philosophical import"
of the book and resist reading it only as a tract; the early Nekhlyudov–Katyusha love
story is the section even the sceptics (Simmons) concede reaches Tolstoy's first rank.

**The Doukhobor genesis.** On the composition history the received view and the corpus
PSS commentary substantially agree: the plot came from the jurist A. F. Koni's Rozalia
Oni case (told 1887); Tolstoy resumed and rushed the long-stalled book in 1898 expressly
to fund the **Doukhobor emigration to Canada**, breaking his own post-1881 refusal of
literary fees to do so (Wikipedia; Simmons 1968; Britannica; Bartlett 2010). The money
figures vary by source and are discussed under triangulation §2.

**The excommunication context.** This is where the conventional account is at its
loosest. The popular formulation makes the **mass-satire chapter the trigger**: Bartlett
(2010) writes that the Church, "finally exasperated by Tolstoy's blistering satire of a
mass in one of its chapters," excommunicated him. The handbooks pair the novel with the
**Toporov = Pobedonostsev caricature** (Wikipedia: Toporov is an "obvious caricature of
Konstantin Pobedonostsev"). A second, more careful strand sets the novel inside the long
arc of Tolstoy's heterodoxy — the gw2ru summary attributes the decree to "his broader
religious writings," naming *Resurrection* alongside *A Confession* and *What I Believe*
and citing John of Kronstadt as the loudest clerical antagonist, not the mass scene. And a
revisionist historiographical strand (socialbites.ca, summarising recent Russian
scholarship) holds that **Metropolitan Antony (Vadkovsky) drove the decision while
Pobedonostsev hesitated or opposed publication**, fearing it would amplify Tolstoy — the
exact inverse of the Soviet-PSS "composed by Pobedonostsev" line. The received view, in
short, is internally split, and the split is the most useful thing in it.

## 2. Triangulation — dive findings against the conventional view

Each entry: **finding** (from the dive) · **conventionalView** (attributed) · **relation**
· **source**.

### F1 — Doukhobor funding was the engine of the 1898 completion
- **Dive finding** (`_genesis_commentary.md` §3): the PSS editors name the Doukhobor
  fund as the explicit reason Tolstoy reopened the stalled novel in July 1898, reversed
  his refusal of fees, and — crucially — *radically expanded* it (the church service, the
  politicals, Toporov all entered only in the 1898 redactions because of this push).
- **conventionalView**: the same causal story is standard — Tolstoy "decided in August
  1898 to finish and sell the novel" to fund the Doukhobors' move to Canada (Wikipedia);
  Simmons (1968) and Britannica say the same.
- **relation: confirms** (with an **extends** edge: the dive's PSS-sourced point that the
  *deadline forced the massive expansion into the politically sharp final text* is sharper
  than the handbooks, which note the funding motive but not its shaping effect on the
  novel's scope).
- **source**: English Wikipedia, *Resurrection (Tolstoy novel)*,
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resurrection_(Tolstoy_novel) ; Simmons 1968,
  https://www.ourcivilisation.com/smartboard/shop/smmnsej/tolstoy/chap12.htm ; Britannica,
  https://www.britannica.com/topic/Resurrection-by-Tolstoy .

### F2 — How much money / the Marks contract
- **Dive finding** (`_genesis_commentary.md` §4): the corpus records the **12 Oct 1898
  Marks/*Niva* contract at 1,000 r. per printer's sheet, 12,000 r. down** (with S. A.
  Tolstaya's diary noting an earlier 1,600-then-500 r./sheet haggle); the dive does not
  assert a single "total raised" figure.
- **conventionalView**: secondary figures diverge. Wikipedia states Tolstoy "contributed
  34,200 rubles (approximately US$17,000)" to the emigration; gw2ru's summary gives "about
  30,000 rubles, half of the emigration fund" and "22,000 rubles from… A. F. Marx."
- **relation: complicates** — the secondary "total" figures (34,200 vs ~30,000 vs the
  22,000 advance) are **not mutually consistent** and none is corpus-anchored in this
  dive. Treat the round totals as approximate; the corpus's hard datum is the *per-sheet
  rate and 12,000 r. advance*, not a lump sum. **Flag: do not state a single "raised"
  figure as fact** without reconciling these.
- **source**: Wikipedia (as above); gw2ru, *How Leo Tolstoy resettled the Doukhobors in
  Canada*, https://www.gw2ru.com/arts/238260-how-leo-tolstoy-resettled-the-doukhobors-in-canada .

### F3 — The communion/church-service chapter (Part I, XXXIX–XL)
- **Dive finding** (`_communion_centrepiece.md`): a sustained *defamiliarised* description
  of the Orthodox liturgy — chasuble as "brocade sack," consecration as "manipulations,"
  the priest who "ate a piece of the body of God"; ch. XL names the rite "the greatest
  blasphemy… in whose name it was all being done."
- **conventionalView**: critics register this as real and as a flaw of taste. Simmons
  (1968) calls it a "lapse of taste… the blasphemously satiric account of the Russian
  Orthodox Church service." Bartlett (2010) calls it Tolstoy's "blistering satire of a
  mass." The device itself is read by scholars as Tolstoy's signature *остранение*
  (defamiliarisation) — the dive's own "Brechtian/flat" reading aligns with the academic
  literature on the novel's "grotesque/estranging aesthetics" (e.g. academia.edu,
  *Estranged and Degraded Worlds*, and the Macksey Journal piece on sensory estrangement).
- **relation: confirms** (on the existence/centrality of the satire and the
  defamiliarisation reading) — though note the *valuation* differs: where Simmons hears a
  "lapse of taste," the dive and the estrangement-aesthetics scholarship read it as a
  deliberate, load-bearing technique. So: **confirms** the fact, **complicates** the
  value-judgement.
- **source**: Simmons 1968 (as above); Bartlett 2010 (via Goodreads description excerpt),
  https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/8205861-tolstoy ; *Estranged and Degraded Worlds:
  The Grotesque Aesthetics of Tolstoy's Resurrection*,
  https://www.academia.edu/5404687/Estranged_and_Degraded_Worlds .

### F4 — Did the communion chapter *cause* the 1901 excommunication?
- **Dive finding** (`_excommunication.md`, verdict **COMPLICATES**): the decree's
  indictment is *doctrinally broad* (Trinity, Christ's divinity/atonement/resurrection,
  virgin birth, afterlife, *all* sacraments); the Eucharist is named but framed by the
  Synod itself as the «последняя и высшая степень» — the climax of that list, not its
  basis. The PSS editor calls *Resurrection* the «толчок» (impulse) to *publish* a decree
  whose substance dated to the 1880s, and locates its personal sting in the **Toporov
  caricature of Pobedonostsev** as much as in the Mass scene. Tolstoy's own diary reads
  the affair as doctrinal («странное отлучение»; "all our Orthodoxy is sorcery").
- **conventionalView**: the *popular* line is precisely the simplification the dive tests
  — Bartlett (2010) attributes the act to the Church being "finally exasperated by
  Tolstoy's blistering satire of a mass in one of its chapters." Wikipedia says the
  novel's "publication led to" the excommunication. A *more careful* secondary strand
  matches the dive: gw2ru attributes the decree to Tolstoy's "broader religious writings"
  (naming *A Confession* and *What I Believe* beside *Resurrection*, and John of Kronstadt
  as antagonist), not to the mass scene.
- **relation: extends / contradicts** — the dive's primary-record reading **contradicts**
  the strong popular "mass-satire-caused-it" formulation (Bartlett's phrasing, the loose
  Wikipedia "led to") by showing from the decree's own text and Tolstoy's *Reply* that the
  charge is doctrinally total and the Eucharist is the rhetorical capstone, not the
  foundation; and it **extends** the careful strand (gw2ru's "broader writings") with the
  corpus's sharper distinction between *long-laid intent (1880s)* and *proximate occasion
  (1899 publication)*, plus the Toporov-caricature-as-political-sting point that the
  English-language handbooks underuse.
- **source**: Bartlett 2010 (Goodreads excerpt, as above); Wikipedia (as above); gw2ru,
  *Why was Tolstoy kicked out of the Russian Orthodox Church?*,
  https://www.gw2ru.com/arts/1348-tolstoy-church-scandal .

### F5 — Pobedonostsev: author/prime-mover, or reluctant opponent of publication?
- **Dive finding** (`_excommunication.md` §4): the corpus (Soviet PSS commentary) makes
  the decree «составленное Победоносцевым» (composed by Pobedonostsev), edited by
  Metropolitan Antony and approved by Nicholas II — Pobedonostsev as author and prime
  mover. The dive *flags as contested* a later strand in which Pobedonostsev hesitated or
  opposed publication.
- **conventionalView**: genuinely split in the secondary literature. (a) The "prime
  mover / long advocate" reading: Anthony Vadkovsky was "a principal initiator… although
  Konstantin Pobedonostsev had been advocating for this action for some time" (per the
  *Anthony Vadkovsky* Wikipedia summary). (b) The revisionist reading: the decision "was
  steered by Metropolitan Anthony… while Konstantin Pobedonostsev… opposed the move,
  concerned it would amplify Tolstoy's appeal rather than diminish it" (socialbites.ca).
- **relation: complicates / contradicts** — the two secondary strands **contradict each
  other**, and the revisionist strand **complicates** the corpus's PSS attribution of
  authorship to Pobedonostsev. The dive's stance (report, do not adjudicate; treat
  "Pobedonostsev reluctantly opposed" with caution as partly a rehabilitative frame) is
  the correct one and is *reinforced* by finding the same unresolved split in the
  independent web sources. **Flag: leave contested; do not let either the "Pobedonostsev
  the antagonist-author" or the "reluctant Pobedonostsev" label harden.**
- **source**: *Anthony Vadkovsky*, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Vadkovsky ;
  socialbites.ca, *Tolstoy Excommunication and Its Aftermath*,
  https://socialbites.ca/culture/tolstoy-excommunication-and-its-aftermath .

### F6 — Censorship: the church chapters cut, the complete text long suppressed
- **Dive finding** (`_genesis_commentary.md` §4, §6): the corpus PSS detail is granular —
  Part I chs XXXIX–XL cut wholesale from *Niva* (only «Началось богослужение» left);
  Bonch-Bruevich's count of **497 distortions / 10,240 words**; only 25 of 123 chapters
  printed clean; the uncut text via Chertkov's «Свободное слово» abroad (itself carrying
  51 stray distortions).
- **conventionalView**: the handbooks register the fact but not the granularity —
  Wikipedia notes censorship "requiring parts to be censored and shortened" and that "the
  complete Russian text wasn't published until 1936, [the complete] English in 1938."
- **relation: extends** — the dive's corpus record is far more precise than the secondary
  literature (specific chapters, the Bonch-Bruevich numbers, the parallel-printing leak of
  censored proofs into the "uncut" Chertkov edition). The secondary view **confirms** the
  bare fact of heavy censorship and adds the useful *dates of first complete publication*
  (1936 RU / 1938 EN) that the dive's PSS commentary does not foreground.
- **source**: English Wikipedia, *Resurrection (Tolstoy novel)* (as above).

### F7 — Critical standing: the propaganda-vs-art valuation
- **Dive finding**: the dive does not itself rank the novel; its evidence (the rushed
  18 Dec 1899 completion under deadline; Tolstoy's own diary "Не хорошо, не поправлено,
  поспешно" — "not good, unpolished, hasty") gives a primary-record basis for the "rough"
  quality the critics name.
- **conventionalView**: dominant verdict "lesser than the two great novels, because
  didactic" (Simmons 1968: "major artistic fault"; Wikipedia: "usually regarded as
  inferior"); minority upward dissent (Aldiss "the greatest"; Wilson "wonderfully rough
  art"); recovery readings (McLean, *Cambridge Companion*, 2002).
- **relation: confirms / extends** — the received "rough/lesser but important" valuation
  is **confirmed** by Tolstoy's own contemporaneous self-assessment in the corpus diary
  (a primary datum the criticism rarely cites); the dive thus **extends** the debate by
  grounding the "unpolished" charge in Tolstoy's own words rather than only in critical
  taste.
- **source**: Simmons 1968 (as above); Wikipedia (as above); McLean in Orwin (ed.) 2002,
  https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-companion-to-tolstoy/resurrection/277688754BA6B3A5D8EF903328052A0B .
